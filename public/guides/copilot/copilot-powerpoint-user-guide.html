What is Copilot in PowerPoint?

AI presentation building — from blank slide to polished deck

Copilot in PowerPoint turns your ideas, documents, and outlines into complete slide decks — without starting from a blank slide. You describe what you need, and Copilot generates slides with titles, content, and layouts. You can also use it on existing presentations to rewrite, expand, summarise, and create speaker notes.

Whether you're building a client pitch, an internal update, a training deck, or a board report, Copilot dramatically reduces the time from idea to presentation-ready. It handles the drafting so you can focus on refining the story.

✨

➕

Add & Expand Slides

Ask Copilot to add a new slide on a specific topic, or expand a brief bullet into a full slide with supporting detail.

✏️

Generate speaker notes for every slide, rehearse your delivery, and get coaching on pacing, clarity, and filler words.

⚠️
Brand Kit Required for Best Results

Copilot uses your organisation's PowerPoint template and brand kit when generating slides. If no template is active, slides use a generic theme. For branded output, always start from your company's PowerPoint template file — open it first, then ask Copilot to create the presentation inside it.

🖼️
2026 Update — Image Model Selector & Web Sources

Copilot in PowerPoint can now use public webpages as source material when building a deck — paste a URL and ask Copilot to draft slides from it. You also get an image-model selector so you can choose between Microsoft AI Image 2 (Efficient) and other models for image generation and editing. The result is faster, more on-brief drafts and image styles you actually control.

02

Create a Presentation from a Prompt

Go from a sentence to a full slide deck in seconds

The fastest way to start a presentation with Copilot is to describe what you need in plain English. Copilot will generate a complete deck — typically 8–12 slides — with titles, body content, and a logical narrative structure. You then refine from there rather than building from scratch.

1

Open PowerPoint with your company template

Start from your organisation's PowerPoint template (.potx) if one exists — this ensures slides use your brand colours, fonts, and layouts. Open the template file, then use Copilot to create the presentation inside it.

2

Open the Copilot panel and describe the presentation

Click the Copilot button (✨) in the Home ribbon. In the Copilot panel, type a detailed description: topic, audience, purpose, and approximate length.

3

Review the generated structure

Copilot produces a draft deck. Review the slide order and overall narrative — this is the time to spot structural issues before refining individual slides.

4

Refine iteratively

Use follow-up prompts to adjust: add slides, remove slides, rewrite specific sections, change the tone, or expand a particular section with more detail.

What Makes a Good Creation Prompt

Slide 1 of 10
Q3 Business Review: Sales Performance
Revenue summary: £2.4M vs £2.1M target
Top performers and regional breakdown
Key wins, risks, and Q4 outlook
Prompt that created this

"Create a 10-slide Q3 business review presentation for our senior leadership team. Include an executive summary, revenue vs target, top-performing regions and reps, key customer wins, pipeline risks, and a Q4 outlook. Tone: confident and data-driven. Use our corporate template."

Slide 1 of 8
AI in Customer Service: What's Changing
What AI can and can't do for your team
3 real-world implementation scenarios
Getting started: a practical roadmap
Prompt that created this

"Create an 8-slide presentation introducing our customer service team to AI tools. Audience: non-technical staff who may be nervous about AI. Cover what AI does well, common myths, three realistic use cases, and a reassuring roadmap. Keep the tone friendly and practical."

💡
Specify Audience, Purpose, and Tone

The more context you give, the better the first draft. "A 10-slide board presentation for non-technical executives, focused on ROI, in a formal but clear tone" produces a dramatically different — and more useful — result than "a presentation about our AI project."

Creating from a Word Document

One of the most powerful Copilot in PowerPoint features is generating a presentation directly from a Word document. This is ideal for turning strategy docs, project briefs, or research reports into presentation-ready decks.

1

Have the Word document accessible

The source document should be saved to OneDrive or SharePoint. Copilot in PowerPoint can reference files from your Microsoft 365 environment via the file picker.

2

Use the "Create from file" option in Copilot

In the Copilot panel, select "Create a presentation from a file" and navigate to your Word document. Copilot reads the structure and generates slides that follow the document's narrative.

3

Review and refine the slide count and structure

Copilot may generate more slides than you need. Identify which slides to keep, merge, or remove — then use follow-up prompts to adjust content depth and slide order.

ℹ️
The Word Doc Becomes the Outline

Copilot maps headings (H1, H2) to slide titles and body text to bullet points. Well-structured Word documents with clear headings produce much better slides. If your doc doesn't use heading styles, add them before creating the presentation.

04

Rewrite & Refine Slides

Edit content, tone, and length — slide by slide or across the whole deck

Copilot can rewrite slide content on demand — adjusting tone, shortening verbose text, simplifying jargon, or strengthening the message. You can target a single slide, a section, or apply a change across the entire presentation.

Before rewrite
Implementation Considerations for the Proposed Platform Migration Initiative
There are a number of significant factors that will need to be carefully considered during the implementation phase of this project
Resource allocation will need to be prioritised accordingly based on available bandwidth
Copilot rewrite prompt

"Rewrite this slide to be clearer and more concise. Shorten the title to under 8 words. Replace the bullet points with 3 punchy, action-oriented statements. Keep the tone professional but direct."

After rewrite
Platform Migration: Key Decisions
Allocate dedicated resource from project kickoff
Confirm data migration approach by end of month
Align stakeholders before any system access changes
What Copilot changed

Title shortened from 12 words to 5. Vague passive statements replaced with direct, action-oriented bullets. Reading time reduced from ~8 seconds to ~3 seconds per bullet — better for presentation delivery.

Useful Rewrite Prompts

What You WantExample Prompt
Simplify for a non-technical audience"Rewrite slide 5 to remove technical jargon. The audience are senior managers, not engineers — focus on business impact, not technical detail."
Make it more concise"Condense slide 7 to no more than 4 bullet points. Remove any bullets that don't directly support the slide title."
Strengthen the message"Rewrite the conclusion slide to end with a stronger, more compelling call to action. The audience needs to leave knowing exactly what we're asking them to decide."
Change the tone globally"Rewrite all slide body text to be more conversational and less formal. This is an internal team update, not a board report."
Add a missing angle"Add a bullet to slide 3 addressing the timeline risk — we haven't mentioned it yet and it's a common question."
✨
Target One Slide at a Time

For best results, click on the slide you want to change in the slide panel before prompting. Copilot uses the active slide as context. "Rewrite this slide…" is more precise than "Rewrite slide 4…" — especially after you've added or reordered slides.

05

Summarise & Understand Decks

Get the key points from a long presentation — fast

When you receive a long presentation from a colleague or client, Copilot can summarise it, extract the key decisions, identify action items, and answer specific questions about the content — without you needing to read every slide in detail.

📋

Summarise the Deck

"Give me a 5-bullet summary of the key points in this presentation." Useful before a meeting when you've received slides you haven't had time to read.

❓

Ask a Specific Question

"Does this presentation mention anything about implementation timeline or budget?" Copilot searches the content and tells you what it found — and where.

✅

Extract Decisions & Actions

"List all the decisions being requested and any action items mentioned in this deck." Useful for meeting prep or following up after receiving a stakeholder presentation.

🎯

Audience Perspective Check

"What questions is a sceptical CFO likely to ask after seeing this presentation?" Copilot anticipates objections based on what the deck does and doesn't address.

💡
Use Copilot to Prep for Someone Else's Meeting

If someone sends you their slide deck before a meeting, ask Copilot: "What are the key decisions this presentation is asking for, and what information might be missing to make those decisions?" This turns a passive read into active preparation — in under two minutes.

06

Speaker Notes & Coaching

Prepare for delivery — Copilot writes notes and coaches your technique

Copilot can generate speaker notes for every slide — transition phrases, talking points, timing cues, and audience engagement prompts. It can also coach you on presentation delivery through Rehearse with Coach, an AI-powered practice mode built into PowerPoint.

Generating Speaker Notes with Copilot

1

Ask Copilot to generate notes for the whole deck

"Generate speaker notes for every slide. For each slide, include: a 1-sentence transition from the previous slide, 3–4 key talking points, and a bridging sentence to the next slide."

2

Refine notes for complex slides

For slides covering difficult or sensitive topics, give more direction: "For slide 6 — the budget constraints slide — write notes that acknowledge the challenge directly but pivot quickly to the mitigation plan. Tone: candid and reassuring."

3

Add timing guidance

"I have 20 minutes for this 10-slide deck. Add a time target to the speaker notes of each slide based on its complexity." Copilot distributes time across slides proportionally.

Rehearse with Coach (AI Delivery Coaching)

PowerPoint's Rehearse with Coach uses AI to listen as you present and give real-time feedback. Access it via Slide Show → Rehearse with Coach. It gives feedback on:

Coaching AreaWhat It MeasuresWhat to Aim For
PacingWords per minute — too fast or too slow120–150 wpm for most presentation contexts
Filler wordsUm, uh, like, you know — and frequencyFewer than 5 per minute
Pitch variationMonotone vs varied intonationNatural variation to maintain audience attention
Reading slidesWhether you're reading vs speakingSpeak from notes, not verbatim from slides
Inclusive languageFlags potentially non-inclusive phrasingUse neutral, accessible language throughout
✨
Use Notes + Coach Together

Generate speaker notes with Copilot, then rehearse with Coach while using those notes. Coach picks up if you're reading verbatim — which forces you to internalise the notes rather than read them. This combination accelerates delivery preparation significantly.

Copy Copilot's data summary from Excel, paste it into Copilot in PowerPoint: "Turn these data insights into 3 slides for our monthly review. Add charts where relevant."

💬→📊

After a strategy meeting, paste the Copilot Teams summary into PowerPoint's Copilot: "Create a 6-slide summary of these meeting outcomes for stakeholders who weren't present."

📊→📧

After finalising your presentation, use Copilot in Outlook: "Draft a pre-read email summarising the attached deck in 5 bullets for an executive audience."

Iterative Deck Building

Professional decks rarely emerge from a single prompt. Use Copilot in passes — each pass serving a specific purpose:

1

Structure pass — get the bones right

Create the initial deck with a broad prompt. Review the slide order and section logic. Move, add, or remove slides until the narrative arc is correct — before touching any copy.

2

Content pass — refine each slide's message

Go through each slide and prompt Copilot to sharpen the content: clearer titles, stronger bullets, audience-appropriate language. One slide at a time.

3

Notes pass — prepare for delivery

Generate speaker notes for the whole deck, then refine notes for your most complex or sensitive slides. Add timing targets.

4

Review pass — stress-test the deck

"What questions is a sceptical [audience role] likely to ask?" and "Is there anything important missing from this presentation?" Use Copilot's critique to close gaps before you present.

Common Mistakes & Fixes

What goes wrong — and how to fix it

Copilot generated too many slides — the deck is too long
This is the most common output from a broad creation prompt. After generating, ask Copilot: "Which 3 slides could be removed without losing any critical information?" or "Merge slides 7 and 8 into a single, concise slide." You can also be explicit upfront: "Create a maximum of 8 slides — cut anything that isn't essential."
The slide content doesn't match my company's context
Copilot generates from your prompt but doesn't know your company specifics — products, customers, or internal data. Treat the first draft as a structure, not finished content. Provide your specific figures, examples, and context in follow-up prompts: "Replace the generic market stats on slide 4 with these figures: [your data]."
Slides look unstyled or use the wrong brand colours
Copilot applies whatever theme or template is active in the current PowerPoint file. If you started from a blank file, slides use the default theme. Always open your company's PowerPoint template (.potx) first, then ask Copilot to create the presentation — it will use the correct brand styles, colours, and fonts.
The "Create from file" option can't find my Word document
The source document must be saved to OneDrive or SharePoint — not to a local drive. Copilot in PowerPoint cannot access local files. Save or move your Word document to OneDrive first, then use the file picker in Copilot to locate it.
Speaker notes Copilot generated are too long to use while presenting
Ask Copilot to condense them: "Rewrite the speaker notes for every slide so each note is no longer than 4 bullet points. These need to be scannable at a glance while speaking — they're not a script." You can also ask for notes in a specific format: "Write each slide's notes as three keywords I can use as memory triggers, not full sentences."

✓ Do This

  • Start from your company's PowerPoint template
  • Include audience, tone, and purpose in your creation prompt
  • Build the deck in passes: structure → content → notes → review
  • Click the target slide before prompting for a rewrite
  • Ask Copilot to critique the deck before you present

✗ Avoid This

  • Starting from a blank file and expecting branded output
  • Using vague prompts like "make a presentation about X"
  • Accepting a 15-slide deck when 8 slides would be better
  • Publishing Copilot's generic content without adding your specifics
  • Generating speaker notes and then reading them verbatim

Suggested Prompts

Ready-to-use prompts for building and refining presentations

Create from a Brief
Create a [number]-slide presentation for [audience — e.g. the senior leadership team / a new client / an all-staff update]. The topic is [topic]. Cover: [section 1], [section 2], [section 3]. Tone should be [tone — e.g. confident and data-driven / clear and accessible / formal]. Use our company template.
Create from a Word Document
Create a presentation based on the Word document I'll attach. Map the main headings to slide titles and summarise the body content into concise bullet points — no more than 4 bullets per slide. The audience is [audience]. Keep slides visual and punchy rather than text-heavy. Aim for [number] slides maximum.
Create a Follow-Up Deck from Meeting Notes
Create a 6-slide summary presentation from these meeting notes: [paste notes or Teams recap]. The deck should cover: key decisions made, agreed actions and owners, outstanding questions, and next steps. Audience: stakeholders who were not in the meeting. Tone: clear, factual, and action-oriented.
Rewrite for a Specific Audience
Rewrite the content of this entire presentation for [audience — e.g. a non-technical executive audience / a technical delivery team / a sales prospect]. Remove [jargon / operational detail / generic content] and replace with [business impact / implementation specifics / benefit-focused language]. Keep slide titles as-is but rewrite all body content.
Generate Speaker Notes
Generate speaker notes for every slide in this presentation. For each slide include: (1) a one-sentence transition from the previous slide, (2) three to four talking points expanding on the slide content, and (3) a bridging sentence to the next slide. I have [X] minutes total — distribute time proportionally. Keep notes conversational, not scripted.
Stress-Test the Deck
Review this presentation from the perspective of a sceptical [audience role — e.g. CFO / procurement manager / non-technical executive]. What questions are they most likely to ask that this deck doesn't currently answer? What are the weakest slides — where might they push back? What's missing? Give me a prioritised list of gaps to address before I present.
