Why AI Matters for Educators

The case for integrating Copilot into your teaching practice

Teaching has always been one of the most demanding professions — you're simultaneously a content creator, assessor, communicator, administrator, and mentor. AI doesn't replace any of those roles. What it does is dramatically reduce the time you spend on the routine parts of each one, freeing you to focus on what only a human teacher can do: build relationships, read the room, and respond to individual students.

Microsoft Copilot is embedded directly into the tools you already use every day — Word, Excel, PowerPoint, OneNote, Teams, and Outlook. That means there's no new app to learn, no separate login, and no workflow disruption. Copilot meets you where you are.

✦
2026 Update — Agent Mode GA in Word, Excel, and PowerPoint

Copilot can now take multi-step actions inside your documents. Rather than just suggesting text, Agent Mode lets Copilot plan and execute a sequence of tasks — create an entire differentiated lesson pack (main lesson plan, extension task, support version, and parent summary) from a single prompt. This is the most significant capability upgrade for educators since Copilot launched.

Setting Up Copilot for Education Work

Five steps to get Copilot working across your M365 suite

Getting started with Copilot requires a Microsoft 365 Copilot licence and a few quick configuration steps. Work through these once and you'll have Copilot active everywhere you need it.

1

Confirm Your Licensing

Microsoft Copilot requires a Microsoft 365 Copilot licence in addition to your standard M365 subscription. This is typically provisioned by your IT admin. Check by opening any M365 app and looking for the Copilot icon (the swirling colour icon) in the toolbar. If it's not there, contact your IT team to request a licence.

2

Enable Copilot in Each App

Once your licence is active, Copilot appears automatically in Word (Draft with Copilot pane), PowerPoint (Designer + Copilot toolbar), OneNote (Copilot in the ribbon), and Teams (Meeting intelligence + Chat). You may need to sign in once per app using your school Microsoft account.

3

Your First Prompt in Word

Open a new Word document. Click the Copilot icon or press Alt + I to open the Draft pane. Type: "Draft a 50-minute lesson plan on photosynthesis for Year 9. Include learning objectives, starter activity, main activity, and an exit ticket." — and see what Copilot produces. This is your baseline to build from.

4

Configure Copilot Chat (BizChat) for Shared Drives

Open Copilot Chat at copilot.microsoft.com (or via the Teams Copilot app). Use the Work tab (not Web) so Copilot can access your school's SharePoint and OneDrive. In the prompt bar, type / to reference specific files — Copilot will search your shared drives and attached documents. Ask your IT team to ensure SharePoint indexing is enabled for your department's drives.

5

Set Up OneNote with Copilot for Student Notes

Open your OneNote notebook for student observations or class notes. In the Copilot panel, try: "Summarise all notes in this section and identify any students flagged for additional support." Make sure your notebooks are stored in OneDrive (not local) so Copilot can access them across devices.

⚠️
School IT Policy

Before using Copilot with student-related data, confirm your school's data governance policy. Many schools require that student names and identifying information are anonymised before being used in Copilot prompts. When in doubt, use descriptions like "a Year 8 student who..." rather than a student's name.

Your Daily AI Workflows

Six high-impact use cases across Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Teams, OneNote, and Outlook

These six workflows cover the core teaching tasks where Copilot delivers the most immediate value. Each one maps to an M365 app you already use — with a ready-to-use example prompt to get you started.

📝
Word
Lesson Planning in Word

Use Copilot in Word to draft complete lesson plans, then use the / shortcut to reference existing curriculum documents, schemes of work, or past lesson plans stored in your OneDrive. Copilot will cross-reference your files and produce a draft that's already aligned — saving the alignment step entirely.

Best practice: Keep your curriculum documents in a shared OneDrive folder so Copilot can reference them via / from any device. Use Agent Mode to produce a full lesson pack (lesson plan + student worksheet + teacher notes) from a single prompt.

Example Prompt
"Draft a 50-minute lesson plan for [TOPIC] for [YEAR/GRADE]. Reference /[CURRICULUM-DOC] for alignment. Include: learning objectives (linked to curriculum standards), a 10-minute starter activity, main activity with differentiation, and an exit ticket for assessment."
📊
Excel
Assessment Analysis in Excel

Paste or enter student assessment data into Excel, then use Copilot to analyse it for patterns, flag outliers, and generate actionable insights. Copilot can build pivot tables, charts, and written summaries on your behalf — turning raw mark data into a differentiation action plan.

Best practice: Use column headers that are clear and consistent (e.g. "Student ID", "Assessment 1", "Assessment 2"). Copilot responds better to structured data. Anonymise student names — use student IDs or codes instead.

Example Prompt
"Analyse this student performance data. Identify: top performers, students needing support (below 60%), trends across assessments over time, and recommendations for differentiation in the next unit."
🎨
PowerPoint
Student-Facing Presentations in PowerPoint

Use Copilot in PowerPoint to generate complete, visually engaging slide decks from a brief or an existing Word lesson plan. Copilot can pull content from a Word doc you reference with / and produce a presentation structured for student audiences — with appropriate language and layout for the year level.

Best practice: After Copilot generates the deck, use the Designer tool to refine slide layouts. Review each slide for age-appropriate language — Copilot defaults to adult-level vocabulary unless you specify the year group.

Example Prompt
"Create a presentation for [TOPIC] suitable for [YEAR/GRADE]. Include: title slide, 3–5 content slides with visual suggestions, a class discussion slide with 2 questions, and a recap slide with key takeaways. Keep language appropriate for [age group] students."
💬
Teams
Meeting Notes & Action Items in Teams

Copilot in Teams transcribes meetings in real time and generates structured summaries — including key decisions, action items, and follow-up owners. This is transformative for parent-teacher conferences and staff meetings, where accurate notes are critical but note-taking interrupts conversation.

Best practice: Enable the Teams meeting recording and Copilot intelligence before the meeting starts. After the meeting ends, ask Copilot to generate a summary before closing the Teams window — it won't be available later without the transcript.

Example Prompt (after meeting ends)
"Summarise this meeting. List: key decisions made, action items with the name of who is responsible for each, and follow-up dates. Also flag any unresolved issues that need a follow-up meeting."
📓
OneNote
Student Observation Notes in OneNote

Use OneNote as your ongoing log of student observations, interventions, and progress notes. Copilot can synthesise entries across a notebook section and produce structured summaries — identifying patterns, flagging concerns, and recommending next steps. This is especially powerful for pastoral care, SEND records, and report writing season.

Best practice: Create a OneNote section per student (or use page tags for different students in a shared section). The more structured your notes, the better Copilot's synthesis. Use consistent language when logging observations.

Example Prompt
"Summarise all notes in this section about [STUDENT/TOPIC]. Identify: key patterns in progress or behaviour, any concerns that have been raised, interventions that have been tried, and recommended next steps for the upcoming term."
📧
Outlook
Parent Communication in Outlook

Use Copilot in Outlook to draft parent emails — from routine updates to more sensitive communications about student progress or behaviour. Copilot can also coach you on tone, suggesting whether a draft reads as warm, professional, or inadvertently cold. This is particularly valuable before sending difficult messages.

Best practice: After Copilot drafts the email, ask it to review the tone and flag anything that might land poorly. For sensitive situations, ask Copilot to suggest a softer phrasing for any direct statements before you finalise.

Example Prompt
"Draft an email to parents about [TOPIC]. Context: [SITUATION]. Make the tone warm and professional, with a clear explanation of the situation and specific next steps parents can take. After drafting, review: is the tone appropriate? Are the next steps clear? Flag anything that might be misread."

Advanced Techniques

Agent Mode, Copilot Studio, BizChat, and Power Automate for power users

Once you're comfortable with the core workflows, these advanced capabilities unlock a new tier of productivity — moving from single-task assistance to autonomous multi-step document creation and institutional-scale automation.

⚡ Agent Mode in Word 2026 GA

Agent Mode allows Copilot to take multiple sequential actions inside a Word document — planning the work, executing each step, and checking back with you at key points. For educators, this means you can prompt Copilot to "Create a complete differentiated lesson pack for [TOPIC]: a main lesson plan, an extension challenge, a support scaffold, and a one-page parent summary" and Copilot will build all four documents from a single instruction. Enable Agent Mode via the Copilot pane in Word — look for the "Agent" toggle at the top of the panel.

🤖 Copilot Studio — Custom Education Bots Requires Admin

Copilot Studio lets you (or your IT team) build custom AI assistants trained on your school's content. Examples: a Homework Helper bot trained on your year group's curriculum documents that students can ask questions of; a Curriculum Q&A bot that teaching staff can query to find alignment documentation; or a Policy Assistant for the staff room. These bots can be deployed into Teams, a school website, or shared via a link. Access Copilot Studio at copilotstudio.microsoft.com — requires a Power Platform licence in addition to M365 Copilot.

🔍 BizChat — Query Across All Your M365 Content Copilot Chat

BizChat (Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat) is the most powerful feature most educators haven't discovered yet. It allows you to query across your entire M365 footprint — emails, Teams messages, SharePoint documents, meeting transcripts, and OneNote notebooks — in a single natural language question. Try asking: "What did we decide about the Year 9 curriculum review in last month's staff meeting?" or "Find all emails from parents about the upcoming sports day." Use the Work tab (not Web tab) to access organisational content. Access via copilot.microsoft.com or the Copilot app in Teams.

⚙️ Power Automate — Set-and-Forget Workflows Power Platform

Power Automate (included in most M365 plans) lets you build automated workflows that Copilot can now help you create with natural language. Education examples: automatically email parents when an assignment is marked in Teams; generate a weekly class summary report from OneNote; send reminder emails to students who haven't submitted work; route parent enquiries from a shared inbox to the right teacher. Open Power Automate from the M365 app launcher and describe the workflow you want — Copilot will help you build the automation step by step.

05

Prompt Library

15 ready-to-use prompts across 5 teaching categories

Copy any prompt, replace the bracketed placeholders, and use it directly in the relevant M365 app. Each prompt is written to give Copilot the context it needs to produce a useful first draft — rather than a generic response.

LESSON PLAN — WORD
"Draft a 50-minute lesson plan for [TOPIC] for [YEAR/GRADE]. Reference /[CURRICULUM-DOC] for alignment. Include: learning objectives (3 max, written as 'Students will be able to...'), a 10-minute engaging starter, main activity with support and extension differentiation, and an exit ticket. Format as a structured table."
UNIT OVERVIEW — WORD
"Create a 6-week unit overview for [SUBJECT] — [UNIT TITLE] for [YEAR/GRADE]. Structure as: week-by-week learning intentions, key vocabulary per week, assessment points (formative and summative), and cross-curricular links. Reference /[SCHEME-OF-WORK] to ensure sequence alignment."
DIFFERENTIATED MATERIALS — WORD (AGENT MODE)
"Using Agent Mode: Create a complete differentiated resource pack for a lesson on [TOPIC] for [YEAR/GRADE]. Produce three versions: (1) Core task — grade-level language and standard scaffolding; (2) Support version — simplified language, sentence starters, visual prompts; (3) Extension challenge — higher-order thinking questions and independent research task. Format each as a separate printable worksheet."
HOMEWORK TASK — WORD
"Draft a homework task for [YEAR/GRADE] students based on our recent lesson on [TOPIC]. The task should take approximately [TIME] to complete, require no additional resources beyond what students have already, and include: clear instructions, a success criteria checklist, and a reflection question at the end."
EXCEL RUBRIC SCORING — EXCEL
"Build a rubric scoring spreadsheet for [ASSESSMENT TASK]. Criteria: [list 4–5 criteria]. Scoring scale: 1–4 (1=Beginning, 2=Developing, 3=Meeting, 4=Exceeding). For each criterion, generate descriptor text for each score level. Add a weighted total column — [CRITERION] should be weighted at [X]%."
QUIZ GENERATION — WORD
"Generate a 15-question quiz on [TOPIC] for [YEAR/GRADE]. Include: 5 multiple-choice questions (4 options each), 5 short-answer questions (2–3 sentence responses expected), and 5 true/false questions. Include an answer key as a separate section at the end. Difficulty: [easy/medium/hard]."
PERFORMANCE ANALYSIS — EXCEL
"Analyse this student performance data across [NUMBER] assessments. Identify: (1) top 20% of performers, (2) students in the bottom 20% who need immediate support, (3) students showing a downward trend across assessments, (4) any assessment where the class average was significantly below expected. Produce a summary table and suggest 3 differentiation actions."
POWERPOINT DECK — POWERPOINT
"Create a presentation for [TOPIC] suitable for [YEAR/GRADE] students. Include: a title slide, 4 content slides (each with a headline, 3 key points as bullet points, and a visual suggestion), a class discussion slide with 2 open-ended questions, and a recap slide listing the 3 key takeaways. Tone: engaging and age-appropriate for [age group]."
STUDENT HANDOUT — WORD
"Create a one-page student reference handout for our lesson on [TOPIC]. Include: a brief introduction (3–4 sentences), key vocabulary with definitions (8–10 terms), a 'how it works' explanation section, 3 example problems or applications, and a 'check your understanding' section with 4 questions students can self-check."
VISUAL AID — POWERPOINT
"Create a single-slide visual aid for [CONCEPT/PROCESS] for [YEAR/GRADE]. The slide should illustrate [the process / the relationship between / the structure of] [TOPIC] with a clear diagram layout, labelled components, and no more than 20 words of text. Suggest the most effective diagram type for this concept (flowchart / cycle / comparison / hierarchy)."
PARENT EMAIL — OUTLOOK
"Draft a professional email to parents about [TOPIC/SITUATION]. Context: [brief description]. The tone should be warm, clear, and reassuring. Include: a brief explanation of the situation, what the school is doing about it, and what parents can do to support. After drafting, review the tone — flag any phrasing that might cause concern or be misread as critical."
TEAMS ANNOUNCEMENT
"Write a Teams channel announcement for [TOPIC] addressed to [teaching staff / department / year team]. Keep it under 150 words. Include: what's happening, when, who needs to act, and what the deadline is. Make the call to action clear — tell people exactly what they need to do and by when."
NEWSLETTER CONTENT — WORD
"Write the [SUBJECT] section for this term's parent newsletter. Include: a 100-word update on what students have been learning this term, highlights from any special projects or events, what's coming up next term, and one thing parents can do at home to support their child's learning. Tone: celebratory, warm, and community-focused."
ONENOTE STUDENT SUMMARY
"Summarise all notes in this OneNote section about [STUDENT ID / TOPIC]. Structure the summary as: (1) Overview of progress — what's going well, (2) Concerns or patterns — what's been flagged, (3) Interventions already tried and their outcomes, (4) Recommended next steps for this term. Keep the summary concise — under 300 words."
BIZCHAT CROSS-APP QUERY — COPILOT CHAT
"Search across my emails, Teams messages, and meeting transcripts from the last [TIMEFRAME]. Find everything related to [TOPIC/DECISION]. Summarise: what was decided, who was involved, what action items were assigned, and what (if anything) is still unresolved. Include links to the source documents where available."

Measuring Your Impact

Track what's working so you can build on it

The educators who get the most from Copilot are the ones who track their usage and identify which workflows save the most time. Start simple — log what you use Copilot for each week and note the time saved. After 4–6 weeks you'll have clear evidence of where it's most valuable in your practice.

Your 4-Week Measurement Framework
1

Week 1: Baseline — Time How Long Tasks Take Without Copilot

Before you change anything, log how long your current lesson planning, email drafting, and assessment work takes. Use a simple note in OneNote or a row in Excel. This is your benchmark.

2

Weeks 2–3: Use Copilot and Track Time Saved

Start using Copilot for 2–3 tasks per week. Log each use: what task, how long it took with Copilot, and whether the output was usable first-try or needed significant editing. Note which prompts worked best.

3

Week 4: Review and Double Down

Review your log. Which workflows saved the most time? Which prompts produced the best results with least editing? Standardise your best prompts — save them in a OneNote prompt library so you don't have to rewrite them each time.

4

Ongoing: Share What Works with Your Team

Share your best prompts with your department via a Teams channel. Create a shared OneNote notebook of educator prompts. The teachers who improve fastest are the ones who share learnings with colleagues.

📌
Quality Over Quantity

Don't measure success by the number of prompts you run. Measure it by the quality of your teaching materials and the time you get back to spend with students. A single well-crafted prompt that saves 45 minutes is worth more than ten shallow ones that require heavy editing.

Common Mistakes & How to Fix Them

Five Copilot-specific pitfalls educators hit — and the exact fix for each

These are the most common mistakes educators make when starting with Copilot. Each one is easily corrected once you know what to look for.

❌
Mistake 1: Not Using / to Reference Files — Writing prompts without referencing existing curriculum documents, leaving Copilot to generate generic content with no alignment to your actual scheme of work.
✅
Fix: Always use the / shortcut to reference your curriculum documents, rubrics, and past lesson plans stored in OneDrive. Type / in the Copilot prompt bar and search for the file by name. Copilot will read the document and ensure its output is aligned to your existing materials.
❌
Mistake 2: Generic Prompts Without App Context — Asking Copilot to "write a lesson plan" without specifying the subject, year level, duration, app context, or desired output format — resulting in bland, unusable drafts.
✅
Fix: Always include: (1) the subject and topic, (2) the year level or age group, (3) any specific M365 files to reference, and (4) the structure you want as output. The more context you provide, the less editing you'll need. Use the prompts in Section 05 as templates.
❌
Mistake 3: Ignoring Agent Mode in Word — Using Copilot only for single-task drafting when Agent Mode is available, missing the ability to produce complete multi-document packs from a single prompt.
✅
Fix: Enable Agent Mode in the Copilot pane in Word (look for the Agent toggle). Use it for your most complex tasks — creating a full differentiated lesson pack, drafting a complete unit of work, or compiling a student progress report. Agent Mode plans and executes multi-step document creation, including creating separate documents and formatting tables automatically.
❌
Mistake 4: Sharing Student Personal Data in Copilot Chat — Including student names, dates of birth, home addresses, or other personally identifiable information (PII) in Copilot prompts, which may conflict with your school's data governance policy and GDPR obligations.
✅
Fix: Always use anonymised descriptions in Copilot prompts. Instead of "Write a report for Emma Thompson (Year 9, DOB 12/03/2011)," use "Write a report for a Year 9 student who..." Student IDs or anonymised codes are safer than names. Check your school's Copilot data governance policy with your IT team — most M365 Copilot data stays within your tenant, but local policy takes precedence.
❌
Mistake 5: Accepting Copilot's Output Without Checking Curriculum Alignment — Publishing or distributing lesson materials directly from Copilot without verifying they align to your school's curriculum, assessment criteria, or year-level expectations.
✅
Fix: Treat every Copilot output as a strong first draft, not a final product. Always review against your curriculum documents, check factual accuracy (especially for subject-specific content), and confirm that the language is appropriate for your year group. Build a quick checklist: (1) Objectives match curriculum? (2) Content factually accurate? (3) Language age-appropriate? (4) Assessment criteria aligned?
💡
The 10-Minute Review Rule

Budget 10 minutes to review every significant Copilot output before using it. A lesson plan that saves 30 minutes to draft should take 10 minutes to review and refine — still a net 20-minute saving. Never skip the review entirely.
