What is Copilot in OneNote?
AI-assisted note-taking, knowledge capture, and notebook synthesis

OneNote is where many people already capture meeting notes, research, and personal knowledge. Copilot in OneNote turns those notebooks from a passive archive into a queryable knowledge base — summarising sections, generating to-do lists from meeting notes, and pulling answers from across an entire notebook.

Best for: anyone with a heavily-used OneNote, especially consultants, executive assistants, students, and project managers.

Opening Copilot in OneNote
The two access points

Copilot in OneNote shows up in two places — the right-side panel and right-click context menus. Both produce the same outputs; the difference is workflow. The panel is for sustained interaction (multi-prompt sessions, refining outputs). Right-click is for one-shot actions on selected content (summarise this section, extract action items, rewrite this paragraph).

  • Home ribbon → Copilot button — opens the side panel
  • Right-click in a page → Copilot — context-aware actions on the selected text or section

Drafting in OneNote
Notes-to-content workflows

Copilot in OneNote can take messy bullets and produce a structured output — a memo, an email draft, a meeting agenda, or a status update — without leaving the notebook.

  • 'Draft a project status email for stakeholders based on this week's notes'
  • 'Turn these meeting notes into a structured agenda for next week'
  • 'Rewrite these rough bullets as a polished briefing memo'

Drafting in OneNote benefits from OneNote's specific affordance: notebooks are already organised by project, client, or topic. When you ask Copilot to draft from your notes, the relevant context is already there — Copilot doesn't need to be told who the audience is or what's been discussed; it can read the surrounding pages.

This is what differentiates OneNote drafting from drafting in Word or Outlook with Copilot. In Word, Copilot reads the current document. In Outlook, it reads the current thread. In OneNote, it reads the project context — which is often what you actually need for a stakeholder update, a status memo, or a project brief.

Tag and Organise
Copilot as your notebook archivist

Copilot can auto-tag pages with topics, surface relevant tags across sections, and suggest where to file new content. For people with chaotic notebooks, this is the biggest single time-saver.

  • Auto-tag: 'Tag this page with the topics it covers'
  • Cross-reference: 'Show me everything in this notebook tagged Project Phoenix'
  • File suggestions: 'Where should this new page go?'

For people with chaotic OneNote notebooks — which is most heavy OneNote users — the tag-and-organise capability is the biggest single time-saver. Notebooks accumulate over years; finding the page you wrote 18 months ago about a recurring topic is genuinely hard without tags, and most people don't tag consistently.

Copilot can retrospectively tag old content. Pick a notebook, ask Copilot to identify the major topics, then have it auto-tag pages with those topics. What was previously a 30-minute hunt becomes a 30-second query.

The retrospective tagging workflow

  • Pick one notebook (start with your most-used, not your largest)
  • Ask Copilot: 'What are the major topics in this notebook?'
  • Refine the topic list — combine similar ones, split too-broad ones
  • Ask Copilot to auto-tag pages with those topics
  • Spot-check 20 pages — adjust tags where wrong
  • Use the tags for future cross-referencing

Integration With Other M365 Surfaces
Where OneNote sits in the stack
  • Teams → OneNote — embed a notebook as a tab in any channel
  • Outlook → OneNote — send an email to OneNote with one click
  • Word → OneNote — promote a page to a Word doc for formal sharing
  • Copilot Chat — reference a OneNote page with the / command

✅ DO THIS

  • Use OneNote as the capture surface; promote outputs to formal docs
  • Tag pages immediately so they're findable later
  • Leverage cross-notebook search for long-running project knowledge
  • Use Copilot Chat with / to reference OneNote pages cross-app
  • Maintain one notebook per major context (project, client, function)
  • Quarterly notebook hygiene — archive completed projects, prune stale pages

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Treating OneNote as the final document format — it's the capture surface
  • Letting tags drift without curation — they lose value
  • Storing sensitive credentials or PII in OneNote without sensitivity labels
  • Mixing personal and work content in shared notebooks
  • Skipping the cross-notebook search feature — it's the 2026 unlock
  • Forgetting that permissions apply — Copilot inherits OneNote access

Common Mistakes & Fixes
What goes wrong — and how to avoid it

Most AI workflows that 'don't work' fall into one of these patterns. The fix is almost always sharper context and stricter review, not switching models or tools.

  • Treating Copilot in OneNote as a finished answer rather than a draft to review
  • Vague prompts that don't specify audience, format, or what you want changed
  • Forgetting to give it context — the file, the brief, the constraints
  • Skipping the verify step — always check numbers, dates, names, and policy claims
  • Using it for tasks where a 30-second human action would be faster

✅ DO THIS

  • Provide context — the file, the brief, the audience, the constraints
  • Iterate; don't just accept the first output
  • Always review numbers, names, dates, and policy claims
  • Treat AI as a fast draft generator, not an answer engine
  • Capture what works so you can replicate it
  • Match the model to the task — fast modes for drafts, reasoning modes for analysis

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Sending AI output without reading it end-to-end
  • Vague prompts with no audience, format, or success criteria
  • Assuming AI can see files or context it hasn't been given
  • Using AI for tasks faster done manually in under a minute
  • Treating numbers, quotes, or citations as verified without checking
  • Defaulting to the slow reasoning mode for every task

Suggested Prompts
Ready-to-use prompts you can adapt to your work

These prompts are designed to land in your real work today. Replace the bracketed placeholders with your actual context — the more specific you make them, the better the output. Treat each one as a starting brief, not a finished script.

MEETING NOTES → MEMO
Turn these meeting notes into a 1-page memo for stakeholders who weren't there. Cover: what was decided, what's outstanding, who's doing what next, and any risks raised.
ACTION ITEM EXTRACTION
Find every action item in this section. For each: who owns it, what's the deadline (if stated), and what's the current status (in progress / blocked / done) if I noted it.
NOTEBOOK SYNTHESIS
I've been keeping notes on Project Atlas for 3 months across 14 pages. Pull together a one-page brief for someone new joining the project — what we've decided, what's open, where the risks are.
