What is Copilot in Dynamics 365?
AI-assisted CRM, ERP, and customer service workflows inside Dynamics

Copilot in Dynamics 365 lives inside Microsoft's CRM and ERP surfaces — Sales, Customer Service, Field Service, Finance, and Supply Chain. It uses the same Copilot intelligence as the M365 surfaces but with deep awareness of Dynamics data: accounts, contacts, opportunities, cases, orders, and financial records.

Best for: sellers writing follow-ups, service agents resolving cases faster, finance teams drafting variance commentary, and field service techs prepping for site visits.

Sales Agent and Customer Service Agent
Autonomous agents inside Dynamics

The Sales Agent autonomously researches accounts, sends qualifying outreach, and books meetings. The Customer Service Agent handles tier-1 cases end-to-end where the playbook is unambiguous. Both inherit your security model and route to humans for anything outside their explicit scope.

🤖 ALWAYS GATE AGENT ACTIONS
For agents that write back to records, send external emails, or commit to customer-facing actions — gate them with approval steps for the first 60 days. Once you've reviewed 50+ actions and the false-positive rate is acceptable, relax the gate.

Without Dynamics Copilot, your CRM doesn't benefit from the email and meeting context M365 Copilot has. Together they close the loop — meetings update CRM, CRM context informs follow-up drafts, and so on.

Common Mistakes & Fixes
What goes wrong — and how to avoid it

Most AI workflows that 'don't work' fall into one of these patterns. The fix is almost always sharper context and stricter review, not switching models or tools.

  • Treating Copilot in Dynamics 365 as a finished answer rather than a draft to review
  • Vague prompts that don't specify audience, format, or what you want changed
  • Forgetting to give it context — the file, the brief, the constraints
  • Skipping the verify step — always check numbers, dates, names, and policy claims
  • Using it for tasks where a 30-second human action would be faster

✅ DO THIS

  • Provide context — the file, the brief, the audience, the constraints
  • Iterate; don't just accept the first output
  • Always review numbers, names, dates, and policy claims
  • Treat AI as a fast draft generator, not an answer engine
  • Capture what works so you can replicate it
  • Match the model to the task — fast modes for drafts, reasoning modes for analysis

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Sending AI output without reading it end-to-end
  • Vague prompts with no audience, format, or success criteria
  • Assuming AI can see files or context it hasn't been given
  • Using AI for tasks faster done manually in under a minute
  • Treating numbers, quotes, or citations as verified without checking
  • Defaulting to the slow reasoning mode for every task

Suggested Prompts
Ready-to-use prompts you can adapt to your work

These prompts are designed to land in your real work today. Replace the bracketed placeholders with your actual context — the more specific you make them, the better the output. Treat each one as a starting brief, not a finished script.

PRE-CALL BRIEF — SALES
I have a call in 30 minutes with [account]. Brief me: account history, last 5 interactions, open opportunities, recent news about them, and 3 questions I should ask based on their stage.
CASE WRAP-UP — SERVICE
Summarise this case for the wrap-up note. Include: root cause, resolution steps, customer sentiment at close, any follow-up needed, and KB article that would prevent this recurring.
VARIANCE COMMENTARY — FINANCE
Draft the variance commentary for this cost centre. Compare this month to budget. Flag the 3 biggest swings, give plain-language reasons based on the data, and suggest what to investigate further.
