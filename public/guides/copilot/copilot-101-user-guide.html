Copilot 101 — Getting Started & Interface Walkthrough

Everything you need to activate, navigate, and start using Microsoft Copilot across your M365 apps — from confirming your licence to writing your first prompts and understanding what Copilot can access.

Version 1.0 M365 Copilot Updated: May 2026 Audience: New Copilot Users
01
What is Microsoft Copilot?
AI assistance built into the tools you already use

Microsoft Copilot for M365 is an AI assistant embedded directly into the Microsoft 365 suite — Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, Teams, OneNote, and more. Unlike a standalone AI chatbot, Copilot connects to your actual work: your emails, documents, meetings, calendar, and chats. It can read what you're working on and act on it in context.

Copilot is powered by the GPT-5 family of models (including GPT-5.2 in the Chat model selector) combined with Microsoft Graph — the data layer that connects your M365 content. You can switch between Quick Response for everyday drafting and Think Deeper for harder analysis and reasoning tasks. Copilot doesn't just answer generic questions — it can summarise your last meeting, draft a reply to your actual email thread, or analyse your specific spreadsheet.

✍️
Write & Draft
Generate emails, reports, presentations and documents from simple instructions — in any tone or format you need.
🔍
Summarise & Analyse
Condense long email threads, meeting transcripts, and documents into clear key points and action items.
🎨
Create & Transform
Turn rough notes into polished slides, convert tables into charts, or restructure content for a different audience.
💬
Chat & Query
Ask natural language questions about your files, emails, and calendar — across all your M365 data in one conversation.
ℹ️
Two tiers — know which one you have. Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat is included free with eligible M365 commercial subscriptions and gives you the standalone Chat experience with web grounding. Microsoft 365 Copilot (Business or Enterprise) is a paid add-on that unlocks the in-app Copilot panels in Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, Teams, and more — plus access to your tenant data through Microsoft Graph. Confirm your tier with your IT admin before troubleshooting missing features.

What Copilot Is Not

  • Not a search engine. Copilot reasons from context you provide and your M365 data. Copilot Chat does include web grounding (the old Bing Chat Enterprise experience, now rebranded) — but in-app Copilot stays within your M365 content unless you explicitly enable web search.
  • Not always right. Copilot can make errors, especially with numbers, dates, and nuanced instructions. Switch to Think Deeper for harder analysis — but always review AI-generated content before sending or publishing.
  • Not a replacement for judgment. Copilot accelerates your work — it doesn't replace your expertise, context, or decision-making.

Getting Started
Licences, activation, and your first Copilot interaction

Before Copilot appears in your M365 apps, two things must be in place: the right licence and admin activation. Once both are confirmed, Copilot shows up automatically — no extra software to install. The Copilot button (a sparkle ✨ icon) appears in the ribbon of Word, Outlook, Teams, Excel, and PowerPoint.

1
Identify your Copilot tier
If your organisation has an eligible M365 commercial subscription, you already have Copilot Chat free — the standalone Chat experience. For in-app Copilot (Word, Excel, Outlook, Teams panels), you also need the paid Microsoft 365 Copilot Business ($18–$21/user/mo) or Enterprise ($30/user/mo) add-on. Check with your IT admin to confirm which you have.
2
Check that admin has enabled Copilot
Your Microsoft 365 admin must assign the paid Copilot licence to your account in the M365 Admin Centre before the in-app Copilot buttons appear. Copilot Chat (the free tier) is enabled by default for eligible subscriptions. If you can't see the in-app Copilot button, raise a ticket with IT — it's almost always a licence-assignment issue.
3
Open any M365 app and find the Copilot button
Open Word, Outlook, PowerPoint, Excel, or Teams. Look for the ✨ Copilot button in the Home ribbon (desktop apps) or in the top right area (web versions). Click it to open the Copilot panel.
4
Try Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat at m365.cloud.microsoft
Copilot Chat is the most flexible entry point — it spans your apps and (with the paid tier) your tenant data. Go to m365.cloud.microsoft, sign in with your work account, pick Quick Response or Think Deeper from the model selector, and ask it to summarise your recent emails or find a document.
💡
Best first move: Open Teams, join or find a recent meeting, and click Recap. Copilot will generate a summary with action items instantly — no prompting needed. This is the fastest way to see Copilot's value on day one.

What You Can Do Right Now

📧

Open a long email thread and click Summarise. Copilot extracts key points and next steps in seconds.
💬

Open a past meeting and click Recap. Copilot produces a full summary with action items and key decisions.

Open a blank document, click Copilot, and type "Draft a project update for stakeholders about [topic]."

The Copilot Interface
Where to find Copilot in each M365 app — and what each panel does

Copilot doesn't have a single fixed home. It appears as a sidebar panel, a floating chat window, or an inline prompt bar depending on which M365 app you're in. The interface is consistent in feel — navy/blue tones, a sparkle icon, and a text input — but the capabilities vary by app context.

Anatomy of the Copilot Panel

Across all apps, the Copilot panel has the same core structure:

  • Model selector: Switch between Quick Response (fast, default) and Think Deeper (slower, better reasoning for analysis, code, and multi-step problems) — visible at the top of Copilot Chat and in many in-app panels
  • Suggested prompts: Quick-action buttons at the top tailored to what you're currently working on
  • Chat input: A text box where you type your request in plain English
  • Response area: Where Copilot displays its output — text, bullet lists, tables, or drafted content
  • Insert / Copy buttons: Apply Copilot's output directly into your document or copy to clipboard
  • Keep / Discard: Accept or reject drafted content before it's added to your file
✨
Copilot Chat is the power tool. Unlike app-specific Copilot panels (which only see the currently open file), Copilot Chat can reference content across all your emails, documents, meetings, and calendar — in a single conversation. Access it at m365.cloud.microsoft or via the Copilot icon in the Microsoft 365 app launcher. With the paid tier it also reaches your tenant data through Microsoft Graph; on the free tier it stays at the chat-and-web-grounding level.

Writing Effective Prompts for Copilot
The patterns that get dramatically better results

Copilot understands plain English — no special commands or syntax needed. But there are four elements that consistently improve output quality. Think of each prompt as a brief: the more clearly you brief Copilot, the better it performs.

ElementWhy It MattersExample
ContextTells Copilot what you're working on and who is involved"Based on our Q3 board meeting transcript…"
TaskSpecific instruction — what you want produced"…write an executive summary…"
FormatHow the output should be structured or presented"…as 5 bullet points, max 20 words each…"
Tone / AudienceRegister, style, and who will read it"…suitable for a non-technical board audience"

Weak vs. Strong Prompts

❌
Weak: "Summarise the meeting." — Copilot will produce a generic summary. It doesn't know which meeting, what's important, who needs it, or what format you want.
✅
Strong: "Summarise today's product roadmap meeting. List the 3 key decisions made, the 4 action items with owners, and any unresolved issues. Format as a short email I can send to the wider team."

The Copilot Prompt Formula

Microsoft's own recommended prompt structure: [Goal] + [Context] + [Source] + [Expectations]

  • Goal: What do you want? (Summarise, Draft, Analyse, List, Rewrite)
  • Context: What's the background? (This is for a client proposal / my manager asked for this)
  • Source: What should Copilot use? (This document / my last 5 emails / the meeting transcript)
  • Expectations: Format, length, tone, audience
ℹ️
Iterate, don't start over. If Copilot's first response isn't right, respond with what needs changing — "Make it shorter", "Change the tone to be more formal", "Add a section about risks". Copilot maintains context within the same conversation.

Working with Your M365 Data
What Copilot can access — and what it can't

One of Copilot's core advantages is that it works with your actual content — not just general knowledge. Understanding what Copilot can and can't see helps you use it more effectively and avoids frustrating dead ends.

Data SourceWhat Copilot Can AccessNotes
Current documentFull content of the currently open fileWorks in Word, Excel, PowerPoint, OneNote
Email threadsSelected email and its thread historyIn Outlook; can also be queried via Copilot Chat
Meeting transcriptsAuto-generated Teams transcriptsRequires transcription to be enabled in Teams admin
CalendarYour meetings, invites, and attendeesVia Copilot Chat (paid tier) with Microsoft Graph access
OneDrive filesFiles you own or have been shared with youReference by name in Copilot Chat; must be in OneDrive/SharePoint
SharePointShared documents your account can viewMust have read permissions on the file or site
Teams chat & channelsChannels and chats you're a member ofAccessible via Copilot Chat with appropriate permissions
⚠️
Permissions apply. Copilot only accesses content you have permission to see. It cannot access files, emails, or channels you're not authorised for — even if a colleague mentions them. Existing Microsoft 365 data governance, sensitivity labels, and DLP policies are all enforced. Copilot does not bypass security controls.

How to Reference Files in Copilot Chat

In Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat, you can explicitly reference files, emails, and meetings using the / command:

  • Type / and start typing a file or document name — Copilot searches your OneDrive and SharePoint
  • Type / + meeting name or date to reference a specific meeting transcript
  • You can reference up to 3 items in a single prompt
  • Example: "Summarise /Q3 Budget Review.xlsx and identify any items that relate to /Project Phoenix Proposal.docx"
🤖
Agents can now act, not just summarise. With Wave 1 agentic features (rolled out 2025–2026), Copilot agents can execute multi-step tasks against your Graph data — drafting a reply and sending it, pulling figures from Excel into a Word brief, or scheduling a follow-up. Always review what an agent is about to do before approving — agents inherit your permissions and your accountability.

Advanced Techniques
Power-user patterns for getting more from Copilot

Multi-Turn Conversations

Copilot maintains context within a single session — each follow-up message builds on the previous response. Use this to refine outputs without re-explaining everything. Start broad, then narrow: ask for a draft, then say "Make the intro shorter and more direct", then "Add a bullet list of risks at the end."

Combining Apps for Complex Tasks

The most powerful Copilot workflows chain apps together. For example: use Copilot Chat to identify the key themes from last quarter's client emails → ask Word Copilot to draft a report based on those themes → use PowerPoint Copilot to turn the Word report into a deck. Each step uses the previous output as input.

Using Copilot in Live Meetings

During a Teams meeting (with transcription enabled), you can open the Copilot panel and ask questions in real-time — without interrupting the call. Ask: "What has been agreed so far?", "What are the open questions?", or "Summarise what [name] said about the budget." This is especially useful for joining late or following complex technical discussions.

Rewriting and Tone Adjustment

In Word and Outlook, Copilot can rewrite existing content — not just generate new content. Select a paragraph and ask: "Make this more concise", "Change the tone to be more assertive", "Rewrite this for a non-technical audience", or "Translate this into a formal email." This is faster than editing manually for large blocks of text.

Pick the Right Reasoning Mode

Copilot Chat (and many in-app panels) now expose a model selector with two practical modes. Use Quick Response for everyday drafting, summarising, and rewriting — it's fast and good enough for the majority of tasks. Switch to Think Deeper when the prompt involves analysis, multi-step logic, code reasoning, or trade-offs you actually want it to chew on. The trade-off is latency: Think Deeper takes noticeably longer per response, so don't default to it for short tasks.

✅ Do This

  • Give Copilot specific context about your audience and goal
  • Iterate on responses — treat it as a conversation
  • Reference specific files using / in Copilot Chat
  • Use Copilot to start drafts — then edit them yourself
  • Enable meeting transcription to unlock full Teams Copilot
  • Review and verify all Copilot-generated content before sharing

❌ Avoid This

  • Sending Copilot drafts without reviewing them first
  • Vague prompts with no context or format instructions
  • Assuming Copilot can see files it hasn't been pointed to
  • Treating Copilot's numbers or dates as fact without checking
  • Using Copilot for sensitive legal or financial decisions without expert review
  • Starting a new conversation when you could continue iterating

Common Mistakes & Fixes
What goes wrong — and how to solve it
❓ "I can't see the Copilot button in Word / Outlook / Teams."
This is almost always a licence or admin issue. First, confirm with your IT admin that your account has been assigned a Microsoft 365 Copilot licence in the M365 Admin Centre. Second, make sure your Microsoft 365 apps are updated to a recent build — older versions may not display Copilot. If you're using a web browser, try refreshing or signing out and back in.
❓ "Copilot gave me a response that seems completely wrong or made up."
Copilot can hallucinate — especially with specific numbers, dates, names, or details not present in the source content. Always verify factual claims, particularly in Excel analysis or meeting summaries. If results seem off, re-prompt with more specific instructions: "Only use information from the document I've provided — do not add external information."
❓ "Copilot Chat can't find the file I'm asking about."
The file must be stored in OneDrive or SharePoint — not on your local drive. Copilot cannot access files saved only to your desktop or local Documents folder. Move or save the file to OneDrive first. Also check that the file name is spelled correctly and that you have access to it yourself — Copilot inherits your permissions.
❓ "The Copilot Recap isn't available for our Teams meetings."
Teams meeting Recap requires transcription to be enabled — both at the admin level (your IT team must enable it in Teams admin policies) and at the meeting level (the organiser must start transcription during the meeting). If transcription wasn't running, there's no transcript to summarise. Going forward, enable "Auto-start transcription" in your Teams meeting settings or policy.
❓ "Copilot in Excel isn't working — the button is greyed out."
Excel Copilot requires your data to be formatted as a proper Excel Table (not just a plain range). Select your data, go to Insert → Table, and confirm your data has headers. Once formatted as a table, the Copilot button becomes active. Also ensure the file is saved to OneDrive or SharePoint, not locally — Copilot in Excel requires cloud-connected files.
❓ "Think Deeper is slow and the answer isn't obviously better."
Think Deeper is built for harder reasoning — analysis, code, multi-step logic, trade-offs. For drafting an email, summarising a doc, or rewriting a paragraph, Quick Response is usually equally good and much faster. Reserve Think Deeper for prompts where you'd accept a longer wait in exchange for more careful reasoning. If a Quick Response answer looks too shallow, re-prompt with Think Deeper — don't default to it.

Suggested Prompts
Ready-to-use prompts for your first week with Copilot

These prompts are designed to get you results immediately. Adjust the specifics to match your actual work context — the more relevant detail you add, the better the output.

First Meeting Recap — Teams
"Summarise this meeting. List the 3 main topics discussed, any decisions made, and all action items with the name of the person responsible. Format as a structured recap I can paste into an email."
Email Thread Summary — Outlook
"Summarise this email thread. What is the core issue being discussed, what has been agreed, and what is the outstanding question or next step? Keep it to 5 bullet points."
First Document Draft — Word
"Draft a 1-page project update for [project name]. Include a brief overview of status, 3 key achievements this month, 2 risks or blockers, and the top 3 priorities for next month. Audience: senior management. Tone: confident and direct."
Cross-App Discovery — Copilot Chat
"What has been discussed about [topic or project name] in my recent emails and meetings? Summarise the key points and any pending actions related to this topic."
Draft a Reply — Outlook
"Draft a reply to this email. Confirm that I can attend the meeting on [date], ask for the agenda in advance, and let them know I'll bring [colleague name] as well. Tone: friendly and professional. Keep it under 100 words."
Presentation from a Brief — PowerPoint
"Create a 6-slide presentation on [topic]. Slide 1: title slide. Slides 2–5: one key point each with 3 supporting bullet points. Slide 6: summary and next steps. Audience: [describe audience]. Tone: [formal/professional/engaging]."
Data Insight — Excel
"Analyse this table and tell me: what are the top 3 performing items by [metric], which items are below average, and are there any unusual patterns or outliers I should be aware of?"
Meeting Prep — Copilot Chat
"I have a meeting with [name/team] tomorrow about [topic]. Based on my recent emails and documents related to this topic, what are the key points I should be across, and what questions should I come prepared to answer?"

Your Roadmap
Four steps from activation to confident daily use

Copilot becomes more valuable the more you use it — but most people plateau at the obvious features. Here's a progression path to get from activated to genuinely integrated into your daily workflow.

01
Activate & Explore
Confirm your licence, find Copilot in each app, try the Teams Recap and Outlook Summarise features. Get a feel for what Copilot can access.
02
Daily Quick Wins
Use Copilot for meeting summaries, email thread catch-ups, and first-draft documents every day for one week. Build the habit before expanding.
03
Go Cross-App
Start using Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat to query across emails, docs, and meetings at once. Build prompts that chain from one app to another.
04
Automate & Scale
Build reusable prompt templates for recurring tasks. Explore Copilot Studio for custom agents. Share workflows with your team to multiply the impact.
✨
The fastest way to build Copilot skills is through deliberate use. Pick one workflow this week — meeting recaps, email drafts, or document summaries — and commit to using Copilot for every instance of it. Consistency builds confidence faster than experimenting across all features at once.
