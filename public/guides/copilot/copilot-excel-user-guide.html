What is Copilot in Excel?
AI data analysis for everyone — no formulas required

Copilot in Excel brings AI analysis directly into your spreadsheets. You can ask questions about your data in plain English and get back insights, calculations, formulas, and charts — without needing to know the syntax yourself. Whether you're a spreadsheet expert or an occasional user, Copilot makes data analysis faster and more accessible.

Copilot works on the data in your active Excel table. It can identify trends, calculate totals and averages, write complex formulas, apply conditional formatting, generate charts, and summarise what your data shows — all from a conversational prompt in the Copilot panel.

🔍
Analyse Data
Ask "What are the top 5 products by revenue?" or "Which region is underperforming?" Copilot reads your table and surfaces the answer.

📊
Charts & Visuals
Ask Copilot to create a chart from your data. It selects the right chart type, configures the axes, and inserts it into your workbook.
🎨
Highlight & Format
Ask Copilot to highlight values above a threshold, colour-code categories, or apply conditional formatting rules — described in plain language.
⚠️ Your Data Must Be in a Table
Copilot in Excel only works on data formatted as an Excel Table (Insert → Table, or Ctrl + T). If your data is in a plain range, Copilot will not be able to analyse it. Always convert your data range to a Table first — this is the most common reason Copilot appears greyed out.
🐍 2026 Update — Python in Copilot for Excel
You can now ask Copilot to use Python for advanced analysis directly in the sheet — no Python knowledge required. Type a prompt like "Use Python to forecast next quarter's sales by region using the last 24 months of data" and Copilot generates and runs Python, returning results inline. This unlocks regression, clustering, time-series forecasting, and complex visualisations the standard formula engine can't handle.

Analyse Data with Copilot
Ask questions about your data in plain English

The core Copilot in Excel experience is a conversation with your data. Open the Copilot panel, ask a question, and get an answer — along with the option to insert any resulting calculations or charts directly into your sheet.

1
Ensure your data is formatted as a Table
Click anywhere in your data range, then press Ctrl + T or go to Insert → Table. Confirm the range and tick "My table has headers". Copilot cannot analyse plain ranges.
2
Open the Copilot panel
Click the Copilot button (✨) in the Home ribbon. The Copilot panel opens on the right side of your workbook.
3
Ask a question about your data
Type your question in natural language. Focus on the insight you need: "What is the average order value by region?" or "Which 3 customers account for the most revenue?"
4
Review the result and choose what to do with it
Copilot returns an answer in text, and often suggests inserting a formula, chart, or pivot table. Click "Insert" to add it to your sheet, or ask a follow-up question.

Analysis Questions That Work Well

Type of AnalysisExample Question
Ranking & top/bottom"Show me the top 10 products by sales volume this quarter"
Aggregates & averages"What is the average deal size by sales rep?"
Trends over time"How has monthly revenue changed over the last 12 months?"
Comparisons"Compare Q2 and Q3 performance by department"
Outliers & anomalies"Which rows have a cost that is more than 2x the average?"
Filtering & segmentation"Show only the rows where the status is 'Overdue' and value is above £10,000"
💡 Name Your Columns Clearly
Copilot's analysis quality directly reflects your column headers. "Revenue Q3 (£)" is much better than "ColC" or "Data". Descriptive headers mean Copilot can correctly match your question to the right column — and give you more accurate results.
03
Formula Assistance
Build, explain, and fix formulas in plain English

Copilot can write Excel formulas for you — describe what you want to calculate and Copilot generates the formula, explains it, and offers to insert it into your sheet. It also works the other way: paste a formula you don't understand and ask Copilot to explain it.

✍️
Write a Formula
Describe the calculation: "Write a formula that calculates the percentage each product contributes to total revenue." Copilot writes it and offers to insert it.
❓
Explain a Formula
Paste any formula and ask: "Explain what this formula does in plain English." Copilot walks through each part of the formula step by step.
🔧
Fix a Formula
Paste a broken formula and describe the error: "This VLOOKUP is returning #N/A — what's wrong and how do I fix it?" Copilot diagnoses and corrects it.
🔄
Convert to Modern Syntax
"Convert this VLOOKUP to XLOOKUP." Or "Rewrite this nested IF using IFS." Copilot modernises legacy formulas to cleaner alternatives.

Formula Examples — From Plain English to Excel

Excel Formula
=SUMIF(Table1[Region],"North",Table1[Revenue])
Plain English Request
"Sum the Revenue column but only for rows where the Region is 'North'"
Excel Formula
=XLOOKUP(A2,Products[ID],Products[Price],0)
Plain English Request
"Look up the product ID in column A and return the Price from the Products table — return 0 if not found"
Excel Formula
=IFERROR([@Revenue]/[@Target]-1,"No target")
Plain English Request
"Calculate percentage above or below target, showing 'No target' if the calculation fails"
Excel Formula
=COUNTIFS(Table1[Status],"Overdue",Table1[Value],">"&10000)
Plain English Request
"Count rows where Status is 'Overdue' AND Value is greater than 10,000"
✨ Always Ask for an Explanation
Even if you plan to use the formula as-is, ask Copilot to explain it. Understanding what the formula does helps you verify it's correct for your data — and means you can adapt it yourself next time rather than asking again.
04
Charts & Visualisations
Turn table data into clear, shareable charts

Copilot can create charts directly from your Excel table. Describe the visualisation you want — what data to show, how to group it, and what type of chart — and Copilot builds and inserts it. No need to manually configure axes, series, or chart types.

1
Ask Copilot to create a chart
Example: "Create a bar chart showing total sales by region for this quarter." Copilot selects the relevant columns, picks an appropriate chart type, and configures the layout.
2
Review the chart suggestion
Copilot shows you a preview of the chart in the panel before inserting. You can accept it or ask for adjustments: "Make it a line chart instead" or "Sort the bars from highest to lowest."
3
Click Insert to add it to your sheet
The chart is inserted as a standard Excel chart object. You can move, resize, and edit it using Excel's standard chart tools after insertion.

Highlight & Format
Apply conditional formatting and colour logic in plain English

Conditional formatting rules — colour-coding cells based on their values — are one of Excel's most powerful features, but the setup is fiddly. Copilot lets you describe the formatting rule you want and applies it for you, saving multiple menu steps and rule dialogs.

🔴
Flag Thresholds
"Highlight all cells in the Margin column red where the value is below 10%." Copilot creates the rule and applies it across the column.
🎨
Colour-Code Categories
"Colour the Status column: green for Complete, amber for In Progress, red for Overdue." Copilot sets up three separate rules in one instruction.
📊
Data Bars & Scales
"Add a data bar to the Revenue column so it's easy to visually compare values at a glance." Copilot applies the built-in data bar formatting.
🏆
Top / Bottom Rules
"Highlight the top 10% of values in the Sales column in bold green." Copilot applies a top N% conditional formatting rule automatically.
ℹ️ Copilot Writes the Rules — You Can Edit Them
Conditional formatting applied by Copilot works like any manually created rule. To view, edit, or remove it, go to Home → Conditional Formatting → Manage Rules. This is useful if you want to tweak a colour, adjust a threshold, or apply the rule to a different range.

Summarise & Explain
Get a plain-English narrative from your spreadsheet data

Copilot can generate a written summary of what your data shows — turning rows of numbers into a narrative that you can paste into a report, email, or presentation. This bridges the gap between raw data in Excel and the story it needs to tell to a non-technical audience.

📝
Data Narrative
Ask: "Write a 3-paragraph summary of what this data shows, suitable for including in a board report." Copilot interprets the trends and writes the narrative.
💡
Key Insights
"What are the 3 most important insights in this dataset?" Copilot highlights the most significant patterns, outliers, or changes — framed as business insights.
⚠️
Flags & Anomalies
"Are there any unusual patterns or anomalies in this data I should be aware of?" Copilot looks for statistical outliers, unexpected gaps, or data quality issues.
📊
Column Explanations
"Explain what each column in this table represents and how they relate to each other." Useful when inheriting a spreadsheet you didn't build yourself.
💡 Use the Summary in Other Apps
Copy Copilot's summary from the panel and paste it directly into Copilot in Word or Outlook. Ask: "Expand this data summary into a formal section for our Q3 board report" or "Write a covering email for this analysis." Your Excel insights become the foundation for written deliverables across M365.

Advanced Techniques
Power queries, cross-app flows, and iterative analysis

Iterative Analysis

The most effective Excel Copilot sessions are conversations, not single queries. Ask a broad question to get oriented, then narrow down to the specific insight you need.

1
Start broad
"Give me an overview of this dataset — key stats for each numeric column." Copilot returns ranges, averages, and notable values for the whole table.
2
Drill into an anomaly
"The Revenue column looks unusual in March — what happened? Show the breakdown by product." Copilot focuses in on that specific slice.
3
Build a formula to track it
"Write a formula that flags any month where revenue drops more than 15% from the previous month." Copilot builds the formula and inserts it as a new column.
4
Visualise the finding
"Create a chart showing monthly revenue with the anomaly months highlighted." You now have an analysis trail ready to drop into a report.

Common Mistakes & Fixes
What goes wrong — and how to fix it
❌ "The Copilot button is greyed out in Excel"
Your data is not formatted as an Excel Table. Click anywhere in your data range, press Ctrl + T, confirm the range and headers, then try Copilot again. This resolves the greyed-out button in almost all cases. Also check that you're using a file saved to OneDrive or SharePoint — local .xlsx files that haven't been saved to the cloud may not support all Copilot features.
❌ "Copilot's formula doesn't match my data"
Copilot uses your column headers to match your question to the right columns. If headers are ambiguous (e.g. "Col A", "Value", "Data"), Copilot may reference the wrong column. Rename your headers to be descriptive before asking for formula help. After Copilot generates a formula, always review the column references before inserting.
❌ "Copilot's analysis gives me a different number than I expected"
Check whether your data has blank rows, merged cells, or hidden rows — these can affect Copilot's calculations. Also check whether your question was ambiguous: "total revenue" could mean sum, count, or average depending on context. Refine your question to be explicit: "sum of the Revenue column" rather than "total revenue".
❌ "The chart Copilot created isn't showing the right data"
Be more specific about which columns to use and how to group the data. Instead of "create a sales chart", try "create a bar chart with Months on the X axis and Total Sales on the Y axis, grouped by Region." The more explicit your chart specification, the more accurate the result.
❌ "Copilot can't see my second sheet"
Copilot in Excel currently analyses the active table on your current sheet. It cannot cross-reference multiple sheets or workbooks in a single query. If you need to analyse data from two sheets, consolidate the relevant data into a single table first, then ask Copilot.
✅ DO THIS
  • Convert data to a Table before using Copilot (Ctrl + T)
  • Use clear, descriptive column headers
  • Ask Copilot to explain formulas it generates
  • Verify formula column references before inserting
  • Use iterative questions to drill into insights
❌ AVOID THIS
  • Using Copilot on plain ranges (not formatted as Tables)
  • Ambiguous column headers like "Value" or "Data"
  • Accepting formulas without reviewing the cell references
  • Expecting Copilot to analyse data across multiple sheets
  • Trusting numerical results without a sense-check against the raw data

Suggested Prompts
Ready-to-use prompts for analysis and formulas
Data Overview — First Look
Give me an overview of this dataset. What are the key statistics for each numeric column — minimum, maximum, average, and total? Flag any columns with blank values or outliers I should be aware of.
Performance Ranking
Rank the [column name — e.g. Sales Rep / Product / Region] by total [metric — e.g. Revenue / Units / Profit] from highest to lowest. Show the top 10 and highlight any that are more than 20% above or below the average.
Trend & Anomaly Check
Show the trend in [metric] over time. Are there any months or periods where the value is significantly higher or lower than the surrounding periods? What might explain those anomalies based on the other columns in the data?
Conditional Sum or Count
Write a formula that [sums / counts] the [column name] column, but only for rows where [condition column] equals [value] AND [second condition]. Explain each part of the formula before inserting it.
Status Traffic Light
Apply conditional formatting to the [column name] column: highlight cells green if the value is above [threshold], amber if it's between [lower] and [upper], and red if it's below [lower threshold]. Apply to the whole column.
Lookup with Fallback
Write an XLOOKUP formula that looks up [lookup value column] in the [lookup column] of [table name] and returns the [return column]. If no match is found, return "[fallback text]" instead of an error. Explain the formula before inserting.
