Copilot meets you exactly where your business already runs.

1

Activate Copilot in Word — Draft Your First Proposal

Open a new Word document. Click the Copilot icon in the Home ribbon or press Alt + I to open the Draft with Copilot pane. Type: "Draft a business proposal for [YOUR SERVICE] targeting [TARGET CLIENT]. Include an executive summary, problem statement, our solution, investment, and next steps." Copilot will produce a complete draft in seconds. Use the / shortcut to reference an existing proposal template or brand guide stored in OneDrive.

2

Enable Copilot in Outlook for Customer Templates

In Outlook, compose a new email and look for the Copilot icon in the compose toolbar. Click Draft with Copilot and type a brief instruction: "Draft a polite follow-up email to a customer who hasn't responded to our proposal in 5 days. Keep it warm but professional." Use the tone selector (Formal / Neutral / Casual) to match your brand voice before inserting the draft.

3

Set Up Excel for Financial Tracking

Create a new Excel workbook and enter or import your invoice, expense, or revenue data. Click the Copilot button in the Home ribbon. Ask: "Summarise this data. Identify monthly revenue trends, flag any months where expenses exceeded 70% of revenue, and suggest two ways to improve cash flow." Copilot will analyse your data, create charts, and write a plain-language summary — all without needing formulas or pivot table skills.

4

Configure BizChat (Copilot Chat) to Access Your Files

Open Copilot Chat at copilot.microsoft.com or via the Microsoft 365 app. Switch to the Work tab (not Web) so Copilot can search your OneDrive and SharePoint. Type / in the prompt bar to reference specific documents — your price lists, brand guidelines, past proposals. Store key business documents in your OneDrive so Copilot can retrieve and cross-reference them from any prompt.

5

Enable Meeting Intelligence in Teams

In Microsoft Teams, go to Settings → Notifications and activity and ensure Copilot meeting features are enabled. Before your next meeting, click Start recording and confirm Copilot is active. After the meeting, ask: "Summarise this meeting, list all action items with owners, and flag any decisions that need follow-up." Teams Copilot will generate this automatically from the transcript.

⚠️
Data & Privacy for Small Business

Copilot in M365 for Business processes data within Microsoft's enterprise data protection boundary — your data is not used to train Copilot models. However, avoid pasting customer names, financial account numbers, or confidential contract details into Copilot prompts until you have reviewed your Microsoft 365 data governance settings with your IT contact or Microsoft partner.

03

Your Daily AI Workflows

Six high-impact use cases across Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, and Teams

These six workflows cover the tasks small business owners spend the most time on — and where Copilot delivers the most immediate return. Each maps to an M365 app you already have, with a ready-to-use example prompt to get you started today.

✍️
Word
Marketing in Word — Blog Posts, Newsletters & Materials

Use Copilot in Word to draft blog posts, email newsletters, flyers, and marketing materials at speed. The key is using the / shortcut to reference your brand voice guide, past successful content, or product/service descriptions stored in OneDrive — Copilot will produce copy that's already aligned to your brand without you having to spell out every guideline from scratch.

Best practice: Keep a short "Brand Voice" document in your OneDrive (tone, target customer, key messages, words to avoid) and reference it with / in every marketing prompt. Use Agent Mode to produce a full content pack — blog post, social caption, and email teaser — from a single brief.

Example Prompt
"Draft a 600-word blog post about [TOPIC] for our [INDUSTRY] business targeting [IDEAL CUSTOMER]. Reference /[BRAND-VOICE-DOC] for tone and style. Include an engaging opening, 3 key sections, and a call-to-action at the end."
📊
Excel
Financials in Excel — Cash Flow, Invoices & Forecasting

Copilot in Excel turns raw financial data into actionable insight without requiring accounting expertise or advanced formula knowledge. Paste or import your invoice records, expense categories, or monthly revenue figures — then ask Copilot to analyse trends, identify risks, and build forecasts. It can create charts, pivot tables, and written summaries automatically.

Best practice: Keep your data in structured tables with clear column headers (Date, Category, Amount, Client, Status). The more consistent your data structure, the more accurate Copilot's analysis. Always ask Copilot to flag its assumptions — especially in forecasts.

Example Prompt
"Analyse this 12-month financial data. Identify: our three highest-revenue months and what drove them, months where expenses exceeded 75% of revenue, outstanding invoices over 30 days, and a 3-month revenue forecast based on current trends. Show results as a chart and a written summary."
🎨
PowerPoint
Pitches in PowerPoint — Client Decks & Product Showcases

Use Copilot in PowerPoint to generate complete client presentations, pitch decks, and product showcases from a brief or an existing Word proposal. Copilot pulls content from documents you reference with / and builds a structured, visually designed slide deck — complete with speaker notes. No more blank-slide anxiety before a big pitch.

Best practice: Provide Copilot with a clear brief including: audience, goal, key messages, and desired number of slides. After generation, use the Designer tool to refine layouts. Always review financial figures and key claims before presenting to clients — Copilot drafts fast but human review is essential before a pitch.

Example Prompt
"Create a 10-slide client pitch deck for [CLIENT/INDUSTRY]. Reference /[PROPOSAL-DOC] for content. Include: title slide, agenda, our understanding of your challenge (3 bullets), our proposed solution, case study or social proof, investment summary, timeline, FAQs, team slide, and next steps. Add speaker notes for each slide."
📧
Outlook
Customer Comms in Outlook — Templates, Follow-ups & Tone Coaching

Copilot in Outlook drafts professional, on-brand customer emails in seconds — and crucially, it coaches the tone of what you've already written before you send it. Use it to generate initial response templates, follow-up sequences, proposal cover emails, and even difficult customer service replies where the right wording really matters.

Best practice: Use the Coaching by Copilot feature (available in the compose window) to get feedback on your own draft before sending. It flags tone issues, unclear wording, and missed action items. Build a library of saved Copilot prompts for your most common customer email types.

Example Prompt
"Draft a follow-up email to [CLIENT NAME] who received our proposal 7 days ago but hasn't responded. The tone should be warm and helpful — not pushy. Mention we're available to answer questions, offer a 15-minute call, and include a gentle nudge toward the next step."
💬
Teams
Team Coordination in Teams — Recaps, Updates & Project Status

Copilot in Teams eliminates the note-taking bottleneck in meetings and keeps your team aligned without you having to write everything up manually. It transcribes meetings in real time, generates structured summaries, captures action items with owners, and can draft channel posts to keep your team or client updated after a meeting ends.

Best practice: Always start recording and enable Copilot at the beginning of a meeting — the transcript is required for Copilot to generate the summary. After important client or team meetings, ask Copilot to draft a channel update post so everyone is aligned even if they weren't in the meeting.

Example Prompt (after meeting)
"Summarise this meeting. Include: the top 3 decisions made, all action items with the person responsible and a due date, any open questions that need follow-up, and draft a short channel post (5 sentences max) to update the team on what was discussed and what happens next."
📄
Word
Business Docs in Word — Contracts, SOPs, Plans & T&Cs

Use Copilot in Word to draft business-critical documents that previously required expensive professional help or hours of template searching — contracts, standard operating procedures, business plans, terms and conditions, onboarding guides, and supplier agreements. Copilot produces a solid working draft you can then review and customise, dramatically compressing the time from "I need this document" to "this is ready to use."

Best practice: Always have a lawyer or qualified professional review any legally binding document Copilot helps you draft — including contracts and T&Cs. Use Copilot to produce the first draft quickly, then use professional review time on the final polish rather than creating from scratch. This saves significant cost.

Example Prompt
"Draft a standard services agreement for my [BUSINESS TYPE] business. Include sections on: scope of services, payment terms (net 30), intellectual property ownership, confidentiality, liability limits, and termination clause. Use plain English where possible. Flag any sections where I should seek legal review."

Advanced Techniques

Agent Mode, BizChat cross-app intelligence, and Copilot Studio for customer service

Once you're comfortable with the daily workflows, these three advanced capabilities unlock the next level of AI productivity for your business. They require a little more setup but deliver dramatically more powerful results.

Agent Mode in Word WORD · 2026

Agent Mode transforms Copilot in Word from a drafting assistant into a multi-step document producer. Instead of drafting a single section and waiting for you to prompt the next one, Agent Mode lets you set a complex goal — like "produce a full marketing plan including strategy, channels, content calendar, and budget breakdown" — and Copilot will plan and execute multiple document sections sequentially, referencing your brand guidelines and business context throughout.

How to activate: In Word, open the Copilot pane and look for the "Agent" toggle or the expanded prompt mode. When activated, you'll see Copilot planning its steps before executing. You can approve, modify, or skip each step. This is currently rolling out — if you don't see it, check that your M365 Copilot licence is on the latest update channel.

Best small business use cases: Full business plans, multi-section tender responses, comprehensive marketing strategies, employee handbooks, and detailed SOPs with sub-procedures. Any document where you previously had to prompt section-by-section is now a single Agent Mode task.

BizChat — Cross-App Intelligence COPILOT CHAT

BizChat (formally Microsoft 365 Chat, now Copilot Chat) is the most powerful and underused tool in the M365 for Business suite. Available at copilot.microsoft.com or in the Microsoft 365 app, BizChat lets you ask questions that draw on information across your entire M365 environment — emails, documents, Teams conversations, calendar, and SharePoint — in a single response.

Example cross-app queries for small business:

  • "What proposals have I sent to [Client Name] in the last 6 months and what was the outcome of each?"
  • "Summarise all emails from [Supplier] this month and flag any outstanding items I need to action."
  • "What decisions did my team make about [Project] across Teams and email in the last two weeks?"
  • "Find all documents related to [Contract/Project] in my OneDrive and give me a summary."

Setup requirement: Switch to the Work tab in Copilot Chat (not Web). Ensure your documents are stored in OneDrive or SharePoint — local files cannot be accessed. Ask your IT contact to confirm SharePoint search indexing is enabled for your business's drives.

Copilot Studio — Your Own Customer Service Bot ADVANCED

Copilot Studio (formerly Power Virtual Agents) lets you build a custom AI agent for your business — without writing a single line of code. The most powerful application for small businesses is a customer service bot that can answer common questions, qualify leads, take appointment bookings, and escalate to a human when needed.

What your custom agent can do: Answer FAQs from your website or knowledge base, collect customer enquiry details and route them to the right person, handle booking requests and connect to Bookings or Outlook Calendar, and send automated follow-up emails via Power Automate.

Getting started: Access Copilot Studio at copilotstudio.microsoft.com. Use the guided setup to connect your agent to a data source (your website, a SharePoint document library, or a manually created FAQ list). Deploy to your website via embedded chat widget or to your Teams channel for internal use. Typical setup time for a basic FAQ bot: 2–4 hours.

ℹ️
Copilot Studio Licensing

Copilot Studio is available as an add-on to Microsoft 365. Basic usage is included in some M365 Business plans — check the Power Platform admin centre to see what's available on your current licence before purchasing additional capacity.

Prompt Library

15 ready-to-use prompts across Marketing, Customer, Sales, Finance, and Operations

Copy any of these prompts directly into Copilot. Replace the items in [BRACKETS] with your specific details. Each prompt is written to produce immediately useful, business-ready output.

Marketing Prompt 1 — Blog Post
"Write a 700-word blog post titled '[TOPIC]' for [BUSINESS NAME], targeting [TARGET CUSTOMER]. Our tone is [PROFESSIONAL/FRIENDLY/EXPERT]. Include: a compelling opening hook, 3 key sections with actionable advice, and a closing call-to-action directing readers to [DESIRED ACTION — contact us / book a call / download X]. Reference /[BRAND-VOICE-DOC] if available."
Marketing Prompt 2 — Email Newsletter
"Draft a monthly email newsletter for [BUSINESS NAME]. This month's theme is [THEME/TOPIC]. Include: a subject line (A/B test two versions), a warm opening paragraph, the main story or update (150 words), one tip or resource for our readers, a spotlight on [PRODUCT/SERVICE/CLIENT WIN], and a clear call-to-action. Keep total length under 350 words."
Marketing Prompt 3 — Social Media Pack
"Create a 5-post social media content pack for [BUSINESS NAME] on the theme of [TOPIC/CAMPAIGN]. For each post provide: the platform (LinkedIn / Instagram / Facebook), the caption (appropriate length for each platform), 3 hashtag suggestions, and a note on the visual style that would work best. Tone: [DESCRIBE YOUR BRAND TONE]. Goal: drive traffic to [WEBSITE/BOOKING PAGE]."
Customer Prompt 1 — Complaint Response
"Draft a professional and empathetic response to a customer complaint about [ISSUE]. The customer's key concern is [SUMMARISE CONCERN]. Our position is [DESCRIBE WHAT WE CAN / CANNOT DO]. The tone should acknowledge their frustration, apologise where appropriate, explain what we're doing to resolve it, and offer [SPECIFIC RESOLUTION — refund / replacement / discount / callback]. Keep it under 150 words and end positively."
Customer Prompt 2 — Onboarding Email Sequence
"Write a 3-email onboarding sequence for new customers of [BUSINESS NAME] who have just purchased [PRODUCT/SERVICE]. Email 1 (Day 0): Welcome + getting started steps. Email 2 (Day 3): Check-in + one key tip for getting the most value. Email 3 (Day 7): Introduce a related product/service + invite to leave a review. Keep each email under 200 words. Friendly, helpful tone."
Customer Prompt 3 — FAQ Document
"Create a customer FAQ document for [BUSINESS NAME / PRODUCT / SERVICE]. Include 10 of the most common questions customers ask about [TOPIC AREA]. For each question: write the question as a customer would phrase it, provide a clear and friendly answer (3–5 sentences), and flag if any answer needs a link to our website or a specific policy. Format for easy copy-paste onto our website."
Sales Prompt 1 — Proposal Draft
"Draft a business proposal for [BUSINESS NAME] to send to [PROSPECT/CLIENT]. Our service is [DESCRIBE SERVICE]. The client's key challenge is [DESCRIBE PROBLEM]. Include: executive summary (3 sentences), understanding of the challenge, our proposed solution with 3–4 key deliverables, why us (2–3 differentiators), investment summary (reference /[PRICING-DOC] if available), timeline, and clear next steps. Professional tone, plain English."
Sales Prompt 2 — Follow-Up Sequence
"Write a 3-touch follow-up sequence for a prospect who attended a discovery call but hasn't responded to our proposal. Touch 1 (Day 3): Value-add follow-up — share one relevant insight or resource. Touch 2 (Day 8): Gentle check-in — ask if they have questions. Touch 3 (Day 14): Last-touch — create a light sense of urgency without being pushy. Each email under 100 words. Warm, professional tone."
Sales Prompt 3 — Discovery Call Prep
"I have a discovery call with [PROSPECT NAME] from [COMPANY / INDUSTRY] tomorrow. Based on what I know about [THEIR BUSINESS / INDUSTRY CHALLENGES], help me prepare: 5 open-ended discovery questions to understand their needs, 3 potential objections they might raise and how to respond to each, 2 relevant case studies or proof points I should mention, and a suggested meeting structure for a 30-minute call."
Finance Prompt 1 — Cash Flow Analysis
"Analyse the cash flow data in this spreadsheet. Identify: months where outflows exceeded inflows and the primary drivers, our average days-to-payment from clients, the top 3 expense categories as a percentage of revenue, and 3 specific recommendations to improve cash flow over the next quarter. Present findings as a brief written report (under 300 words) plus a summary table."
Finance Prompt 2 — Invoice Tracker & Overdue Alert
"Review this invoice data and identify: all invoices more than 30 days overdue, total value of outstanding receivables, clients with the longest payment delays, and draft a polite but firm payment reminder email for each overdue invoice. Sort overdue invoices by value (highest first). Flag any invoices approaching 60 or 90 days for escalation."
Finance Prompt 3 — Revenue Forecast
"Using this 12-month revenue and pipeline data, build a 3-month revenue forecast. Assumptions to apply: [GROWTH RATE % if known, or 'based on trend']. Show: baseline forecast (trend only), optimistic scenario (+20%), and conservative scenario (-15%). Identify the top 2 revenue risks for the next quarter and 2 opportunities worth pursuing. Present as a chart and a 200-word written summary."
Operations Prompt 1 — Standard Operating Procedure
"Write a standard operating procedure (SOP) for [PROCESS NAME] at [BUSINESS NAME]. The process involves [BRIEF DESCRIPTION]. Include: purpose statement, scope (who this applies to), step-by-step procedure (numbered, with sub-steps where needed), common issues and how to resolve them, and a version history table. Format for easy printing and team distribution. Plain language, no jargon."
Operations Prompt 2 — Staff Onboarding Document
"Create a first-week onboarding document for a new [JOB TITLE] joining [BUSINESS NAME]. Include: welcome message from the owner, day-by-day schedule for week 1, list of tools and systems access they'll need with notes on how to get set up, key contacts and their roles, the 3 most important things to know about how we work, and answers to the 5 most common questions new staff ask."
Operations Prompt 3 — Meeting Agenda & Recap
"Create a structured agenda for a [DURATION] [MEETING TYPE — team meeting / client review / strategy session] on [DATE]. Attendees: [NAMES/ROLES]. Goals for this meeting: [LIST 2–3 GOALS]. After the meeting, use this prompt to generate the recap: 'Summarise this meeting transcript. Include decisions made, action items (with owner and due date), any unresolved items for next meeting, and a 3-sentence summary I can paste into our project channel.'"

Measuring Your AI Impact

Four key metrics to track the return on your Copilot investment

To justify your Copilot investment — to yourself, your accountant, or your team — you need to measure what's changing. These four metrics give you a clear, practical way to quantify the impact Copilot is having on your business.

4–6
Hours Saved Per Week
Track time spent on writing, analysis, and admin tasks before and after. Most small business owners report 4–6 hours weekly within the first month.
3×
Faster Content Output
Measure how many blog posts, proposals, or emails you produce per week. Copilot users typically produce 3x more content in the same time budget.
↑ 22%
Proposal Conversion Rate
Better-structured, more personalised proposals convert at higher rates. Track your proposal win rate before and after adopting Copilot for sales docs.
-15%
Admin Cost Reduction
Tasks that previously needed outsourcing — copywriting, financial reporting, document creation — can now be done in-house. Track your outsourcing spend monthly.
How to track your impact — practical method
1

Baseline Week — Before Copilot

In your first week, spend 5 minutes at the end of each day noting which tasks you completed and roughly how long each took. Focus on writing, analysis, email, meetings, and document creation. This is your baseline. You only need to do this once.

2

Track Week — After 30 Days with Copilot

After 30 days of active Copilot use, repeat the same 5-minute daily tracking exercise for one week. Compare completion times for the same task types against your baseline. Most users are surprised by how stark the difference is — especially for writing-heavy tasks.

3

Calculate Your ROI

Multiply weekly hours saved by your effective hourly rate (your annual revenue divided by your working hours). If you save 5 hours/week at $100/hour, that's $500/week, $26,000/year — against a Copilot licence cost of roughly $360/year. That's a 70:1 ROI before you account for quality improvements.

4

Report Monthly in Excel

Use Copilot in Excel to build a simple monthly tracker: hours saved, content produced, proposals sent, customer response time, and outsourcing spend. Ask Copilot to generate a one-page summary at the end of each month. This report is invaluable if you ever need to justify the investment to a business partner, investor, or accountant.

Common Mistakes & How to Fix Them

Five avoidable errors small business owners make with Copilot — and what to do instead

Most frustration with Copilot comes from a small number of predictable mistakes. These five patterns cover 90% of what goes wrong — and each one has a simple fix.

✗
Not using / references for brand and context. Asking Copilot to write marketing copy without referencing your brand voice document results in generic, off-brand output that still requires heavy editing.
✓
Create a Brand Voice doc and reference it every time. Build a 1-page document in Word covering your tone, target customer, key messages, and words to avoid. Save it in OneDrive and start every marketing prompt with "/[Brand-Voice-Doc]" to give Copilot the context it needs to write on-brand copy from the first draft.
✗
Sending generic customer communications without customising the tone. Copilot's default tone is often more formal or corporate than a small business's natural voice — copy-pasting without review makes your emails sound like they came from a large corporation.
✓
Use the tone selector and Coaching by Copilot. After Copilot drafts an email, use the built-in tone slider (Formal/Neutral/Casual) to match your style. Then click Coaching by Copilot to get feedback on your revised version before sending. Small personalisation — a customer's name, a reference to something they mentioned — makes a significant difference to response rates.
✗
Pasting customer PII into Copilot prompts. Including customer names, addresses, financial account details, or other personally identifiable information in Copilot prompts raises data governance risks — particularly if you're subject to GDPR, CCPA, or similar privacy regulations.
✓
Use placeholders and descriptors instead of real PII. Replace actual customer names with [Customer Name] or descriptors like "a long-term client in the construction industry". For financial analysis, use anonymised data in Excel (replace client names with codes). M365 Copilot for Business has enterprise-grade data protection, but building the habit of minimal PII exposure is good practice regardless.
✗
Using Agent Mode without reviewing the plan first. Agent Mode can execute multiple steps automatically — but if you don't review the plan it proposes, Copilot may structure a document differently from what you intended, requiring significant rework.
✓
Always review and approve Agent Mode's plan before execution. When Agent Mode proposes a multi-step plan, take 60 seconds to read each step. Adjust the structure, add missing sections, or remove unnecessary ones before approving. The plan review step is where you direct the output — skipping it means trusting Copilot's default interpretation of your brief, which may not match your vision.
✗
Treating Copilot's financial analysis as final without verification. Copilot in Excel will produce confident-sounding financial summaries and forecasts — but it can misinterpret data, calculate percentages incorrectly, or make assumptions that don't match your business context without flagging them.
✓
Always verify key figures and ask Copilot to show its working. After any financial analysis, check 3–5 key numbers manually against your source data. Ask Copilot: "What assumptions did you make in this analysis? List them." This surfaces any misinterpretations before they influence a business decision. Use Copilot for speed and pattern spotting — use your own judgement (or your accountant's) for decisions that involve real money.
