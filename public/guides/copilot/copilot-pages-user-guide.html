What is Copilot Pages?

AI responses that live on — as editable, shareable, collaborative documents

Every time you use Copilot in chat, the responses disappear the moment you close the conversation. Copilot Pages solves this. With one click — "Edit in Pages" — any Copilot response becomes a persistent, editable document that you can refine, share with your team, and embed across Microsoft 365.

Pages are built on Microsoft Loop technology, which means they're more than static documents. They're live components that stay in sync wherever they're shared — paste a Page into a Teams message and it updates when anyone edits the original. Send it in Outlook and it stays current. Embed it in a Loop workspace and it becomes part of your team's shared canvas.

📌

Persist Great Responses

Stop losing valuable Copilot output to chat history. Click "Edit in Pages" and any response becomes a permanent document you can return to, build on, and share.

✏️

Keep Refining with AI

Copilot doesn't stop at the Page boundary. Continue asking Copilot to expand, rewrite, and improve content directly inside the Page — AI stays available throughout.

👥

Collaborate in Real Time

Share a Page link with colleagues and edit simultaneously. Everyone sees changes instantly — no emailing attachments, no version conflicts.

🔗

Embed Everywhere

Paste a Page into Teams, Outlook, or Loop as a live component. It stays in sync — edit the Page and every embed updates automatically.

Creating a Page from Copilot

One click turns any Copilot response into a shareable document

The core Pages workflow starts in Copilot chat. You ask Copilot something useful — draft a plan, summarise a document, generate a proposal — and when the response is good enough to keep, you click "Edit in Pages." The response opens as a full document that you and your team can edit and build on.

1

Generate a useful Copilot response in chat

Work in Copilot chat until you have something worth keeping. For Pages, the most valuable content is structured output: plans, summaries, proposals, agendas, briefs, or frameworks — anything with clear sections that benefits from collaborative refinement.

2

Click "Edit in Pages" below the response

Every Copilot response has an "Edit in Pages" button underneath it. Click it — the response opens as a new Page in your browser, with the content already populated and formatted.

3

Give the Page a clear, findable name

Pages are stored in your Microsoft 365 environment and appear in search. Name them so they're easy to find later: "Project Kickoff — Contoso Rebrand — June 2026" beats "Untitled Page 3."

4

Continue refining inside the Page

Once in the Page, Copilot is still available. Ask it to expand a section, add a missing element, or rewrite a paragraph — all while you (and colleagues) can also edit manually.

03

Editing & Refining in Pages

Copilot stays available — keep improving after the Page is created

Opening a Page is not the end of the AI process — it's the continuation. The Copilot panel stays available within the Page, so you can keep refining content section by section. Meanwhile, team members can edit manually and leave comments in parallel.

➕

Expand a Section

"Expand the 'scope review' section to include a detailed breakdown of what is and isn't in scope. Add a table with three columns: In Scope, Out of Scope, and To Be Confirmed."

✂️

Condense & Sharpen

"The open questions section is too long. Condense to the 5 most important questions and remove anything that can be resolved before the meeting."

🎯

Rewrite for Audience

"Rewrite the goals section for a client who isn't familiar with our process. Avoid internal jargon and focus on the outcomes they care about."

📋

Add Missing Elements

"Add a pre-read section at the top listing the three documents attendees should review before the session. Format it as a checklist."

Manual Editing + AI Editing

Pages support both approaches simultaneously. You can type directly into any block — adding context, correcting details, or inserting data that Copilot couldn't know — while also using the Copilot panel to restructure or rewrite other sections. The two modes are designed to work together, not replace each other.

Use Manual Editing When…Use Copilot Editing When…
Adding specific names, dates, or figures Copilot doesn't knowExpanding a brief section into detailed content
Correcting a fact or updating a detailRestructuring a long section into a table or bullets
Adding your own commentary or analysisRewriting for a different tone or audience
Inserting a Loop component or linkGenerating a missing section from scratch
Reviewing and approving colleague editsSummarising a long section into a TL;DR
ℹ️
Select Text to Target Copilot's Edits

In Pages, you can select a specific block of text before opening the Copilot panel. Copilot then targets its suggestions to that selection rather than the whole document. This is the most precise way to improve a specific paragraph without affecting the rest of the Page.

04

Real-Time Collaboration

Share a Page and your whole team edits together — live

Pages are designed for teams. Share a link and multiple people can edit simultaneously — like Google Docs, but within Microsoft 365. Everyone sees changes in real time. There are no attachments to email, no "which version is current?" confusion, and no merge conflicts.

1

Share via link

Click Share in the Page toolbar. You can share with specific people (who get notified), with your whole organisation, or generate a view-only link for external stakeholders. Set edit vs view-only permissions as needed.

2

Edit simultaneously

When multiple people have the Page open, you'll see each person's cursor and the section they're editing. Changes appear instantly — no need to save or refresh. The presence indicators show who's active.

3

Leave comments and @mentions

Highlight any text and leave a comment to flag something for a specific colleague. Use @mention to notify them directly — they receive a Teams or Outlook notification and can reply in-context without leaving the Page.

4

Review the version history

Pages keep a full version history. Click the version icon to see who changed what and when. You can restore any previous version if a change needs to be undone.

Use Pages Instead of Email Attachments

The Page is always the current version — and Copilot is still available to help you action the feedback.

05

Pages as Loop Components

Embed your Page anywhere in M365 — it stays live wherever it's shared

The most powerful feature of Pages is that they're built on Microsoft Loop — which means they can be embedded as live components in Teams, Outlook, and other Microsoft 365 apps. When someone edits the component in any of these places, it updates everywhere it's been shared. One source of truth, accessible from everywhere.

💬

Embed in Teams

Paste a Page link into a Teams chat or channel message. It appears as an interactive component that team members can read and edit directly in Teams — without opening a separate browser tab.

📧

Insert into Outlook

In Outlook, insert a Page as a Loop component into your email. Recipients see the live, editable content inside the email — and any edits they make update the source Page instantly.

🔄

Sync Across Locations

Edit the Page in the browser and it updates in the Teams embed. Edit it in the Outlook email and it updates in the Page. All instances stay in sync — automatically, in real time.

🏠

Live in Loop Workspaces

Add a Page to a Microsoft Loop workspace as a persistent workspace component. This is the most structured use — a dedicated shared space where the team's active Pages live together.

Traditional documents break when shared: you email a Word doc, someone edits their copy, and now you have two versions. Loop components eliminate this. There is always one version. Here's how it plays out in practice:

Loop component embedding in Teams and Outlook requires appropriate Microsoft 365 licences for all participants. External collaborators (outside your organisation) can view Pages via a shared link but may not be able to edit embedded Loop components depending on your tenant settings. Check with your IT team if you need external editing access.

Advanced Techniques

Cross-app flows, Pages libraries, and connecting Pages to Notebooks

Cross-App Workflows with Pages

💬→📄

Teams Meeting → Page

After a meeting, open the Copilot recap in Teams. Click "Edit in Pages" on the summary. Add action owners and due dates, then post the Page link back into the channel as the official follow-up.

📈→📄

Excel Analysis → Page

Copy Copilot's data narrative from Excel. Paste it into a Copilot chat prompt: "Format this as a structured analysis Page with an executive summary, key findings, and recommendations." Click "Edit in Pages."

📄→📊

Page → PowerPoint

Share a finished Page with Copilot in PowerPoint: "Create a presentation from the structure in this Page." The Page's headings become slides — skipping the blank-deck starting point entirely.

📄→📓

Page → Notebook Reference

Export key Pages as reference documents into a project Notebook. Approved decisions, finalised briefs, and completed deliverables belong in the Notebook for Copilot to reference going forward.

Building a Pages Library

As you create more Pages, they accumulate across Teams channels, Outlook threads, and Loop workspaces. Without a system, they become hard to find. A simple library structure prevents this:

1

Use a consistent naming convention

Format: "[Project] — [Page Type] — [Date]" e.g. "Contoso Rebrand — Kickoff Agenda — 2026-06-03". This makes Pages findable in Microsoft Search and scannable in Loop.

2

Organise Pages into Loop workspaces

Create one Loop workspace per project or team. Add all related Pages to that workspace. The workspace becomes the single location for all AI-assisted documents on that project.

3

Pin active Pages to Teams channels

For Pages that the team accesses frequently — a running status doc, an action tracker — pin them as tabs in the relevant Teams channel. One click from any channel message to the current document.

4

Archive completed Pages

When a project phase closes, move its Pages to an "Archive" section in the Loop workspace. They remain searchable but don't clutter the active workspace.

Common Mistakes & Fixes

What goes wrong — and how to fix it

I can't find Pages I created last week
Pages are searchable in Microsoft Search (the search bar in any M365 app). Search for the Page name or keywords from the content. If you didn't name Pages consistently, use Microsoft Search to filter by type: "Pages" or "Loop." Going forward, use the naming convention "[Project] — [Type] — [Date]" for every Page you create — this makes them reliably findable weeks or months later.
A colleague edited the Page and removed content I needed
Pages have full version history. Click the version history icon in the toolbar to see every edit, who made it, and when. You can restore any previous version or copy specific content from an earlier version. For Pages where content stability matters, restrict edit permissions to key team members and use "Commenter" access for reviewers who only need to provide feedback.
The Loop component in Teams isn't updating when I edit the Page
Loop components sync in real time, but there can be a brief delay on slow connections. Refresh the Teams client if changes don't appear within 30 seconds. If the problem persists, check that the component was inserted correctly — a link to a Page is not the same as a Loop component. Re-paste the Page link into Teams using the Loop component insert option, not just a plain URL.
The Page Copilot generated is too long and poorly structured
Before clicking "Edit in Pages," ask Copilot to reformat: "Rewrite this response with clear H2 headings for each section, no more than 4 bullet points per section, and a one-sentence summary at the top." A well-structured Copilot response produces a cleaner, more navigable Page than a wall of text or an overly long list.
External collaborators can't edit the Page I shared with them
External editing depends on your organisation's Microsoft 365 tenant settings — some organisations restrict Loop component editing to internal users only. If external editing isn't available, share the Page as a PDF snapshot for review, or use the "Commenter" link to allow comment-only access. Check with your IT administrator if you need to enable external Loop collaboration.

✓ Do This

  • Name every Page consistently from day one
  • Ask Copilot to structure output before clicking "Edit in Pages"
  • Use Pages instead of emailing Word doc attachments
  • Pin frequently-used Pages as tabs in Teams channels
  • Use version history before assuming content is lost

✗ Avoid This

  • Leaving Pages named "Untitled" — they become unfindable
  • Giving everyone edit access when Commenter is enough
  • Creating a new Page every week instead of updating the existing one
  • Sharing a plain link when a Loop component embed would keep it live
  • Forgetting to export important Pages to your project Notebook

Suggested Prompts

Ready-to-use prompts for creating and refining Pages

Meeting Follow-Up Page
Based on this meeting summary [paste summary or Teams recap], create a structured follow-up document with: (1) a 3-bullet executive summary of what was decided, (2) a table of action items with columns for Action, Owner, Due Date, and Status, (3) a list of open questions that still need resolution, and (4) the date of the next meeting. Format with clear H2 headings for each section — I'll click "Edit in Pages" to share this with the team.
Project Kickoff Document
Create a project kickoff document for [project name]. Include: project overview and goals, scope (in scope / out of scope / TBC), key milestones and target dates, team roles and responsibilities (RACI format), key risks and mitigations, and open decisions needed in the first two weeks. Use clear H2 headings and bullet points throughout — this will become a shared Page for the whole project team to edit.
Decision Record Page
Create a decision record document for the following decision: [describe the decision]. Structure it with: the decision summary in one sentence, the context and problem it addresses, the alternatives that were considered (with pros and cons for each), the rationale for the chosen option, who was involved in the decision, the date agreed, and any conditions or review dates attached to the decision. This will be saved as a permanent Page record.
Expand a Section in the Page
The [section name] section of this Page is too brief. Expand it to cover: [what's missing — e.g. specific risks with likelihood and impact ratings / a worked example / the step-by-step process]. Keep the same heading and formatting style as the rest of the Page. Aim for [number] bullet points or [number] paragraphs.
Prepare a Page for External Sharing
I'm about to share this Page with [external stakeholder / client / partner]. Review the content and: (1) flag any internal jargon, acronyms, or references that an external reader won't understand, (2) suggest where I should add context or explanation, (3) identify anything that looks sensitive or that we might not want to share externally, and (4) suggest a brief cover note I can add at the top for the external reader.
Summarise a Long Page into a TL;DR
Add a TL;DR section at the very top of this Page. It should be no more than 5 bullet points and cover: the key decision or conclusion, the most important actions, any critical deadlines, and one thing the reader absolutely must not miss. Write it for someone who will only read the TL;DR and nothing else. Keep it under 100 words.
