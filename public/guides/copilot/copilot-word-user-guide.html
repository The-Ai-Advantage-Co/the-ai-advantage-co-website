01
What is Copilot in Word?
Your AI writing partner — built into every document

Copilot in Word is an AI writing assistant embedded directly in Microsoft Word. It can draft entire documents from a brief description, rewrite selected passages for clarity or tone, summarise long files into key points, and transform content into new structures — all from a simple text instruction.

Unlike a standalone AI tool, Copilot in Word works inside your document. It sees what you're writing, can reference other files in your Microsoft 365 environment, and keeps your content in place — no copy-pasting between apps.

✍️
Draft from Scratch
Open a blank document and describe what you need. Copilot generates a full draft — with structure, headings, and paragraphs — ready for you to refine.
🔁
Rewrite & Improve
Select any text and ask Copilot to rewrite it — make it shorter, change the tone, simplify the language, or adjust for a different audience.
📖
Summarise & Understand
Open a long document and ask Copilot to summarise it, extract key points, or answer specific questions about the content — in seconds.
🔀
Transform & Restructure
Convert prose to bullet points, turn notes into a report, restructure sections for better flow, or reformat content for a different document type.
ℹ️ Where to Find Copilot in Word
Click the Copilot button (✨) in the Home ribbon to open the side panel, or press Alt + I anywhere in the document body to open the inline "Draft with Copilot" prompt. Both modes are available — the panel for broader tasks, the inline prompt for generating content at a specific location.
🤖 2026 Update — Agent Mode in Word is GA
Copilot's agentic capabilities went generally available across Word, Excel, and PowerPoint in April 2026. In Word that means Copilot can take multi-step actions inside a document on your behalf — pulling in a Reference doc, restructuring the entire piece against a brief, applying a tone shift across all sections, then summarising what it changed for you to review. Look for the Agent toggle at the top of the Copilot panel.
02
Draft with Copilot
From a blank page to a structured first draft in seconds

The "Draft with Copilot" experience is the fastest way to overcome a blank page. You describe what you need — the topic, audience, tone, and approximate length — and Copilot generates a complete structured draft. The draft is a starting point, not a finished product; your job is to shape it from there.

1
Open a new or existing Word document
Drafting works best in a new document. You can also use it to add a new section to an existing document by placing your cursor where you want the draft to appear.
2
Press Alt + I or click the inline Copilot icon
A prompt box appears in the document body. This is the "Draft with Copilot" input — type your instruction here, not in the side panel.
3
Describe what you want Copilot to write
Be specific: include the topic, the purpose, the audience, and the tone. Example: "Write an executive summary of our Q3 sales performance for senior leadership. Highlight top-line results, one key challenge, and our Q4 focus. Professional tone, 200 words."
4
Review the draft and choose Keep, Regenerate, or Refine
Copilot produces a draft with options: Keep it (accepts the draft into the document), Regenerate (try again with the same prompt), or type a refinement instruction like "Make it shorter" or "Add a section on risks".
5
Edit the draft as your own work
The accepted draft is now your document. Edit it like any Word content — change words, restructure paragraphs, add your own knowledge and context. Copilot gives you the structure; you give it the substance.

Anatomy of a Good Drafting Prompt

Example prompt — Draft with Copilot
"Write a 300-word project status update for our IT stakeholders about the CRM migration project. Include: current phase (data migration, 65% complete), a risk flagged this week (data mapping discrepancy in the legacy system), and next milestone (UAT begins 15 November). Tone: professional and factual."
Why this prompt works
It specifies who it's for (IT stakeholders), what to include (phase, risk, next milestone), specific facts to use (65%, discrepancy, 15 Nov), length (300 words), and tone (professional, factual). Copilot fills the structure; your facts prevent hallucination.
💡 Give Copilot Your Facts
Copilot is a structuring and writing engine, not a data source. Always supply the specific numbers, names, dates, and details in your prompt. Copilot will write around them accurately — but if you leave them out, it may invent plausible-sounding but incorrect content.
03
Rewrite & Refine
Improve existing text for clarity, tone, and audience

Once you have text in your document — whether drafted by Copilot or written yourself — Copilot can help you improve it. Select the text you want to work on, open the Copilot panel, and ask for a rewrite. This is one of the most practical everyday uses of Copilot in Word.

📏
Change Length
"Make this shorter — cut to the key point in 2 sentences." Or "Expand this paragraph with more detail about the technical implementation."
🎯
Adjust Tone
"Rewrite this in a more formal tone for an executive audience." Or "Make this friendlier and less jargon-heavy — it's for a non-technical team."
✂️
Simplify Language
"Rewrite this so a non-expert can understand it. Remove all technical acronyms and explain concepts in plain English."
🌍
Adapt for Audience
"Rewrite this for a client audience rather than an internal one. Remove internal references and focus on value and outcomes."

Before & After: Copilot Rewrite in Action

Before — rough draft
The implementation of the new system has encountered several issues related to legacy data compatibility which has caused delays to the go-live date and stakeholders have been informed that a revised timeline will be communicated at the next steering committee meeting which is scheduled for next week.
After — Copilot rewrite ("concise, professional")
The go-live date has been delayed due to legacy data compatibility issues. Stakeholders have been notified; a revised timeline will be confirmed at next week's steering committee meeting.

How to Rewrite Selected Text

1
Select the text you want to improve
Highlight any amount of text — a sentence, a paragraph, or a whole section. Copilot works on exactly what you select.
2
Click the Copilot icon that appears near the selection
A small Copilot sparkle (✨) appears near the selected text. Click it to open a context menu with quick rewrite options: "Make it shorter", "Make it longer", "Rewrite".
3
Choose a quick option or type a custom instruction
Quick options apply immediately. For a custom rewrite, choose "Rewrite" and type your specific instruction into the Copilot prompt box.
4
Accept, try again, or keep both versions
Copilot shows the rewrite alongside your original. Accept the rewrite to replace the original, or dismiss it to keep your own version. You can also ask for another attempt.
✨ Copilot Tip: Iterate Don't Accept First
Don't feel obligated to accept the first rewrite. Type a follow-up instruction like "Good, but make the opening sentence punchier" or "Now remove the passive voice throughout". Copilot treats each instruction as a refinement of the previous one.
04
Summarise & Understand
Get the key points from any document in seconds

Copilot can read and summarise any Word document — whether it's a lengthy report you need to brief others on, a contract you need to understand quickly, or a colleague's document you need to review. Open the Copilot side panel and ask directly about the document's content.

ℹ️ How to Open the Copilot Side Panel
Click the Copilot button (✨) in the Home ribbon (not the inline draft icon). The panel opens on the right side of the document. This panel mode is best for summarising and asking questions — it can read the whole document, not just the section near your cursor.

What to Ask in the Copilot Panel

Ask CopilotBest Use Case
"Summarise this document in 5 bullet points"Creating a briefing from a long report
"What are the key recommendations in this document?"Extracting actionable content from strategy or review docs
"What obligations does this document place on [party]?"Quick contract or policy review
"List all the dates and deadlines mentioned in this document"Project plans, proposals, or SLAs
"What risks or concerns are raised in this document?"Risk reviews, board papers, audit reports
"Write a 3-sentence executive summary of this document"Preparing a covering note or email intro
⚠️ Always Verify for Important Documents
Copilot's summaries are reliable for general content, but for legal, financial, or compliance documents — always verify the key points against the original before acting on them. Copilot can miss nuance, especially in densely structured contracts.

Summarise for Others

Use Copilot's summary output to communicate with colleagues who don't have time to read the full document. Ask Copilot to "write an email summarising this document for my team" or "produce talking points I can use in a 5-minute briefing on this report". The content stays in the side panel — copy and paste it into the channel or email of your choice.

Transform & Restructure
Convert, reformat, and reshape your content

Copilot in Word isn't limited to writing and rewriting — it can change the form of your content. Convert prose to bullet points for a slide deck, turn a set of notes into a structured report, or restructure a document's flow for a different audience. These transformations save hours of reformatting work.

📄→📋
Prose to Bullets
Select a dense paragraph and ask: "Convert this to a bullet list with one point per key idea." Ideal when the content needs to go into a presentation or briefing document.
📋→📄
Notes to Report
Paste in rough meeting notes or a brain dump and ask: "Turn these notes into a structured report with an introduction, key findings, and next steps."
🔀
Restructure for Flow
"Reorganise this document so the recommendations come first, followed by the supporting evidence." Copilot rewrites the structure while keeping your content.
📝→📊
Add a Table
"Convert this comparison into a table with the options as rows and the criteria as columns." Copilot turns prose comparisons into clean structured tables.

06
Reference & Extend
Bring in other M365 content to enrich your document

Copilot in Word can draw on other Microsoft 365 content when generating or extending your document. Using the /file reference in the Draft with Copilot prompt, you can ask Copilot to base its draft on a specific document, email, or meeting transcript stored in your M365 environment.

ℹ️ How to Reference Another File
In the "Draft with Copilot" prompt box, type / and then start typing the file name. A picker will appear showing recent and relevant files from your OneDrive and SharePoint. Select the file you want Copilot to reference when drafting.

Reference Use Cases

📎
Draft from a Brief
Reference a project brief or requirements doc: "Draft a proposal based on /[Project Brief]. Structure it as: Executive Summary, Approach, Timeline, Budget."
📧
Expand from Email Notes
Reference an email or Teams chat summary: "Using /[Meeting Summary], write a formal report of the decisions made and next steps." Saves manual transcription.
📋
Compare Documents
"Summarise the key differences between /[Draft A] and /[Draft B]." Copilot reviews both and returns a structured comparison in the side panel.
🔗
Combine Sources
"Write an overview section drawing on /[Report 1] and /[Report 2]. Synthesise the main themes into a single coherent narrative."
⚠️ File Reference Limitations
Copilot can only reference files you have access to and that are stored in OneDrive or SharePoint (not local-only files). Very large files may be partially read. Always verify that Copilot has correctly interpreted the referenced content before accepting a draft based on it.

Advanced Techniques
Multi-pass drafting, cross-app workflows, and style control

Multi-Pass Drafting

Don't try to get a perfect document in one prompt. The most effective approach is to build in passes: structure first, content second, polish third. Each pass uses a different type of Copilot instruction.

1
Pass 1 — Structure
"Create a document outline for a [document type] covering [topic]. Use headings only — no body content yet." Accept the structure, adjust the headings manually, then proceed.
2
Pass 2 — Content
Place your cursor under each heading and use Draft with Copilot to fill in each section individually, providing specific facts and context for each.
3
Pass 3 — Polish
Select the full document and ask Copilot: "Review this document for consistency of tone, remove any repetition, and tighten the language throughout."

Cross-App Workflows

💬→📄
Teams Recap → Word Report
Copy a Teams meeting recap into the Copilot prompt and ask: "Expand this into a formal meeting report with an introduction, decisions, and actions table."
📊→📄
Excel Analysis → Word Narrative
Copy key figures from Excel, paste into Copilot's prompt: "Write a narrative around these numbers for the CFO's board report. Explain trends and implications."
📧→📄
Email Thread → Policy Doc
Paste key decisions from an email chain: "Turn this email discussion into a formal policy statement with clear rules and rationale."
📄→📊
Word Report → Slide Brief
"Summarise this report into 6 slide titles with 3 bullet points each. Format it so I can paste directly into PowerPoint."

Controlling Copilot's Style

Common Mistakes & Fixes
What goes wrong — and how to fix it
❌ "Copilot's draft doesn't include the specific facts I need"
Copilot generates plausible content — it does not know your internal numbers, names, or project details unless you supply them. Always include specific facts in your prompt. Rule: anything that must be accurate must appear in your prompt. Copilot structures around your facts; it does not invent them reliably.
❌ "The draft is too generic — it could be about anything"
Your prompt lacked specificity. Add: the exact audience ("our IT steering committee"), the purpose ("to request budget approval"), the key message ("we need £120k for Phase 2"), and the tone ("formal, direct"). The more context you give, the more specific the output.
❌ "Copilot keeps using the wrong tone"
Paste an example of the tone you want: "Match the tone of this paragraph: [paste example]." Copilot calibrates to examples much better than abstract tone descriptors like "professional" or "engaging".
❌ "The /file reference isn't finding my document"
Copilot's file picker only searches OneDrive and SharePoint — not local files saved to your desktop or Documents folder. Move the file to OneDrive first. Also ensure the file has been saved recently so it appears in the recency-sorted picker.
❌ "I accepted a Copilot draft but now I can't undo specific parts"
Use standard Ctrl+Z to undo the entire Copilot draft insertion. If you've already edited the draft, use Track Changes (Review → Track Changes) on future Copilot-assisted documents so you can clearly see and accept/reject Copilot contributions.
✅ DO THIS
  • Supply your own facts, numbers, and names in prompts
  • Use multi-pass drafting — structure, then content, then polish
  • Iterate with follow-up instructions rather than starting over
  • Paste tone examples instead of using vague descriptors
  • Review all AI-generated content before sharing or publishing
❌ AVOID THIS
  • Accepting the first draft without reading it carefully
  • Expecting Copilot to know your internal context automatically
  • Using vague prompts like "write a report about sales"
  • Sharing Copilot output without fact-checking numerical claims
  • Treating Copilot as a one-shot tool rather than an iterative partner

Suggested Prompts
Ready-to-use prompts for drafting and refining
Executive Summary — From Facts
Write a 250-word executive summary for [audience] about [topic]. Include: [key result 1], [key result 2], [main risk or challenge], and the recommended next step. Professional tone. Start with the most important point.
Project Status Update
Write a project status update for [project name]. Current phase: [phase, % complete]. This week's highlight: [achievement]. Risk to flag: [risk]. Next milestone: [milestone and date]. Audience: [team/stakeholders]. Tone: factual and concise. Around 200 words.
Meeting Notes → Formal Report
Turn these meeting notes into a formal report. Structure: 1) Meeting Purpose, 2) Attendees, 3) Key Decisions, 4) Action Items (with owner and deadline), 5) Next Steps. Professional tone. Notes: [paste your notes here].
Sharpen & Shorten
Rewrite the selected text to be 40% shorter without losing the key message. Cut filler words, remove redundant phrases, and ensure every sentence carries weight. Keep the same professional tone.
Audience Shift — Internal to Client-Facing
Rewrite this for an external client audience. Remove all internal references, team names, and jargon. Replace with outcome-focused language. Tone: confident, professional, and benefit-oriented. The client cares about results, not our internal process.
Full Document Polish
Review this document and improve it for: (1) consistent formal tone throughout, (2) active voice — rewrite any passive constructions, (3) clarity — split any sentence over 30 words, (4) remove clichés like "leverage", "synergy", or "going forward". Return the improved version.
