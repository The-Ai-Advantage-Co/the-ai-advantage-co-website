Prompting for Images
What good prompts look like

Image prompts have 4 elements: subject, style, composition, and constraints.

  • Subject — what's in the image, specifically
  • Style — photorealistic / illustration / 3D render / watercolor / brand-specific
  • Composition — viewpoint, lighting, mood, framing
  • Constraints — what to avoid (text, logos, specific colors)

The four elements that consistently land

Image prompts have 4 elements. Skip any one and quality drops. The four-element discipline is what separates publishable images from 'AI-generated' ones.

  • Subject — what's in the image, specifically
  • Style — photorealistic / illustration / 3D render / watercolor / brand-specific
  • Composition — viewpoint, lighting, mood, framing
  • Constraints — what to avoid (text, logos, specific colors)
  • Example: 'Hero image for blog post. Subject: business team in collaborative discussion. Style: photorealistic editorial, warm natural lighting. Composition: rule of thirds, centered subject. Avoid: visible text, brand logos, gimmicky elements.'

Brand Consistency
Getting on-brand images consistently

Define a visual style brief once. Reuse it across every image prompt. For ongoing brand work, save it as a Custom GPT.

🎨 THE BRAND VISUAL BRIEF
Tone (warm/cool, soft/sharp), palette (primary + 2 accents), illustration style (specific), composition rules (centred / asymmetric / negative space), what to never include (specific elements). 5 sentences max — reuse on every image.
🎨 THE BRAND VISUAL BRIEF
Tone (warm/cool, soft/sharp), palette (primary + 2 accents with specific hex), illustration style (specific), composition rules (centred / asymmetric / negative space), what to never include (specific elements). 5 sentences. Reuse on every image — keeps a hundred different images visually consistent.

Brand-consistent imagery requires reusing a visual brief across every prompt. Define once: tone (warm/cool, soft/sharp), palette (primary + 2 accents), illustration style (specific), composition rules (centred / asymmetric), things to avoid. Five sentences. Apply to every image prompt.

For ongoing brand work, encode the brief in a Custom GPT — every image generated through the GPT inherits the brand. Saves the 30 seconds of pasting the brief each time and prevents drift over hundreds of images.

Iteration Pattern
From first draft to final
  • First generation — accept it's a draft
  • Refine — 'same image but with [specific change]'
  • Variations — 'show me 4 variations of [aspect]'
  • Specific edits — point at part of the image, request a change
  • Final — once you have the right composition, regenerate at higher quality

5
Final at quality
Once composition is right, regenerate at highest quality for final asset.

What It's Not Good At
Honest limits
  • Photo-real product photography — fingers, faces, and product details still glitch
  • Recognisable people — generates likenesses but with policy restrictions on real public figures
  • Highly technical diagrams (engineering schematics, anatomical accuracy)
  • Brand logos — never let AI generate a brand logo, it won't be defensible
  • Anything you'll print at scale without manual cleanup

✅ DO THIS

  • Use for editorial imagery, illustrations, concept art
  • Use for slide deck visuals
  • Use for blog and social headers
  • Use for mockup illustrations
  • Use for infographics with text
  • Combine with brand brief for consistency

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Photo-real product photography — fingers, faces, product details glitch
  • Recognisable real people — restricted by policy
  • Brand logos — never let AI generate; not defensible
  • Highly technical diagrams (engineering schematics, anatomical accuracy)
  • Anything you'll print at scale without manual cleanup
  • Customer-facing assets without human review pass

Rights and Disclosure
Who owns the output
  • OpenAI's terms — you generally own commercial use rights on generated images
  • Training data risk — small risk that elements resemble copyrighted training images; review for high-stakes uses
  • Disclosure — be honest about AI-generated visuals where audiences expect human work (illustrations, fine art)
  • Public figures — restricted; don't generate likenesses of named real people for commercial use
⚠️ RESTRICTIONS WORTH KNOWING
Don't generate named real people for commercial use — restricted by policy and often by jurisdiction. Don't generate brand logos — won't be defensible. Watermark or disclose AI-generated images where audiences expect human work (fine art, illustrations, regulated industries).
⚖️ RIGHTS AND DISCLOSURE
OpenAI's terms generally grant commercial rights on paid tiers. Small risk that elements resemble copyrighted training images — review for high-stakes uses. Be honest about AI-generated visuals where audiences expect human work (fine art, illustrations). Public figures and named real people are restricted by design.

Common Mistakes & Fixes
What goes wrong — and how to avoid it

Most AI workflows that 'don't work' fall into one of these patterns. The fix is almost always sharper context and stricter review, not switching models or tools.

  • Treating ChatGPT Image Generation as a finished answer rather than a draft to review
  • Vague prompts that don't specify audience, format, or what you want changed
  • Forgetting to give it context — the file, the brief, the constraints
  • Skipping the verify step — always check numbers, dates, names, and policy claims
  • Using it for tasks where a 30-second human action would be faster

✅ DO THIS

  • Provide context — the file, the brief, the audience, the constraints
  • Iterate; don't just accept the first output
  • Always review numbers, names, dates, and policy claims
  • Treat AI as a fast draft generator, not an answer engine
  • Capture what works so you can replicate it
  • Match the model to the task — fast modes for drafts, reasoning modes for analysis

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Sending AI output without reading it end-to-end
  • Vague prompts with no audience, format, or success criteria
  • Assuming AI can see files or context it hasn't been given
  • Using AI for tasks faster done manually in under a minute
  • Treating numbers, quotes, or citations as verified without checking
  • Defaulting to the slow reasoning mode for every task

Suggested Prompts
Ready-to-use prompts you can adapt to your work

These prompts are designed to land in your real work today. Replace the bracketed placeholders with your actual context — the more specific you make them, the better the output. Treat each one as a starting brief, not a finished script.

BRAND IMAGE BRIEF
Generate an image. Subject: [subject]. Style: photorealistic editorial, soft natural lighting. Palette: warm cream and deep teal. Composition: centred, negative space top-left, mood is calm and focused. Avoid: text, logos, gimmicky elements.
ITERATION
Same image but: [specific change]. Keep everything else identical.
VARIATIONS BRIEF
Show me 4 variations of [the current image]. Vary: composition only (one centred, one rule-of-thirds, one asymmetric, one wide). Keep subject and style identical.
