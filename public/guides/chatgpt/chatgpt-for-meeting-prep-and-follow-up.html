02
The Three-Part Meeting Loop
Pre, during, after
  • Pre — brief: attendees, agenda, history, suggested questions
  • During — capture: real-time notes (you, not ChatGPT, unless using a transcription tool)
  • After — actions: extract decisions and tasks, send recap, schedule follow-ups

The pre/during/after pattern is what makes ChatGPT meaningful for meetings — not a single trick but a recurring habit. Pre-meeting: brief from history + agenda + suggested questions. During: your own notes (not AI transcription unless legally appropriate). After: extract decisions, actions, open questions; draft the recap.

Of these, the pre-meeting brief is the highest-leverage. 5 minutes of preparation produces 50% better meeting outcomes — you arrive knowing who's in the room, what they care about, what was last agreed, and what to ask. ChatGPT compresses this prep from 30 minutes to 5.

Why the loop pattern produces compound returns

Individual meetings vary; the meeting loop is repeatable. The compound return comes from running the loop for every meaningful meeting — not from any single meeting's gain.

  • Pre — 5 min prep beats 30 min unprepared. Cumulative: 8+ hours weekly for back-to-back roles.
  • During — manual notes preserve discretion; AI transcription has consent requirements
  • After — recap and actions in 3 min vs 30. Faster follow-through; fewer dropped threads.
  • Recurring — context compounds. Each meeting builds on previous; less re-orienting.
  • Memory + Projects — carry context across cycles without manual re-briefing

Pre-Meeting Brief
The 5-minute prep that saves the call

Brief ChatGPT on the meeting context. Get back: who's attending, what they care about, recent history, agenda, and 3 questions you should ask but might forget.

  • Attendees — name, role, what they care about, recent interactions
  • Agenda — explicit topics + likely implicit ones
  • History — what was agreed last time, what's outstanding
  • Open questions — what's unresolved between meetings
  • Prep questions — 3 you should ask

Political dynamics, your goals, what's NOT on the table — anything ChatGPT can't infer.

In-Meeting Capture
Your notes, not ChatGPT's

During the meeting, take rough notes yourself — bullets, not sentences. ChatGPT processes them after. Don't rely on AI transcription in regulated or confidential settings without explicit consent.

🎙️ RECORDING HAS RULES
AI transcription of meetings is regulated in many jurisdictions — most require all-party consent. Even where legal, the trust cost of recording without explicit agreement is high. Default to manual notes unless recording is openly disclosed.
🎙️ AI TRANSCRIPTION HAS LEGAL RULES

Post-Meeting Recap
From rough notes to shareable summary

Paste your rough notes (or the transcript if recorded with consent). Ask for: decisions, action items with owners, open questions, and a 1-paragraph summary for stakeholders who weren't there.

  • Decisions made — what's now settled
  • Action items — who's doing what by when
  • Open questions — what needs follow-up
  • Risks flagged — what was raised but not resolved
  • Stakeholder summary — 100 words for the people who weren't there

The recap extraction prompt that works

Paste rough notes, get structured artefacts. The prompt below produces consistently usable output across meeting types.

  • Paste meeting notes (rough bullets are fine; abbreviations OK)
  • 'Extract: decisions made, action items (who/what/when), open questions, risks raised'
  • 'Write a 100-word stakeholder summary for people who weren't there'
  • 'Draft a follow-up email if external; skip if purely internal'
  • Review carefully before sending — ChatGPT can confabulate when notes are sparse
06
Follow-Up Email
The 'as discussed' message

After internal meetings, a recap email is often unnecessary. After external meetings (customers, prospects, partners, board), it's essential. ChatGPT drafts it from your notes in seconds.

  • Open with thanks and 1-line meeting summary
  • List the decisions/agreements
  • List the actions with names and dates
  • Confirm the next meeting (date, agenda)
  • Short, businesslike — not chatty

Recurring-Meeting Patterns
Where Memory shines

For meetings that recur (weekly 1:1s, monthly customer reviews, quarterly business reviews), set up a Memory scope or a Project. ChatGPT then carries the history forward without you re-explaining each time.

  • Weekly 1:1 — Memory scope per direct report
  • Customer monthly review — Project per customer
  • Quarterly business review — Project per account
  • Board meetings — Project for the board, history of prior recaps

✅ DO THIS

  • Set up a Memory scope or Project for any recurring meeting
  • End each recurring meeting by saving key decisions/commitments to memory
  • Start each new recurring meeting with 'brief me from memory'
  • Quarterly memory review — prune stale facts, update positions
  • Tag artefacts (decision logs, briefing notes) with date and context
  • Reference prior meeting outputs explicitly to maintain thread

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Treating each instance of a recurring meeting as fully independent
  • Storing sensitive personnel matters in shared memory
  • Letting memory accumulate without periodic curation
  • Over-relying on AI prep at expense of your own reading
  • Skipping the memory update at end of meeting — context drifts
  • Mixing personal and professional memory in one scope

Calendar Triage
Deciding which meetings deserve prep

Not every meeting deserves the full prep loop. ChatGPT can help triage:

  • Stakes — high-stakes meetings (customer-facing, exec, board) always get prep
  • New context — meetings with new people or new topics get prep
  • Recurring with stable cadence — light prep (last meeting's actions + 1 question)
  • Internal team standups — usually no prep needed
  • Anything you'd be embarrassed to be unprepared for — prep

Deciding which meetings deserve prep

Not every meeting deserves the full loop. Triage based on stakes, novelty, and the cost of being unprepared.

  • Stakes high — customer-facing, executive, board: always prep
  • New context — new people or new topics: prep
  • Recurring with stable cadence — light prep: last meeting's actions + 1 question
  • Internal team standups — usually no prep needed
  • Anything you'd be embarrassed to be unprepared for — prep regardless of stakes
  • Decline or restructure meetings that don't merit prep AND don't need you live

Common Mistakes & Fixes
What goes wrong — and how to avoid it

Most AI workflows that 'don't work' fall into one of these patterns. The fix is almost always sharper context and stricter review, not switching models or tools.

  • Treating ChatGPT for Meeting Prep and Follow-Up as a finished answer rather than a draft to review
  • Vague prompts that don't specify audience, format, or what you want changed
  • Forgetting to give it context — the file, the brief, the constraints
  • Skipping the verify step — always check numbers, dates, names, and policy claims
  • Using it for tasks where a 30-second human action would be faster

✅ DO THIS

  • Provide context — the file, the brief, the audience, the constraints
  • Iterate; don't just accept the first output
  • Always review numbers, names, dates, and policy claims
  • Treat AI as a fast draft generator, not an answer engine
  • Capture what works so you can replicate it
  • Match the model to the task — fast modes for drafts, reasoning modes for analysis

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Sending AI output without reading it end-to-end
  • Vague prompts with no audience, format, or success criteria
  • Assuming AI can see files or context it hasn't been given
  • Using AI for tasks faster done manually in under a minute
  • Treating numbers, quotes, or citations as verified without checking
  • Defaulting to the slow reasoning mode for every task

Suggested Prompts
Ready-to-use prompts you can adapt to your work

These prompts are designed to land in your real work today. Replace the bracketed placeholders with your actual context — the more specific you make them, the better the output. Treat each one as a starting brief, not a finished script.

PRE-MEETING BRIEF
Meeting in 30 min with [name(s)], [role(s)]. Topic: [topic]. History I can give you: [paste recent emails/notes]. Brief me: who's coming and what they care about, the explicit and implicit agenda, what was last agreed, what's outstanding, and 3 questions I should be ready to ask.
POST-MEETING EXTRACTION
Rough notes from a meeting: [paste]. Extract: decisions made, action items (who/what/when), open questions, risks flagged. Then write a 100-word stakeholder summary for people who weren't there.
RECURRING 1:1 SETUP
Set up a Memory scope for my weekly 1:1 with [person]. Track: what we discussed, what they raised, decisions and commitments, and how their priorities are evolving. Before each weekly, brief me from the running context.
