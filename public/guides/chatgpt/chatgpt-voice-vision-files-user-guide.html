What is Multimodal ChatGPT?

Beyond text — inputs and outputs across voice, images, and files

Most people use ChatGPT by typing. But ChatGPT can understand and work with far more than text: it can hear you speak and respond in a natural voice, analyse images you share with it, read and extract insights from documents, run calculations on spreadsheet data, and generate charts from raw numbers — all in a single conversation.

These capabilities are called multimodal — the ability to work across multiple types of input and output. Together they transform ChatGPT from a text assistant into a versatile thinking and analysis partner.

🎙️

Voice Mode

Real-time spoken conversation with ChatGPT. Talk naturally, get spoken responses. Advanced Voice Mode adds emotional nuance, accents, and real-time interruption.

👁️

Vision

Upload images, screenshots, photos, or diagrams and ask ChatGPT to analyse, describe, extract information, or answer questions about what it sees.

📄

Files & Documents

Upload PDFs, Word documents, spreadsheets, code files, and more. ChatGPT reads the content and can summarise, answer questions, extract data, or compare documents.

📊

Data Analysis

Upload a CSV or spreadsheet and ChatGPT can run calculations, spot trends, identify outliers, and generate charts — all through natural language requests.

💡
Plan Availability

Voice mode, vision, and file upload are available on ChatGPT Plus, Team, Enterprise, and Pro. Free users get limited access — typically image upload but reduced file and voice capabilities. Advanced Voice Mode is Plus and above.

02

Voice Mode

Hands-free, natural conversation with ChatGPT

ChatGPT has two voice modes: Standard Voice (available on Free and Plus) converts your speech to text, processes it, and reads the response back. Advanced Voice Mode (Plus and above) is fundamentally different — it processes your voice directly, enabling real-time conversation with natural pacing, emotional nuance, laughter, and the ability to interrupt mid-sentence.

Advanced Voice Mode in Action

Advanced Voice Mode Listening
Can you walk me through the key points from my meeting notes and suggest three follow-up actions?
You
✦
Sure! Based on your notes, the three main themes are budget approval, the Q3 timeline, and the new hire decision. For follow-ups — first, I'd send the budget summary to finance today. Second, schedule a check-in on the timeline before end of week. And third, loop in HR about the hiring decision by Friday. Want me to draft any of those?
Yes — the budget email first.
You

Start Voice Mode

Tap the headphone icon in the ChatGPT app (mobile) or the microphone button on desktop. The interface switches to a waveform display — you're live.

Interrupt Naturally

In Advanced Voice Mode, just speak while ChatGPT is talking to interrupt. It will stop and respond to your new input — just like a real conversation.

Choose a Voice

Settings → Personalisation → Voice. Pick from multiple voice options (Cove, Ember, Juniper, etc.) — each with different tone and energy.

Use While Mobile

Voice mode is ideal for hands-free use: while driving, walking, or cooking. The app stays active with screen off on iOS and Android.

Voice mode excels for: morning briefings, hands-free brainstorming, language practice, verbal dictation, and talking through a problem when you don't want to type. It's not ideal for tasks requiring precise formatting or code output — those are better handled in text mode.

Vision & Image Analysis

Upload images — ChatGPT sees, reads, and interprets what's there

ChatGPT can analyse any image you upload — photos, screenshots, diagrams, charts, handwritten notes, slides, or whiteboards. It can describe what it sees, extract text, answer questions about the image content, compare multiple images, or use visual context to help solve a problem.

What ChatGPT Can See and Do

📝

Read Text in Images

Extract text from screenshots, photos of documents, receipts, whiteboards, business cards, or handwritten notes — even with imperfect image quality.

📊

Interpret Charts & Graphs

Describe what a chart shows, extract the underlying data, identify trends, spot anomalies, or explain a visualisation in plain language.

🖼️

Describe & Analyse Photos

Identify objects, people, locations, or situations. Useful for product photos, event images, real estate photos, and visual troubleshooting.

🗂️

Review UI & Designs

Upload a screenshot or mockup and get feedback on layout, accessibility, design patterns, or UX flow — without needing to describe it in text first.

How to Upload an Image

1

Click the paperclip or image icon

In the ChatGPT input bar, click the attachment icon and select your image file, or drag-and-drop it directly into the chat.

2

Type your question about the image

The image is context — your question directs what ChatGPT does with it. Be specific: "What does the handwriting say?" beats "Analyse this."

3

Combine image and text context

You can paste in additional text alongside the image — e.g. "This is a mockup of our checkout page. What UX issues do you see?" gives the model context it can use.

4

Upload multiple images

You can upload several images at once to compare them — e.g. two design versions, before-and-after photos, or a series of screenshots showing a workflow.

💡
Be Specific About What You Want

Vision analysis improves significantly with a focused question. "Extract all the line items and amounts from this receipt" produces far more useful output than "What's in this image?" Tell ChatGPT exactly what to look for or do with what it sees.

⚠️
Limitations to Know

ChatGPT cannot identify real people by face, read highly degraded images reliably, or process video. For very small text or complex diagrams, image quality matters — a clear, well-lit photo or high-res screenshot will always give better results than a blurry or compressed one.

Data Analysis

From raw spreadsheet data to charts and insights — through conversation

When you upload a CSV or spreadsheet, ChatGPT's Data Analysis capability (formerly called Advanced Data Analysis or Code Interpreter) activates. It can write and execute Python code behind the scenes to process your data, run calculations, identify patterns, and generate charts — all through natural language. You don't need to know Python or statistics.

📈

Generate Charts

"Create a bar chart of sales by region" — ChatGPT writes the code, runs it, and returns the chart as a downloadable image.

🔍

Spot Trends & Outliers

"Which rows have unusually high values in the cost column?" — statistical analysis with plain-language explanations of what it found.

🧮

Run Calculations

Aggregations, percentages, year-over-year changes, averages, correlations — any calculation a spreadsheet can do, expressed in natural language.

🧹

Clean & Transform

"Remove duplicate rows", "standardise the date format", "split the full name column into first and last" — data wrangling without formulas.

A Typical Data Analysis Workflow

1

Upload your file

Attach a .csv or .xlsx file to the chat. ChatGPT will confirm it has loaded and tell you what it sees — column names, row count, data types.

2

Ask for a summary first

"Give me an overview of this data — key columns, any missing values, and what kind of analysis might be useful." This orients both you and the model.

3

Ask specific analysis questions

Be precise: "What were the top 5 products by revenue in Q3?" rather than "Analyse the data." Specific questions get specific, useful answers.

4

Request charts and download

"Now generate a line chart of monthly revenue." ChatGPT creates the chart and provides a download link. You can specify chart type, colours, and labels.

💡
Ask for the Python Code

After any analysis, you can ask: "Show me the Python code you used for that." This lets you learn from the analysis, verify it, or run it yourself. Even non-developers find this useful for understanding what was done to their data.

ℹ️
Data Privacy Note

Files you upload to ChatGPT are processed by OpenAI's servers. For sensitive business data, check your organisation's policy on AI tool use. Enterprise and Team plans have stronger data privacy controls than Plus or Free.

Combining Modalities

Mixing voice, vision, and files for compound workflows

The real power of multimodal ChatGPT emerges when you combine capabilities. A single conversation can use files for context, images for visual analysis, and voice for natural iteration — creating workflows that would previously require multiple tools.

Compound Workflow Examples

1

Photo → Text → Action

Photograph a whiteboard from a meeting, upload it, ask ChatGPT to extract the notes into structured action items, then switch to voice mode to talk through the priorities hands-free on your commute.

2

Document → Analysis → Voice

Upload a report or research paper, get a written summary and key findings, then switch to voice mode to ask follow-up questions conversationally — like a briefing you can talk through.

3

Data → Chart → Explanation

Upload a spreadsheet, generate a chart, then ask for a plain-language explanation of the trend suitable for a client slide — all in the same conversation without switching tools.

4

Screenshot → Feedback → Draft

Upload a design mockup or page screenshot, get UX and copy feedback, then ask ChatGPT to rewrite the copy based on its feedback — image analysis leading directly into content creation.

Voice + Image

On mobile, open a live camera feed in voice mode and describe what you're looking at in real time. Useful for visual troubleshooting, cooking guidance, or describing an environment hands-free.

File + Voice

Upload a document, then switch to voice mode to ask questions about it out loud. ChatGPT maintains file context across the mode switch — the document stays in the conversation.

Multiple Files

Upload two or more documents to compare them: "Compare the scope of work in these two proposals" or "What clauses appear in contract A but not B?"

Image + Data

Upload a chart image and a spreadsheet together: "The chart shows X — does the underlying data in this spreadsheet support that trend?"

💡
Context Persists Through Mode Switches

Files and images uploaded earlier in a conversation remain accessible even after switching to voice mode or after several follow-up exchanges. You don't need to re-upload — ChatGPT holds the full conversation context including all uploaded materials.

Advanced Techniques

Power patterns for getting the most from multimodal capabilities

These techniques move beyond basic upload-and-ask to more sophisticated uses of ChatGPT's multimodal capabilities — combining them with Projects, custom instructions, and iterative workflows.

Persistent File Context with Projects

The biggest limitation of file uploads is that they disappear when the conversation ends. Projects solve this: any file added to a Project is available in every conversation within that Project, indefinitely. This enables:

Reference Documents

Brand guidelines, style guides, company policies, product documentation — upload once, available always. No more re-uploading the same file.

Ongoing Research

Papers, reports, and sources for a research project. Ask questions across all of them in any conversation without re-uploading.

Client Context

Client briefs, contracts, past deliverables — uploaded to a client Project, always available for reference when drafting or analysing new work.

Living Documents

Replace a file in a Project with an updated version and the new content immediately becomes the reference for all future conversations.

Use Vision for Inaccessible Formats

Some file types don't upload cleanly — old PDFs with embedded images, scanned documents, or proprietary formats. Workaround: take a screenshot of each page and upload the images instead. ChatGPT's vision reads image-based text well, even from scanned documents.

Common Mistakes & Fixes

What goes wrong with multimodal ChatGPT and how to address it

❌ Uploading a file without asking a specific question
Fix: ChatGPT needs direction — "Analyse this" is too vague. Pair every file upload with a specific question: what you want extracted, summarised, compared, or done. The more specific your request, the more useful the output.
❌ Assuming file analysis covers the whole document when it's long
Fix: Very long documents (100+ pages) may not be fully processed. Ask ChatGPT to confirm which sections it has read. For critical documents, work section by section and ask it to cite page numbers when quoting — this makes gaps visible.
❌ Using voice mode for tasks that need precise text output
Fix: Voice mode is optimised for conversational flow, not formatted output. For tasks requiring structured output — tables, code, formatted documents — switch back to text mode. You can switch mid-conversation without losing context.
❌ Uploading sensitive data without considering privacy implications
Fix: Files you upload are processed by OpenAI. For personal health records, sensitive financial data, or confidential business information, check your plan's data privacy policy. Consider anonymising data before upload — replace names with "Client A", specific numbers with approximations — while still getting useful analysis.
❌ Re-uploading the same reference files in every new conversation
Fix: Use Projects. Any file in a Project persists across all conversations in that project. If you find yourself uploading the same brand guide, policy document, or research paper repeatedly, it belongs in a Project.
❌ Treating data analysis output as final without checking
Fix: Always ask ChatGPT to show its methodology or the Python code behind any calculation. Data analysis can produce plausible-looking errors — especially with complex formulas or ambiguous column names. Verify key figures against the source data for anything you'll act on or share.

Suggested Prompts

Ready-to-use prompts for multimodal workflows

Document Deep-Dive
I've uploaded [document type]. Please: (1) give me a 150-word executive summary of the key points, (2) extract any specific obligations, commitments, or action items into a bullet list, (3) flag anything that seems unusual, risky, or that I should pay close attention to, and (4) list the three questions I should ask after reading this.
Image & Screenshot Analysis
I've uploaded [image type — e.g. screenshot, photo, diagram]. Please: (1) describe what you see in detail, (2) extract any text visible in the image and format it clearly, (3) answer this specific question about it: [your question], and (4) note anything that appears incomplete, incorrect, or worth flagging.
Cross-Document Comparison
I've uploaded two documents — [Doc A description] and [Doc B description]. Compare them across these dimensions: (1) [dimension 1], (2) [dimension 2], (3) [dimension 3]. Present the comparison in a clear table. Then give your overall assessment: which is stronger, and why?
Spreadsheet Analysis
I've uploaded a spreadsheet. First, give me an overview: column names, row count, data types, and any obvious data quality issues. Then: (1) identify the top 5 [metric] by [dimension], (2) show month-over-month trend for [key metric], (3) flag any outliers or anomalies in the data, and (4) generate a chart showing [specific visualisation]. Show me the Python code you used.
Voice Brainstorm Starter
I want to think through [topic or problem] out loud. Play the role of a thoughtful sparring partner — ask me clarifying questions one at a time, push back on anything that seems underdeveloped, and help me reach a clear conclusion. Don't give me a list of ideas upfront — let's work through this conversationally.
Whiteboard to Action Items
I've uploaded a photo of a whiteboard from a recent meeting. Please: (1) extract and transcribe all the text you can read, (2) organise the content into logical themes or sections, (3) convert any tasks or decisions into a structured action item list with Owner / Action / Deadline columns, and (4) flag anything that's unclear or that you couldn't read.
