Why AI Matters for Operations & Admin
The efficiency shift that's redefining how ops teams work in 2026

Operations and administration are the backbone of every organisation, but they've always been among the most time-intensive functions. The work is essential but repetitive: writing SOPs, preparing meeting agendas, chasing vendor quotes, compiling weekly reports, coordinating schedules, and maintaining the documentation that keeps everything running. In 2026, AI has changed the economics of every one of these tasks.

The operations professionals seeing the biggest gains aren't replacing their judgment with AI. They're using ChatGPT to compress the cognitive labour that doesn't require their institutional knowledge — the first draft of a process document, the structure of a meeting agenda, the comparison framework for a vendor evaluation. That frees them to focus on what only they can do: managing stakeholders, resolving exceptions, and driving continuous improvement across the business.

The shift is measurable. Admin professionals using ChatGPT daily report reclaiming 25-35 hours per month. That's not a marginal productivity gain — it's the equivalent of adding another team member. The key is knowing which tasks to delegate to AI, how to prompt it effectively, and where human oversight remains non-negotiable.

02
Setting Up ChatGPT for Operations Work
Five steps to a professional, operations-ready ChatGPT environment

Getting ChatGPT configured correctly from the start means every conversation begins with the right operational context. This five-step setup takes under 30 minutes and transforms ChatGPT from a generic chatbot into a dedicated operations assistant that understands your organisation's structure, processes, and communication standards.

1
Choose the Right Plan — Plus vs Team for Shared GPTs
ChatGPT Plus ($20/mo) gives you full access to GPT-5.5, file uploads, Projects, and Custom GPTs — everything in this playbook. If your ops team has 3+ people who need shared access to the same Custom GPTs and consistent configurations, the Team plan ($30/user/mo) adds shared GPT libraries and enhanced data privacy (your data is never used for training). For a solo admin professional, Plus is the right starting point.
2
Create a Custom GPT — Your "Ops Assistant"
Go to chatgpt.com → Explore GPTs → Create. Name it "Ops Assistant" and upload your company's key SOPs, org chart, process templates, and style guides as knowledge files. In the instructions, tell it: your organisation name, your role, the formatting standards you expect (numbered steps, tables, clear headings), and that it should always ask clarifying questions before producing a document. This single GPT becomes your default starting point for all operations work.
3
Configure Custom Instructions for Consistent Formatting
Custom Instructions apply to every conversation. Set them to: always use your preferred document formatting (numbered lists for procedures, bullet points for summaries, tables for comparisons), default to professional tone, include version dates on documents, and use your organisation's terminology. This eliminates having to repeat formatting preferences in every prompt.
4
Set Up Projects for Recurring Operations
Create separate Projects for each operational domain: Weekly Reports (upload past report templates and KPI definitions), Vendor Management (upload vendor contracts, comparison criteria, and communication templates), Meeting Management (upload agenda templates and standing action items), and HR & Hiring (upload job descriptions, onboarding checklists, and policy docs). Each Project retains context across conversations.
5
Enable Memory for Ongoing Team Context
Turn on Memory in Settings → Personalization → Memory. This allows ChatGPT to remember team structures, recurring deadlines, your preferred report format, vendor names, and project timelines across sessions. Over time, it builds an operational context layer that makes every interaction faster and more relevant — no need to re-explain your team's setup each time.

Daily Workflows — 6 Core Use Cases
Ready-to-use ChatGPT workflows for the tasks that define an operations professional's week

The following six workflows cover the tasks that consume the most time for operations and admin professionals. Each includes a description of the workflow, the context for when and why to use it, and a copy-ready prompt — replace the placeholders in brackets and run. These prompts are designed to work standalone or inside your Ops Assistant Custom GPT for even better results.

📋
1 — SOP Creation & Documentation
Transform rough notes into structured, version-controlled SOPs

Standard operating procedures are the foundation of consistent operations, but they take forever to write well. ChatGPT excels at transforming rough process notes, verbal descriptions, or bullet-point outlines into fully structured SOPs — complete with purpose statements, scope definitions, role assignments, numbered procedures with sub-steps, quality checkpoints, and escalation paths.

Best for: New process documentation · Updating legacy SOPs · Onboarding procedure manuals · Compliance procedures · IT/system process guides · Safety protocols

SOP CREATION — STRUCTURED DOCUMENT PROMPT
Act as an operations documentation specialist. I need to create a standard operating procedure for [PROCESS — e.g., "employee onboarding for new hires in the marketing department"]. Include the following sections: Purpose (why this SOP exists), Scope (who and what it covers), Roles & Responsibilities (who does what), Step-by-Step Procedure (numbered with sub-steps where needed), Quality Checks (verification points at key stages), Escalation Path (what to do when exceptions occur), and a Revision History table (with columns for Version, Date, Author, Changes). Format in clear sections with professional headings. The audience is [TEAM/ROLE — e.g., "HR coordinators and team leads"] and their technical level is [BEGINNER/INTERMEDIATE/ADVANCED]. Use concise, action-oriented language throughout.
📝
2 — Meeting Agendas, Minutes & Action Items
Pre-meeting agendas, real-time minutes templates, post-meeting action tracking

Meetings are the heartbeat of operations work — but the admin around them (agendas, minutes, action items) consumes disproportionate time. ChatGPT can generate structured agendas before the meeting, provide a minutes template during it, and extract action items with owners and deadlines afterward. Use your Meeting Management Project to maintain context about recurring meetings and standing agenda items.

Best for: Weekly team standups · Board/leadership meetings · Project status reviews · Cross-department syncs · Client check-ins · All-hands preparation

MEETING AGENDA — STRUCTURED PROMPT
I need a structured meeting agenda for our [MEETING TYPE — e.g., "weekly operations review"]. Attendees: [LIST NAMES AND ROLES]. Duration: [TIME — e.g., "45 minutes"]. Key topics to cover: [TOPICS — e.g., "project status updates, budget review Q3, vendor contract renewal decision, new hire onboarding timeline"]. For each topic, include: time allocation in minutes, discussion lead (assign from attendee list), desired outcome (Decision / Update / Brainstorm / Action Required), and any pre-read materials needed. Add a standing "Parking Lot" section for off-topic items and an "Action Item Tracker" table at the end with columns for Action, Owner, Deadline, and Status. Format professionally — this will be distributed to attendees beforehand.
📧
3 — Vendor Communication & Comparison
RFP drafts, vendor comparison matrices, negotiation emails

Vendor management involves a constant stream of communication — requests for proposals, comparison analyses, contract summaries, and negotiation follow-ups. ChatGPT can draft professional vendor communications, build weighted scoring matrices for vendor evaluation, and generate comparison documents that present options clearly for stakeholder decision-making.

Best for: RFP/RFI drafts · Vendor comparison matrices · Contract summary briefs · Negotiation emails · Service review communications · Vendor onboarding documentation

VENDOR COMPARISON — DECISION MATRIX PROMPT
Draft a professional vendor comparison analysis. I'm evaluating [NUMBER — e.g., "3"] vendors for [SERVICE/PRODUCT — e.g., "managed IT support services"]. For each vendor, analyse: Pricing (include tier/plan breakdowns if available), Service Level Agreement terms (response times, uptime guarantees), Implementation timeline (from contract signing to go-live), Support quality (response times, dedicated account manager availability), Integration capabilities (with our existing systems: [LIST]), and Contract flexibility (minimum terms, exit clauses, scaling options). Present the analysis as a weighted decision matrix with scores out of 10 for each criterion. Include a weighting column so stakeholders can adjust priorities. Add a summary recommendation paragraph at the end. Vendors to compare: [LIST WITH KEY DETAILS YOU KNOW ABOUT EACH].
🔄
4 — Process Mapping & Workflow Design
Document existing processes, identify bottlenecks, design improvements

Process improvement is the highest-leverage operations activity, but it requires time to document current state, analyse bottlenecks, and design future state. ChatGPT can accelerate each phase — turning your verbal description of a process into a structured flow, identifying inefficiencies, and proposing redesigned workflows with clear ownership and SLA timeframes at each step.

Best for: Current-state process documentation · Bottleneck identification · Process redesign proposals · Automation opportunity assessment · Handoff risk analysis · Exception handling design

PROCESS MAPPING — OPTIMISATION PROMPT
Help me map and optimise this business process: [DESCRIBE CURRENT PROCESS — e.g., "When a new client signs a contract, the sales team emails the ops team, who manually create a project folder, assign a project manager, set up the client in our CRM, schedule a kickoff meeting, and send a welcome pack. Currently takes 3-5 business days with frequent handoff delays"]. First, document the current state as a numbered step-by-step flow with the responsible person/team at each step. Then identify: bottlenecks (where delays typically occur), redundant steps (work being duplicated), handoff risks (where information gets lost between people/teams), and automation opportunities (steps that don't require human judgment). Finally, redesign the process with: a streamlined workflow (fewer steps), clear ownership at each step, SLA timeframes for each handoff, and exception handling procedures for when things go wrong. Format as a structured document with Current State, Analysis, and Proposed Future State sections. Mark decision points clearly.
📊
5 — Report Generation & Dashboards
Weekly operations reports, KPI summaries, executive briefings

Reporting is one of the most time-consuming recurring tasks in operations. ChatGPT can generate report templates, structure executive summaries from raw data, create KPI dashboards with traffic-light status indicators, and produce narrative explanations of trends. When paired with the Data Analysis feature (upload CSV or Excel files), it can analyse your actual data and generate insights automatically.

Best for: Weekly operations reports · Monthly KPI summaries · Executive briefings · Department performance reviews · Project status dashboards · Board report preparation

WEEKLY OPERATIONS REPORT — TEMPLATE PROMPT
Generate a weekly operations report template for [DEPARTMENT/TEAM — e.g., "the Operations team at a 50-person SaaS company"]. Include the following sections: KPI Summary (with a traffic-light status system — Green/Amber/Red — for each metric, plus a brief one-line commentary for any non-green items), Key Achievements This Week (3-5 bullet points), Issues & Blockers (with severity ratings: Critical/High/Medium/Low and assigned owner for each), Resource Utilisation (team capacity overview — who's at capacity, who has bandwidth), Upcoming Milestones (next 2 weeks, with dates and owners), and Risks & Mitigations (potential issues on the horizon with proposed preventive actions). Format for executive readability — use bullet points, not paragraphs. Keep the total report to one page equivalent. Include placeholder data I can replace with actuals each week.
📅
6 — Scheduling & Resource Coordination
Shift planning, project resource allocation, capacity planning

Resource coordination — matching the right people to the right tasks at the right time — is a puzzle that operations professionals solve continuously. ChatGPT can build allocation matrices, identify over-utilisation risks, suggest backup assignments for critical tasks, and flag skill gaps before they become problems. While it can't replace scheduling software for real-time management, it excels at the planning and analysis phase.

Best for: Project resource allocation · Team capacity planning · Shift scheduling frameworks · Cross-functional team assignments · Seasonal workload planning · Backup and succession planning

RESOURCE ALLOCATION — PLANNING PROMPT
I need to create a resource allocation plan for [PROJECT/PERIOD — e.g., "Q3 product launch across 4 workstreams"]. Team members: [LIST WITH ROLES AND AVAILABILITY — e.g., "Sarah (Project Lead, full-time), James (Developer, 60% available — also on maintenance), Priya (Designer, 80% available), Tom (QA, full-time), Lisa (Content, 3 days/week)"]. Deliverables: [LIST WITH DEADLINES — e.g., "Website redesign by July 15, Marketing collateral by July 30, Training materials by Aug 10, Launch event coordination by Aug 20"]. Constraints: [BUDGET/TIME/SKILL CONSTRAINTS — e.g., "No additional headcount, James cannot be allocated more than 60%, design work must be completed before content can start"]. Create an allocation matrix showing: who is assigned to what deliverable, for which time periods (weekly blocks), percentage of their capacity used per week, backup assignments for critical-path tasks, and flagged warnings for any over-allocation (>100%) or skill gaps. Format as a table with a summary paragraph highlighting the top 3 risks to this plan.

Advanced Techniques
Custom GPTs, Projects, Memory, Data Analysis, and Workflows for operations power users

Once you're running the six core workflows confidently, these advanced techniques multiply your impact further. Each builds on the foundation from Section 02 and works best when you've already established your Ops Assistant Custom GPT and operational Projects.

Power Features for Operations Teams
🤖
Custom GPTs as Department Assistants
Build dedicated GPTs for each operational domain: an HR Bot loaded with your employee handbook, leave policies, and onboarding checklists; an IT Helpdesk GPT trained on your troubleshooting guides and system documentation; a Procurement Checker that knows your approved vendor list, budget thresholds, and purchasing policies. Each becomes a specialist assistant that anyone on the team can query.
📁
Projects for Ongoing Ops Management
Each ChatGPT Project retains conversation history and uploaded files across sessions. Structure one Project per operational domain: Weekly Reporting (stores past reports, KPI definitions, and trend notes), Vendor Management (contracts, comparison history, communication logs), Process Improvement (current process maps, improvement proposals, implementation trackers). Context compounds — the more you use each Project, the better the outputs become.
🧠
Memory for Persistent Team Context
ChatGPT's Memory feature remembers facts across all conversations: team member names and roles, recurring meeting schedules, your preferred report format, vendor contacts, project codenames, and organisational structure. After a few weeks of regular use, ChatGPT anticipates your context — drafting your Monday report already formatted the way you like it, referencing team members by name without being reminded.
📈
Data Analysis for Operations Metrics
Upload CSV or Excel files directly into ChatGPT and use its Data Analysis (Advanced) feature to generate charts, identify trends, calculate KPIs, and produce narrative summaries of your operational data. Upload your time-tracking exports, budget spreadsheets, incident logs, or resource utilisation data — and get visualisations and insights without needing a BI tool or analyst.
Workflows — Multi-Step Automation Chains

ChatGPT Workflows (available on Plus and Team plans) allow you to build multi-step automation sequences that run in order. For operations teams, this unlocks powerful repeatable processes:

  • Monday Morning Report Pipeline: Upload this week's data → Generate KPI dashboard → Write executive summary → Format for email distribution → Output final report
  • New Vendor Onboarding: Input vendor details → Generate comparison against existing vendors → Create contract summary brief → Draft welcome communication → Produce onboarding checklist
  • Meeting-to-Action Converter: Paste meeting transcript → Extract key decisions → Identify action items with owners → Generate follow-up email → Update project tracker format
  • Process Audit Sequence: Describe current process → Identify waste and bottlenecks → Benchmark against best practices → Generate improvement proposal → Create implementation timeline

Each Workflow runs as a single trigger — you provide the initial input once, and ChatGPT executes every step in sequence. This eliminates the need to run each prompt separately and ensures consistency across the entire chain.

💡
Start With One Custom GPT, Then Scale

Don't try to build five department GPTs in one sitting. Start with your Ops Assistant (Section 02), use it daily for two weeks, and refine its instructions based on what works. Then clone it as a starting point for your next department-specific GPT. The knowledge and formatting patterns you develop in the first GPT transfer directly — you're building an operational template, not starting from scratch each time.

Prompt Library — 15 Ready-to-Use Prompts
Copy-ready prompts across five categories — documentation, communications, analysis, planning, and templates
PROCESS DOCUMENTATION TEMPLATE
Create a comprehensive process documentation template for [PROCESS NAME — e.g., "Invoice Processing and Payment Approval"]. Structure it with these sections: 1) Process Overview (purpose, scope, process owner), 2) Prerequisites (what must be in place before the process starts), 3) Detailed Procedure (numbered steps with sub-steps, each step identifying the responsible role, required system/tool, and expected output), 4) Exception Handling (common exceptions and how to resolve each), 5) Quality Checkpoints (verification gates at critical stages), 6) Related Documents (linked SOPs, forms, and policies), 7) Revision History table. Write in clear, imperative language. Target audience: [ROLE/TEAM]. Include time estimates for each major step.
POLICY DRAFT — INTERNAL ORGANISATIONAL POLICY
Draft an internal policy document for [POLICY TOPIC — e.g., "Remote Work and Flexible Working Arrangements"]. Include: Policy Title and Reference Number, Effective Date, Policy Owner, Purpose Statement (why this policy exists), Scope (who it applies to), Definitions (key terms), Policy Statements (the actual rules, numbered), Procedures for Implementation (how to comply), Roles and Responsibilities (who enforces what), Exceptions and Approval Process (how to request an exception), Compliance and Consequences (what happens if violated), Review Schedule (when the policy will be reviewed), and Approval Signatures section. Tone: authoritative but clear. Avoid legal jargon — this needs to be understood by all employees. Organisation context: [BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF YOUR ORGANISATION].
KNOWLEDGE BASE ARTICLE — INTERNAL REFERENCE
Write an internal knowledge base article for [TOPIC — e.g., "How to Submit an IT Support Ticket"]. Format for a searchable internal wiki. Include: a clear title optimised for search, a 2-sentence summary at the top, Prerequisites (what you need before starting), Step-by-Step Instructions (numbered, with screenshots placeholders marked as [Screenshot: description]), Troubleshooting section (3-5 common issues and solutions), Related Articles (suggest 3 related topics), and a "Last Updated" date field. Write at a non-technical reading level. Keep under 500 words — knowledge base articles should be scannable, not essays.
STAKEHOLDER UPDATE EMAIL
Draft a stakeholder update email for [PROJECT/INITIATIVE NAME]. Recipients: [STAKEHOLDER GROUP — e.g., "Senior Leadership Team"]. Key updates to communicate: [LIST 3-5 UPDATES]. Current status: [ON TRACK / AT RISK / DELAYED]. Structure the email with: a one-line executive summary at the top (bold), a 3-bullet status update (what's been completed, what's in progress, what's coming next), any decisions or approvals needed (with deadline), and risks/blockers requiring attention. Tone: confident, concise, and action-oriented. Keep under 200 words — executives scan, they don't read. Sign off as [YOUR NAME, TITLE].
CHANGE MANAGEMENT ANNOUNCEMENT
Write an internal communication announcing [CHANGE — e.g., "migration from Slack to Microsoft Teams starting August 1"]. Audience: [ALL STAFF / SPECIFIC DEPARTMENT]. Include: what is changing (clear, specific), why it's changing (the business reason — be honest), when it takes effect (key dates and phases), what employees need to do (specific actions with deadlines), what support is available (training sessions, help desk, FAQs), who to contact with questions. Tone: transparent, empathetic, and practical. Acknowledge that change is disruptive. Open with the "what" — don't bury the lead. Keep under 300 words. Format with clear headings and bullet points for scannability.
CROSS-DEPARTMENT REQUEST
Draft a professional email requesting [WHAT YOU NEED] from [DEPARTMENT/PERSON]. Context: [WHY YOU NEED IT — the business reason]. Deadline: [DATE]. What you need specifically: [DETAILED REQUIREMENTS — be precise about deliverables, format, and scope]. What you've already done on your end: [PREPARATION COMPLETED]. Impact if delayed: [CONSEQUENCE — frame in terms of shared goals, not blame]. Offer: [WHAT YOU CAN PROVIDE IN RETURN — reciprocity helps]. Tone: collaborative and respectful of their workload. Keep under 150 words. End with a specific ask and suggested next step (e.g., "Could we do a 15-minute call Thursday to align on scope?").
PROCESS AUDIT CHECKLIST
Create a comprehensive process audit checklist for [PROCESS/DEPARTMENT — e.g., "Accounts Payable invoice processing"]. Structure as a table with columns: Audit Item, Expected Standard, Actual Finding (blank for auditor to fill), Compliance Status (Compliant/Non-Compliant/Partial), and Notes. Include audit items across these categories: Process Documentation (are SOPs current and accessible?), Process Execution (are steps being followed as documented?), Controls and Approvals (are authorisation thresholds being respected?), System Accuracy (does data in systems match process outputs?), Timeliness (are SLA targets being met?), and Exception Handling (are exceptions documented and escalated properly?). Add 5-8 items per category. Include a Summary section with overall compliance score methodology and recommended actions format.
VENDOR EVALUATION FRAMEWORK
Build a vendor evaluation framework for selecting a [SERVICE/PRODUCT CATEGORY — e.g., "cloud-based project management platform"]. Create a weighted scoring matrix with these evaluation categories (suggest appropriate weights totalling 100%): Functionality and Feature Fit, Pricing and Total Cost of Ownership, Implementation Complexity and Timeline, Vendor Stability and Track Record, Support Quality and SLA Terms, Security and Compliance, Integration with Existing Systems ([LIST CURRENT SYSTEMS]), and Scalability. For each category, list 3-5 specific evaluation criteria with a 1-5 scoring guide (define what 1, 3, and 5 mean for each). Include a template for vendor demo evaluation notes and a final recommendation summary format. Output as a structured document our procurement team can use immediately.
BUDGET VARIANCE ANALYSIS
Create a budget variance analysis template and narrative for [DEPARTMENT/PROJECT — e.g., "Operations Department Q2 2026"]. Budget categories to analyse: [LIST — e.g., "Personnel, Software/Tools, External Contractors, Travel, Office/Facilities, Training"]. For each category, structure the analysis as: Budgeted Amount, Actual Spend, Variance ($ and %), Explanation of Variance (template text prompting for root cause), and Corrective Action (if over budget). Include a summary section with: total budget performance, top 3 over-budget areas with impact assessment, top 3 under-budget areas with reallocation recommendations, and a forecast for remainder of period. Format for presentation to [FINANCE TEAM / LEADERSHIP]. Use professional financial reporting language.
PROJECT KICKOFF BRIEF
Create a project kickoff brief for [PROJECT NAME — e.g., "Office Relocation to New Premises"]. Include: Project Overview (what, why, and expected outcomes in 3-4 sentences), Project Scope (in-scope and explicitly out-of-scope items), Key Stakeholders (RACI matrix format — Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed), Timeline and Milestones (major phases with target dates), Budget Overview (high-level allocation by category), Key Risks (top 5, each with likelihood, impact, and mitigation), Success Criteria (how we'll know it's done well), Communication Plan (who gets updated, how often, via what channel), and Immediate Next Steps (first 5 actions with owners and deadlines). Format as a professional one-pager that can be distributed at the kickoff meeting. Keep concise — this is a reference document, not a project plan.
QUARTERLY OPERATIONS REVIEW
Create a Quarterly Operations Review (QOR) template for [Q_ 2026]. Sections to include: Executive Summary (3-4 sentences covering quarter performance), KPI Dashboard (table with: KPI Name, Target, Actual, Status [Green/Amber/Red], Trend [Up/Down/Flat] — include 8-10 standard ops KPIs), Major Accomplishments (5-7 items with business impact quantified), Challenges and Lessons Learned (what went wrong and what we learned), Process Improvements Implemented (changes made this quarter with measured results), Resource and Capacity Overview (team utilisation, open positions, attrition), Next Quarter Priorities (top 5 with success metrics and owners), and Budget Performance (summary with YTD tracking). Format for a 30-minute leadership presentation — include speaker notes for key slides. Use placeholder data I can replace with actuals.
CAPACITY PLANNING MODEL
Build a capacity planning model for [TEAM/DEPARTMENT — e.g., "a 12-person operations team"] for the next [PERIOD — e.g., "6 months"]. Current team composition: [LIST ROLES AND HEADCOUNT]. Known upcoming work: [LIST PROJECTS/INITIATIVES WITH ESTIMATED EFFORT]. Standing operational load: [DESCRIBE RECURRING WORK — e.g., "daily ticket processing averages 40 tickets/day, weekly reporting takes 8 hours, monthly audit prep takes 3 days"]. Constraints: [BUDGET FOR NEW HIRES / CONTRACTOR BUDGET / TRAINING TIME]. Build a model showing: current capacity in hours per week, projected demand per month (broken down by standing ops + project work), capacity gap analysis (where demand exceeds supply), recommendations (hire, redistribute, automate, defer — with cost/benefit for each), and a risk assessment for peak periods. Format as a structured document with summary table and narrative explanation.
ONBOARDING CHECKLIST GENERATOR
Generate a comprehensive new employee onboarding checklist for [ROLE — e.g., "Operations Analyst joining the Melbourne office"]. Structure in phases: Pre-Start (1-2 weeks before Day 1 — IT setup, workspace, access provisioning, welcome email), Day 1 (arrival logistics, orientation schedule, key introductions, system access verification), Week 1 (training sessions, shadowing schedule, key document reading list, first tasks), Month 1 (deeper training, first project assignment, 30-day check-in meeting, feedback collection), and 90-Day Milestones (performance expectations, independence checkpoints, probation review preparation). For each item include: task description, responsible person (HR / Manager / IT / Buddy), due date (relative to start date), and a checkbox placeholder. Include a "Tools and Systems Access" sub-checklist with common business systems: [LIST YOUR SYSTEMS]. Make it printable and trackable.
INCIDENT REPORT TEMPLATE
Create a professional incident report template for [TYPE OF INCIDENT — e.g., "operational disruption / system outage / workplace safety"]. Include these sections: Incident Reference Number (format suggestion), Date/Time of Incident, Date/Time Reported, Reported By (name, role, contact), Incident Category (dropdown options: [SUGGEST 6-8 CATEGORIES]), Severity Level (Critical/High/Medium/Low with definitions for each), Incident Description (structured prompts: What happened? When did it start? Who was affected? What systems/processes were impacted?), Immediate Actions Taken (numbered list), Root Cause Analysis (5 Whys framework with guided prompts), Impact Assessment (operational impact, financial impact, reputational impact, compliance impact), Corrective Actions (table: Action, Owner, Deadline, Status), Preventive Measures (what changes will prevent recurrence), Lessons Learned, and Sign-Off section (investigator, department head, senior management). Format as a fillable template.
STANDARD MEETING MINUTES
Create a professional meeting minutes template and then populate it based on these meeting notes: [PASTE YOUR RAW MEETING NOTES — bullet points, shorthand, or voice-to-text transcript]. Meeting details: [MEETING NAME], [DATE], [ATTENDEES]. Format the minutes with: Meeting Header (title, date, time, location/virtual, attendees present, apologies), Agenda Items Discussed (for each: topic, key discussion points summarised in 2-3 sentences, decisions made, and action items), Action Items Summary Table (columns: Action Item, Owner, Deadline, Priority, Status — extract ALL actions mentioned in the notes), Parking Lot Items (topics raised but deferred), Next Meeting (date, time, proposed agenda items). Common Mistakes & What to Do Instead
Five patterns that limit results — and the fix for each
Mistake 1 — Pasting Confidential Data Without Redacting
❌ Avoid

Copying and pasting raw HR data — employee names, salaries, performance ratings, disciplinary records, or personal contact details — directly into ChatGPT. The same applies to unredacted financial data, customer PII, and confidential contract terms. This data is processed on OpenAI's servers and may be retained.

✓ Do This Instead

Anonymise all sensitive data before sending. Replace employee names with "Employee A/B/C", remove salary figures or replace with bands, strip personal identifiers, and use generic descriptions for confidential terms. ChatGPT doesn't need real names to generate an SOP or process document — it needs the structure. Keep the structure, remove the sensitivity.

Mistake 2 — Using AI Output as Final SOPs Without Review
❌ Avoid

Publishing ChatGPT-generated SOPs, policies, or process documents directly without subject-matter expert review. AI can produce structurally perfect documents that contain procedural errors, miss critical safety steps, misinterpret compliance requirements, or describe a process that doesn't match how your organisation actually operates.

✓ Do This Instead

Treat every ChatGPT output as a high-quality first draft. Always route SOPs and policies through the process owner for validation before publishing. Build a simple review workflow: AI generates draft → process owner reviews accuracy → compliance/legal check if applicable → sign-off → publish with version control. The AI saves you 80% of the drafting time; the human review ensures 100% accuracy.

Mistake 3 — Creating One Mega-Prompt for Everything
❌ Avoid

Trying to get ChatGPT to produce an entire operations manual, complete quarterly review, or multi-phase project plan in a single prompt. Overloaded prompts produce shallow, generic output because the model tries to cover everything without depth on anything. The result reads like a template, not a useful document.

✓ Do This Instead

Break complex operational tasks into sequential, focused prompts. First: generate the structure/outline. Second: expand each section individually with full detail. Third: review and refine specific sections. This "chain of prompts" approach produces dramatically better output because each prompt gets the model's full attention on one task. Use Projects to maintain context across the sequence.

Mistake 4 — Ignoring Custom GPTs for Repetitive Tasks
❌ Avoid

Re-explaining your organisation's context, formatting preferences, terminology, and process standards in every single conversation. If you're typing the same context paragraph at the start of every session — your company name, team structure, reporting format, compliance requirements — you're wasting time and getting inconsistent results.

✓ Do This Instead

Build department-specific Custom GPTs loaded with your actual SOPs, templates, style guides, and organisational context. Your Ops Assistant should already know your company name, team structure, preferred formatting, and key processes. One-time setup, permanent time savings. Every conversation starts with context instead of from zero.

Mistake 5 — Not Version-Controlling AI-Generated Documents
❌ Avoid

Generating SOPs, policies, and process documents with ChatGPT and saving only the final output — without recording which prompt produced it, what version of the Custom GPT was used, or what instructions were given. When the document needs updating or auditing, you have no reproducibility trail and no way to regenerate consistently.

✓ Do This Instead

Save the prompt alongside every significant AI-generated document. Create a simple "Prompt Log" — a spreadsheet or shared document with columns for: Document Name, Date Generated, Prompt Used, Custom GPT Name, Key Modifications Made, and Document Version. This gives you an audit trail, makes updates reproducible, and allows your team to reuse successful prompts. For compliance-sensitive documents, this log is essential for demonstrating how the document was created.
