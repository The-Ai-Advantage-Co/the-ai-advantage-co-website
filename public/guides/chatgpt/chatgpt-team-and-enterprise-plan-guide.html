What is ChatGPT Team and Enterprise Plan Guide?
The paid plans for organisations — what's included, when to choose which, and how to deploy

ChatGPT Team and ChatGPT Enterprise are OpenAI's organisational plans. Team is for small to mid-sized teams (2-200 users); Enterprise is for larger organisations with serious admin, compliance, and security requirements. Both share the same model capability but differ on admin, data, and support.

Best for: anyone evaluating which ChatGPT plan to deploy organisationally, or planning a rollout.

👥
Team Plan
2-200 users, $25-30/user/mo. Basic admin, no SSO enforcement. Right for SMB and most mid-market.
🏢
Enterprise Plan
150+ seats, custom pricing. SSO, audit logs, data residency, BAA. Required for regulated industries.
🔐
Data Protection
Both: training opt-out by default. Enterprise adds enforcement and data residency choice.
📊
Admin & Compliance
Enterprise SSO, user management, audit logs, dedicated support, SLAs.
✨ Selecting between Team and Enterprise for your organisation ✨
✨¢ 2026 UPDATE — BETTER COMPLIANCE POSTURE
Both Team and Enterprise have meaningfully improved on compliance through 2025-2026. Both now support data residency in major regions, both have SOC 2 Type II + ISO 27001, and Enterprise has the BAA option for HIPAA workloads. The plan choice is less driven by compliance than it was 12 months ago.

  • Custom GPTs with org-wide sharing
  • Memory and Memory Sources
  • Canvas, image generation, voice, advanced data analysis
  • Code Interpreter and file uploads

The shared base — what every paid org user gets

Both Team and Enterprise share the full feature surface. The differentiator is admin and compliance, not capability.

  • Latest GPT-5.5 family models (Instant, GPT-5.5, GPT-5.5 Pro)
  • Custom GPTs with org-wide sharing within the workspace
  • Memory and Memory Sources for cross-conversation context
  • Canvas, image generation, voice mode, advanced data analysis
  • Code Interpreter and file uploads
  • Data not used for model training (this is the default for both)
04
What Enterprise Adds
Beyond Team
  • SSO (SAML, OIDC) — required for most enterprise deployments
  • Admin user management — provisioning, deprovisioning, group controls
  • Audit logs — every interaction logged for compliance
  • Data residency — choose region for processing
  • Custom models — fine-tuning on your data (with strict isolation)
  • Dedicated support — named contact, defined SLAs
  • Volume pricing — meaningful discounts at 1000+ seats
  • BAA — HIPAA workloads supported under business associate agreement

Enterprise's premium is mostly about admin and compliance, not capability. The model access is the same as Team. What Enterprise adds: SSO (required for most enterprise IT teams), audit logs, dedicated support, volume pricing at 500+ seats, BAA availability for HIPAA workloads, data residency choices, and stronger SLAs.

The decision shortcut: if your IT team requires SSO for business-critical SaaS, you need Enterprise. The other Enterprise-only features matter more in regulated industries (finance, healthcare, public sector) than in tech or general business.

05
Which to Choose
Decision shortcut

Team is right for: 2-200 user organisations that want managed ChatGPT without heavy admin requirements. Enterprise is right for: anyone needing SSO, audit logs, data residency, or volume pricing. Above ~150 users you're usually in Enterprise territory.

🎯 THE SSO THRESHOLD
If your IT team requires SSO for any business-critical SaaS, you need Enterprise. SSO is the most common single reason organisations move from Team to Enterprise — usually within 6-12 months of starting on Team.

✅ DO THIS

  • Default to Team for SMB and mid-market without compliance requirements
  • Choose Enterprise if SSO is required for business-critical SaaS in your org
  • Choose Enterprise for HIPAA workloads (BAA available)
  • Choose Enterprise if data residency choice matters
  • Choose Enterprise at 500+ seats for volume pricing
  • Pilot first — 4 weeks on either tier before broad commitment

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Choosing Enterprise for capability — both have the same model access
  • Starting on consumer Plus and migrating later — data continuity is messy
  • Skipping the pilot — surfaces blockers before broad rollout
  • Over-buying licences — start with active users, expand as adoption grows
  • Treating ChatGPT licences as 'set and forget' — needs active adoption work
  • Letting individuals expense Plus separately — creates shadow IT, no admin

Rollout Patterns
From licence to active usage
  • Pilot first — 20-50 users for 4 weeks before broader rollout
  • Champion model — identify 1-2 champions per function for adoption support
  • Training — 30 min onboarding + 4-week structured use-case introduction
  • Use case capture — collect what works in week 4, share org-wide
  • Measure — weekly active usage; time-saved cases per function

Comparison vs Other Plans
How Team/Enterprise differ from Plus and Pro
  • Plus ($20/mo, individual) — personal tier, no admin, data may be used for training unless opt-out
  • Pro ($200/mo, individual) — power user features, no org controls
  • Team — small-team org plan with basic admin
  • Enterprise — full org plan with SSO, admin, compliance
💼 WHEN TO MOVE UP THE PLAN STACK
Plus ($20/mo individual) → Team when more than one user OR when business data is involved. Team → Enterprise when SSO required, HIPAA workloads, or 500+ seats. Don't move up until you actually need the upgrade — the cost compounds quickly.

Common Mistakes & Fixes
What goes wrong — and how to avoid it

Most AI workflows that 'don't work' fall into one of these patterns. The fix is almost always sharper context and stricter review, not switching models or tools.

  • Treating ChatGPT Team and Enterprise Plan Guide as a finished answer rather than a draft to review
  • Vague prompts that don't specify audience, format, or what you want changed
  • Forgetting to give it context — the file, the brief, the constraints
  • Skipping the verify step — always check numbers, dates, names, and policy claims
  • Using it for tasks where a 30-second human action would be faster

✅ DO THIS

  • Provide context — the file, the brief, the audience, the constraints
  • Iterate; don't just accept the first output
  • Always review numbers, names, dates, and policy claims
  • Treat AI as a fast draft generator, not an answer engine
  • Capture what works so you can replicate it
  • Match the model to the task — fast modes for drafts, reasoning modes for analysis

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Sending AI output without reading it end-to-end
  • Vague prompts with no audience, format, or success criteria
  • Assuming AI can see files or context it hasn't been given
  • Using AI for tasks faster done manually in under a minute
  • Treating numbers, quotes, or citations as verified without checking
  • Defaulting to the slow reasoning mode for every task
