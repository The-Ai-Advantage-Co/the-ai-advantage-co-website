What is ChatGPT?

The AI that started the revolution — and what it actually does

ChatGPT is a conversational AI made by OpenAI. You type a message — a question, a task, an instruction — and it responds in natural language. It can write, analyse, code, reason, search the web, generate images, process documents, and have multi-turn conversations that feel surprisingly human.

Underneath, it's a large language model (LLM) — a system trained on vast amounts of text that learned to predict and generate language. But you don't need to understand the mechanics to use it well. What matters is knowing what it's good at, what it struggles with, and how to communicate with it effectively.

✍️

Write & Edit

Drafts, emails, reports, proposals, social posts, scripts — in any tone or style you specify. Edits your existing writing for clarity, tone, or length.

🧠

Reason & Analyse

Works through complex problems, summarises long documents, compares options, critiques arguments, and draws conclusions from information you provide.

💻

Code & Build

Writes, explains, debugs, and refactors code across most programming languages. Helps non-developers automate tasks and understand technical concepts.

🔍

Search & Research

Searches the live web for current information, summarises sources, compares products, and researches topics with citations — no browser tab-switching needed.

What ChatGPT is not

ChatGPT is not a search engine (it doesn't always know what happened yesterday), not a calculator (it can make arithmetic errors), and not infallible (it can confidently state incorrect things). Understanding its limits is as important as knowing its strengths.

02

Models & Modes

Choosing the right model for the right task

ChatGPT isn't one AI — it's a family of models with different strengths. The model switcher (top-left of the interface) lets you choose. Pick the wrong one and you're working against yourself. Pick the right one and the quality gap is significant.

GPT-4o
Default · Everyday

The general-purpose workhorse. Fast, capable, multimodal (handles text, images, files, voice). Handles the vast majority of everyday tasks excellently.

Best for: writing, editing, research, coding help, image analysis, most business tasks

o1 / o3
Reasoning · Complex

Slower, but thinks more carefully through hard problems. Designed for tasks requiring multi-step reasoning, logic, mathematics, and complex analysis where accuracy matters more than speed.

Best for: complex strategy, legal/financial analysis, multi-step problems, scientific reasoning

o3-mini
Fast Reasoning

The reasoning capability of o1 at much faster speed and lower cost. Good balance between analytical depth and response time for moderate-complexity tasks.

Best for: structured analysis, code review, quick reasoning tasks where speed matters

GPT-4o mini
Lightweight · Fast

A smaller, faster version of GPT-4o. Good for simple tasks where you need a quick answer. Less powerful for nuanced or complex requests.

Best for: simple Q&A, short summaries, quick formatting tasks, high-volume lightweight use

💡
Start with GPT-4o

For 90% of tasks, GPT-4o is the right choice. Only switch to o1/o3 when you're doing something that genuinely requires deep multi-step reasoning — legal analysis, complex maths, structured argument building. The speed trade-off isn't worth it for everyday work.

03

Starting Conversations

How to prompt, how to iterate, and how to get what you actually need

Most people write vague prompts and get vague results, then conclude ChatGPT isn't very good. The quality of what you get is almost entirely determined by the quality of what you ask. A well-structured prompt is the single most important skill for getting value from ChatGPT.

The Prompt Formula
🎭

Role (optional but powerful)

"Act as a senior marketing strategist…" — setting a role frames the perspective and expertise level of the response before you've said anything else.

🎯

Task — be precise

"Write a 300-word email to a potential client…" is better than "write an email". Specify the exact deliverable, length, format, and purpose.

📋

Context — what ChatGPT needs to know

Industry, audience, constraints, background. "Our clients are SME business owners in the UK financial services sector" changes the response dramatically.

📐

Format — how you want it

"Return this as a numbered list / table / bullet points / three-paragraph structure". ChatGPT defaults to paragraphs — if you want structure, ask for it.

Iterating — the real skill
1

Send your initial prompt

Don't try to write the perfect prompt first time. Get a first response, then refine. Conversation is cheaper than perfection.

2

Tell it what to change — specifically

"Make it shorter" is less useful than "cut this to 150 words, keeping only the three strongest points". The more specific your feedback, the better the revision.

3

Ask it to try again differently

"Try again but write it as if you're talking to a non-expert" or "give me three alternative versions with different tones". Multiple options are usually better than one revision.

4

Use follow-up questions

"What are the risks of this approach?" or "What did you leave out?" — follow-ups unlock more value than single-shot prompts in almost every case.

📝
Conversations have memory within a session

ChatGPT remembers everything you've said in the current conversation. You don't have to repeat context with every message — just refer back: "Based on the email draft above, now write the follow-up." Use this to build on earlier outputs naturally.

04

Memory & Continuity

How ChatGPT remembers — and how to make it remember what matters

By default, each new conversation starts completely fresh — ChatGPT has no memory of previous sessions. But with Memory enabled (Settings → Personalisation → Memory), ChatGPT builds a persistent profile of facts you've shared, preferences you've expressed, and context you've provided across all conversations.

Memory typeHow it worksBest for
Within conversationFull recall of everything said — automatic, always onMulti-step tasks, iterative drafting, complex projects in one session
Memory (saved facts)ChatGPT saves facts about you and your preferences across sessionsRole, industry, tone preferences, ongoing projects, regular context
Custom InstructionsYou write a permanent system-level instruction that applies to all chatsSetting your default persona, response style, or context once

Custom Instructions — set them once, benefit forever

Go to Settings → Personalisation → Custom Instructions. You get two fields: what ChatGPT should know about you, and how it should respond. Fill these in once and every future conversation starts with that context pre-loaded.

💡
What to put in Custom Instructions

Your role and industry, your organisation's name, your typical audience (clients, colleagues, executives), the tone you prefer (professional, direct, no jargon), what you're working on, and anything you never want ChatGPT to do (e.g. "never use bullet points unless I ask"). This alone transforms the quality of responses from your very first message.

GPTs & Custom Assistants

Pre-built specialist AI tools — and how to build your own

GPTs are customised versions of ChatGPT with specific instructions, knowledge, and tools pre-loaded. Instead of configuring every prompt from scratch, you open the right GPT and it's already set up for its specific job.

🔍

Research Assistants

Pre-configured to search, summarise, and cite sources for specific domains — finance, legal, scientific literature.

✍️

Writing Specialists

Copywriting, SEO, email writing, academic writing — each with a system prompt tuned to that specific style and output.

💻

Coding Assistants

Language-specific helpers, code reviewers, debugging assistants — pre-loaded with the relevant coding conventions and patterns.

📊

Data Analysts

Upload CSVs and get charts, summaries, correlations, and insights without writing a single line of Python.

🏢

Brand Assistants

Company-specific GPTs pre-loaded with brand guidelines, tone of voice, product knowledge, and FAQ documents.

🛠️

Your Own GPT

Build a custom GPT in minutes: name it, write its instructions, upload your documents, and share it with your team.

Building a GPT — three steps
1

Open the GPT Builder

Click your profile → My GPTs → Create a GPT. You can describe what you want in plain English and the builder generates the configuration, or set it up manually for more control.

2

Write the system prompt and upload knowledge

The system prompt is the GPT's "job description" — role, tone, scope, what it should and shouldn't do. Upload PDFs, Word docs, or text files to give it your specific knowledge base.

3

Publish and share

Set access to "Only me", "Anyone with link", or "Public". Share the link with your team. A well-built GPT for a specific use case saves hours of prompt-writing each week.

06

Voice, Vision & Files

Beyond text — the full multimodal toolkit

ChatGPT isn't limited to typed text. GPT-4o is natively multimodal — it can hear you, see images and documents, read files, generate images, and talk back. Most users only use 30% of what's available.

🎤

Voice Mode

Talk to ChatGPT and hear it respond in natural speech. Advanced Voice Mode can detect tone and emotion, interrupt mid-sentence, and maintain genuinely conversational flow. Available on mobile and desktop.

👁️

Vision — Image Input

Upload a photo, screenshot, chart, whiteboard, or document image. ChatGPT reads, describes, analyses, and answers questions about it. Paste a screenshot of a spreadsheet and ask it to summarise the trends.

🎨

Image Generation (DALL·E)

Describe an image and ChatGPT creates it. Marketing visuals, concept illustrations, presentation graphics, social media images — without needing design skills or tools.

📄

File Analysis

Upload PDFs, Word docs, spreadsheets, CSVs, or code files. ChatGPT reads, summarises, cross-references, and answers questions about the content. Process 100-page reports in seconds.

⚠️
Data privacy before uploading files

ChatGPT uses uploaded files to respond to your messages. Unless you're on a Team or Enterprise plan with data privacy controls, uploaded content may be used to improve OpenAI's models. Never upload confidential client data, personal information, or proprietary financial/legal documents to a personal plan.

Advanced Techniques

Prompting patterns that separate good results from great ones

🪜

Chain of Thought

Add "think step by step" or "reason through this before answering" to complex prompts. This forces ChatGPT to show its reasoning process and dramatically reduces errors on analytical tasks.

🔁

Regenerate & Compare

Ask for "3 different versions" of any output, then pick the best one. Or ask "What are the weaknesses of your last response?" — self-critique often surfaces improvements the first pass missed.

📌

Pinned System Context

Start long work sessions with a context block: "For this conversation: I'm a [role] at [company], working on [project], and the audience is [audience]." This front-loads context for all follow-up messages.

🗂️

Projects for Organisation

Use Projects (left sidebar) to group related conversations and upload persistent files. All chats within a Project share the same uploaded documents — no re-uploading each session.

The operator mode prompt pattern

For complex, multi-part tasks, structure your prompt like a brief:

ElementWhat to write
Context"I'm preparing a quarterly business review for a retail client with £5M turnover..."
Deliverable"Write an executive summary — max 200 words — covering: performance vs target, key wins, key risks, recommended focus for Q3."
Constraints"Tone: professional but plain English, no jargon. Do not include specific revenue figures (I'll add these manually)."
Format"Return as four short paragraphs. No bullet points."
08

Common Mistakes & Fixes

Why ChatGPT disappoints — and how to fix it

The response is too generic and could apply to anyone
You didn't give it enough context. Add your specific industry, audience, company size, and goal. "Write a follow-up email" gives nothing to work with. "Write a follow-up email to a UK-based SME owner who attended our AI workshop last Tuesday but hasn't booked a follow-up call" gives it everything.
ChatGPT made up a fact or cited a source that doesn't exist
This is called hallucination — the model generates plausible-sounding content that isn't accurate. Always verify specific facts, statistics, citations, and legal/medical/financial claims independently. Enable web search for current information, or provide the source material yourself as an uploaded file.
It won't do what I'm asking — refuses or hedges everything
Rephrase with more context about purpose. "Write a persuasive sales script" may be hedged; "Write talking points a sales rep can use when explaining the benefits of our software in a B2B demo" is more concrete and contextual. If refusal persists, the task may genuinely be outside what ChatGPT is built for.
The output is too long and padded with obvious statements
Add length and quality constraints. "Write this in under 150 words" + "No filler phrases, no obvious statements, no preamble — get straight to the point." You can also ask it to "edit this response to remove any sentences that don't add specific value."
It forgets what we discussed earlier in a long conversation
Very long conversations can exceed the context window — ChatGPT starts to "forget" early messages. Solution: start a new conversation with a brief recap of the key context. Or use Projects, which maintain uploaded files across conversations. Alternatively, paste key earlier content back in as a reminder.

Suggested Prompts

Ready-to-use prompts across core use cases

Meeting follow-up email
Write a follow-up email after a [type] meeting with [audience]. Key points covered: [list 3–5 points]. Tone: professional but warm. Include: a brief thank-you, a summary of what was agreed, next steps with owners, and a proposed date for the next meeting. Max 200 words.
Summarise a document
Summarise the document I'm about to paste. I need: (1) the main purpose in one sentence, (2) the 5 key points, (3) any recommendations or action items, (4) anything that needs a decision or follow-up. Keep the summary under 300 words. Here is the document: [paste text]
Analyse and improve a piece of writing
Here is a piece of writing I need to improve: [paste text]. The audience is [audience]. Please: (1) identify the 3 biggest weaknesses, (2) rewrite it addressing those weaknesses, (3) explain what you changed and why. Target length: [word count].
Custom Instructions starter
I am a [role] at [type of company] in [industry]. My typical work involves [tasks]. My audience is usually [audience]. When responding: be direct and specific, avoid jargon unless technical context requires it, don't use filler phrases, and always give me something actionable. Preferred format: [bullets / paragraphs / tables as appropriate to the task].
Build a GPT system prompt
Help me write a system prompt for a custom GPT that acts as a [role — e.g. customer service assistant / onboarding guide / proposal writer] for [company type]. It should: answer questions from [knowledge base — I'll upload the files], always respond in [tone], decline to answer [out-of-scope topics], and ask clarifying questions when [condition]. Keep the system prompt under 500 words.
Session context opener
For this session: I'm a [role] working on [project or task]. The audience for everything I produce is [audience]. The goal is [outcome]. Key constraints: [list any constraints — length, tone, format, things to avoid]. All responses should be [tone]. Now: [first actual request].

See It In Action

A real work session — from blank canvas to finished output

Below is a complete working session: a manager needs to prepare a brief for a team restructure proposal. Watch how the conversation builds from context → draft → refinement → final format.

◉ GPT-4o
New conversation · Projects: Team Strategy
I need to write a brief for a team restructure proposal. We're merging two teams — Digital and Marketing — into one Content & Digital team under a new director. I need to get buy-in from senior leadership. Audience: CFO and COO. Tone: confident, concise, no waffle. Format: one-page executive brief with headings.
◉ ChatGPT
Here's an executive brief for the restructure proposal:

PROPOSAL: Content & Digital Team Integration

Purpose
Merge the Digital (6 FTE) and Marketing (8 FTE) teams under a unified Content & Digital function to eliminate duplication, accelerate delivery, and create a single point of accountability for brand and channel performance.

The Case for Change
Current structure creates three friction points: overlapping channel ownership, slower campaign approvals, and split reporting lines that delay decisions. Both teams are already collaborating informally — the restructure formalises what is already happening.

Proposed Structure
One Director of Content & Digital (new hire or internal promotion), two leads (Content, Digital), total headcount unchanged at 14 FTE.

Expected Outcomes
30% reduction in approval cycles · Single P&L line · Clearer succession planning

Decision Required
Approval to proceed to detailed implementation planning. Proposed effective date: [Quarter].
Good structure. Make the "Case for Change" section stronger — I need it to feel more urgent. Also add a risks section at the end.
◉ ChatGPT
Updated with stronger urgency and a Risks section added:

The Case for Change
Without consolidation, we will continue to lose 2–3 weeks per campaign cycle to alignment meetings between teams that share a brief but not a strategy. Competitors with unified content-digital functions are moving faster. Delaying this restructure costs time, budget, and talent retention — two senior members of both teams have flagged organisational clarity as a factor in their ongoing commitment.

Risks & Mitigations
Integration disruption: Phased transition over 90 days with joint working groups. Talent uncertainty: Communication plan pre-announcement, retention conversations within 2 weeks. Director selection: Internal process running in parallel, external search as contingency.
◉
What happened here

One initial prompt with strong context → solid first draft → a single, specific refinement request → significantly improved output. Total time: under 5 minutes. The key was the quality of the initial context: audience, tone, format, and purpose were all specified upfront.

Your Roadmap

From first prompt to confident daily user

Four stages — each one unlocks meaningfully more value than the last. Move at your own pace.

01

Send Your First Real Prompt

Take a piece of work you did this week and ask ChatGPT to help. Don't use a toy example — use a real task. See what it produces. Judge it honestly.

02

Set Up Your Defaults

Write your Custom Instructions. Enable Memory. Set up one Project for your most active work area. These three things change every future interaction.

03

Try All the Modalities

Use Voice Mode for a planning session. Upload a document for analysis. Use DALL·E for a quick visual. Try o1 on something complex. Learn what each tool is actually for.

04

Build a GPT for Your Team

Take your most-repeated task — the same brief, the same type of email, the same kind of report — and turn it into a GPT. Share it. Let it save everyone time, not just you.

💡
Your Quick Win

Open ChatGPT right now. Paste in the last email you had to write that took you more than 10 minutes. Ask it to write a better version in under 100 words. Then spend the 9 minutes you saved reading the rest of this guide.
