Section 10 — See It In Action
A short walkthrough

One paragraph. A concrete scenario showing the user applying the guide to a real situation.

The recognition test

Section 10 is one paragraph. A concrete scenario showing the user applying the guide to a real situation. Pick a scenario the reader will recognise — a Monday morning, a board prep, a difficult customer email.

  • One paragraph, no longer
  • Concrete scenario — specific people, specific tasks
  • The goal is recognition, not exhaustive coverage
  • Should read like a moment in someone's real work
  • Often the most-quoted section in client-facing material

Voice and Visual Identity
Brand application
  • Plain language, no hype
  • Lead with outcomes
  • Acknowledge limits honestly
  • Australian English
  • ChatGPT palette: green primary (#10A37F), charcoal sidebar (#1C1C1C), light grey page (#F9F9F9)
  • Include 2026 Update callout where relevant

✅ DO THIS

  • Plain language, no hype
  • Lead with outcomes ('cut meeting prep from 45 min to 10')
  • Acknowledge limits honestly
  • Australian English where applicable
  • ChatGPT palette: green (#10A37F), charcoal (#1C1C1C), light grey page (#F9F9F9)
  • Include 2026 Update callout where relevant

❌ AVOID THIS

  • 'Transformative', 'unlock', 'leverage', 'revolutionary' — hype tells
  • Throat-clearing openers ('In today's fast-paced world...')
  • Feature-list framing — readers skim past it
  • Soft-pedalling limits — undermines credibility
  • Mixing model versions across sections — pick current canonical
  • Letting sections drift past 500 words — restructure into sub-sections

Common Mistakes & Fixes
What goes wrong — and how to avoid it

Most AI workflows that 'don't work' fall into one of these patterns. The fix is almost always sharper context and stricter review, not switching models or tools.

  • Treating GPT User Guide — The AI Advantage Co Template as a finished answer rather than a draft to review
  • Vague prompts that don't specify audience, format, or what you want changed
  • Forgetting to give it context — the file, the brief, the constraints
  • Skipping the verify step — always check numbers, dates, names, and policy claims
  • Using it for tasks where a 30-second human action would be faster

✅ DO THIS

  • Provide context — the file, the brief, the audience, the constraints
  • Iterate; don't just accept the first output
  • Always review numbers, names, dates, and policy claims
  • Treat AI as a fast draft generator, not an answer engine
  • Capture what works so you can replicate it
  • Match the model to the task — fast modes for drafts, reasoning modes for analysis

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Sending AI output without reading it end-to-end
  • Vague prompts with no audience, format, or success criteria
  • Assuming AI can see files or context it hasn't been given
  • Using AI for tasks faster done manually in under a minute
  • Treating numbers, quotes, or citations as verified without checking
  • Defaulting to the slow reasoning mode for every task
