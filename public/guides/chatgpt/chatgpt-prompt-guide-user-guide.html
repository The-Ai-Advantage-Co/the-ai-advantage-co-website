01

What is Prompt Engineering?

Why the words you choose determine everything you get

Prompt engineering is the practice of crafting inputs to AI models to reliably produce high-quality outputs. It's not about memorising tricks — it's about understanding how language models process information and communicating in a way that gives them exactly what they need to do their best work.

The same question, phrased two different ways, can produce results that are worlds apart. A skilled prompter consistently gets results that feel like they came from a subject matter expert. An unskilled prompter gets generic responses and concludes that AI isn't very good. The difference is technique, not the tool.

🎯

Specificity Beats Brevity

The more specific and contextual your prompt, the better the output. ChatGPT doesn't benefit from vague instructions — it thrives on precision.

🔁

Prompting is a Conversation

One-shot prompts are rarely optimal. The best results come from treating ChatGPT as a collaborator — an initial prompt followed by targeted refinements.

🧠

Context is the Multiplier

ChatGPT has no knowledge of your specific situation unless you provide it. Every piece of relevant context you add multiplies the relevance of the output.

📐

Structure Drives Quality

Structured prompts produce structured outputs. Learning a small number of reusable prompt structures gives you reliable, predictable results across any topic.

◉
The prompting payoff

Research consistently shows that well-structured prompts improve output quality by 30–50% compared to vague equivalents. In practice, that gap is often larger — a bad prompt produces something you can't use; a great prompt produces something you can send or publish with minimal editing.

02

Anatomy of a Great Prompt

The four building blocks — and how to use them together

Every high-performing prompt is built from the same four components. You won't always use all four — but knowing each one means you know what's missing when results fall short.

R

Role

Who ChatGPT should be — expertise, perspective, persona

T

Task

What it should produce — verb, deliverable, scope

C

Context

What it needs to know — audience, background, constraints

F

Format

How to structure the output — length, layout, style

Weak vs strong — the same request, two very different results
Weak prompt
Write a case study about a client.
No role, no context, no format. ChatGPT will invent everything — industry, client type, outcomes, tone. The result will feel generic and unusable.
Strong prompt
You are a B2B marketing copywriter. Write a 400-word case study for a UK-based HR software company. The client is a 200-person professional services firm that reduced onboarding time by 40% using our platform. Audience: CFOs and HR directors. Tone: confident, data-led. Structure: challenge → solution → result. No jargon.
Role + specific task + full context + format instructions. Outputs something close to final copy.
Building a prompt — step by step
1

Start with the Role (optional but powerful)

"Act as a senior financial analyst…" or "You are an experienced HR business partner…" — this primes ChatGPT with the right knowledge base, vocabulary, and perspective before you ask for anything.

2

State the Task precisely

Use a strong verb: write, analyse, summarise, compare, critique, rewrite, list, draft. Then specify the deliverable and its scope. "Write a 250-word email" beats "write an email" every time.

3

Load the Context

Audience, industry, company size, goal, existing material, constraints, what to avoid. Don't make ChatGPT guess — anything it doesn't know, it invents. Anything you tell it, it uses.

4

Specify the Format

Word count, structure (bullet / paragraph / table / numbered list), sections to include, tone (professional, casual, direct, warm). Without format instructions, ChatGPT defaults to padded paragraphs.

💡
You don't need all four every time

A simple question doesn't need a full RTCF structure. Use it for anything where output quality matters — documents, emails, analysis, creative work. The more consequential the output, the more components you should include.

03

Prompt Patterns & Techniques

Eight proven patterns that unlock different capabilities

Prompt patterns are reusable structures that reliably produce specific types of output. Learn these eight and you have a toolkit for almost any task.

🎭
Role Prompting
When: you need expert-level knowledge or a specific perspective

Assign ChatGPT a specific expert identity. The role shapes vocabulary, depth, assumptions, and framing throughout the entire response.

"You are a senior employment lawyer in England. Advise me on the key risks of the following contract clause: [paste clause]"
🪜
Chain of Thought
When: complex analysis, multi-step problems, decisions with many variables

Ask ChatGPT to reason step-by-step before giving a conclusion. Reduces errors and surfaces assumptions you can challenge.

"Before answering, think through this step by step: [problem]. Show your reasoning, then give your recommendation."
📝
Few-Shot Prompting
When: you need output that matches a specific style, format, or pattern

Show ChatGPT 2–3 examples of what good looks like before asking it to produce something new. It learns the pattern and replicates it.

"Here are two examples of the tone I want: [Example 1] / [Example 2]. Now write a similar paragraph about [topic]."
🔄
Reverse Prompting
When: you're not sure what to ask, or want to discover the right question

Ask ChatGPT what it would need to know to answer your question well. Surfaces missing context you hadn't thought to provide.

"I want help with [vague goal]. Before we start, what information do you need from me to give the best possible answer?"
⚖️
Critic & Revise
When: you want to improve your own work or ChatGPT's first output

Ask ChatGPT to critique first, then revise. Self-critique produces significantly better second drafts than simple "improve this" requests.

"First, identify the three biggest weaknesses in the following text. Then rewrite it fixing those weaknesses: [paste text]"
🌿
Constraint Setting
When: outputs are too long, too generic, or off-tone

Set explicit constraints — what to include, exclude, length, reading level, tone, format. Constraints force precision and prevent padding.

"Write this in under 120 words. No bullet points. No filler phrases. No opening sentence that begins with 'I'. Get straight to the point."
🔀
Perspective Shifting
When: you need to stress-test an idea or see it from multiple angles

Ask ChatGPT to respond as different stakeholders, critics, or personas. Surfaces objections and considerations you might have missed.

"Respond to this proposal from three perspectives: (1) a sceptical CFO focused on ROI, (2) a team member worried about workload, (3) a customer who would be affected."
🔗
Prompt Chaining
When: a complex deliverable that's too large for a single prompt

Break the task into sequential prompts where each output feeds the next. Produces higher-quality results than trying to do everything in one go.

Prompt 1: "Outline the structure for a report on [topic]." → Prompt 2: "Write Section 1 from the outline above." → Prompt 3: "Write Section 2…"
04

Loading Context Effectively

How to give ChatGPT the information it needs to do its best work

Context is the single biggest lever in prompting. ChatGPT has no knowledge of your company, your clients, your industry nuances, or your specific situation. Every gap you leave, it fills with a generic default. Every context you provide, it uses to tailor the output.

Types of context — what to load and when
Context typeWhat to includeExample
Who you areRole, industry, organisation type, team size"I'm a marketing director at a 50-person UK SaaS company targeting enterprise clients."
Who the audience isJob title, knowledge level, concerns, what they care about"The audience is non-technical HR directors who are sceptical of AI tools."
What already existsPrevious drafts, source material, related documentsPaste the relevant text directly into the prompt or upload the file.
What the goal isDesired outcome, action you want the reader to take"The goal is to get the CFO to approve a £50K budget for a pilot."
What to avoidTone traps, forbidden phrases, topics out of scope"Don't mention competitors by name. Avoid technical jargon. No passive voice."
The context block technique

For complex tasks, open with a dedicated context block before the task instruction. This keeps context separate and readable:

Buried context
Write me a proposal for a project management tool for my company which is a mid-sized UK firm in professional services that has about 150 staff and currently uses spreadsheets and the audience is the ops director and it needs to be persuasive and professional.
Context is buried in one long run-on sentence. Easy for the model to miss elements.
Structured context block
Context: I work at a 150-person UK professional services firm. Current state: managing projects in spreadsheets. Audience: Operations Director. Goal: approve investment in a project management platform. Tone: persuasive, professional, concise.

Task: Write a one-page business case for upgrading to a dedicated project management tool. Include: problem statement, proposed solution, business benefits, indicative ROI, and recommended next step.
Context block + clear task. Every element is visible and used.
ℹ️
Use Custom Instructions for permanent context

Anything that's always true — your role, your industry, your default tone preference — belongs in Custom Instructions (Settings → Personalisation → Custom Instructions), not in every prompt. Reserve prompt-level context for task-specific information that changes between requests.

05

Iterating & Refining

How to turn a good first draft into the output you actually need

The best prompters don't write perfect prompts first time — they iterate efficiently. The conversation is the feature. ChatGPT remembers everything in the current session, so each message builds on what came before.

Refinement types — and when to use each
✂️

Length & Density

"Cut this to 150 words keeping only the three strongest points." / "Expand the second section — it's underdeveloped." Be specific about what to keep and what to cut.

🎨

Tone Adjustment

"Make this more direct — less diplomatic." / "Rewrite in a warmer, more conversational tone." / "Remove any language that sounds like corporate jargon." Tone refinements are often where the biggest quality jumps happen.

🔀

Structure Change

"Reorganise this — lead with the recommendation, then the rationale." / "Convert this into a table." / "Break this into three distinct sections with headings."

🎯

Focus Narrowing

"This is too broad — focus only on the commercial implications." / "Ignore the technical aspects, the audience won't care. Focus on business outcomes."

The refinement conversation — what good looks like
1

Send a solid first prompt

Use the RTCF structure. Don't try to write the perfect prompt — a strong first prompt gives you a useful starting point, not a finished product.

2

Evaluate specifically

Don't just say "this isn't right." Identify exactly what's wrong: too long, wrong tone, missing a section, wrong audience assumption. The more specific your diagnosis, the better the revision.

3

Give targeted instructions

"Keep everything except the third paragraph — rewrite that to focus on cost savings rather than features." Targeted beats vague every time.

4

Ask for alternatives when stuck

"Give me three alternative versions of the opening sentence with different tones." Multiple options unstick you faster than iterating on one version that isn't working.

5

Use self-critique to raise the bar

"What are the three weakest parts of your last response?" Then: "Now fix them." This often surfaces issues you wouldn't have spotted yourself.

💡
2–3 iterations is the sweet spot

Most professional outputs reach publishable quality in 2–3 exchanges. If you're on iteration 6 and it still isn't right, the issue is usually a missing piece of context. Stop iterating and add the context explicitly.

06

Output Format Mastery

How to get the exact structure, length, and layout you need

ChatGPT defaults to long, padded paragraphs unless you tell it otherwise. Format instructions are one of the highest-leverage additions to any prompt — they change not just how the output looks, but how usable it actually is.

Format goalWhat to say in your prompt
Short, tight output"Maximum 150 words. No filler. No preamble. Start with the first substantive point."
Bullet list"Return this as a bullet list. Maximum 6 bullets. Each bullet: one idea, one sentence."
Table"Format as a table with columns: [Column A] / [Column B] / [Column C]."
Structured document"Use the following structure: [Heading 1] / [Heading 2] / [Heading 3]. Each section: 2–3 paragraphs."
Numbered steps"Return as numbered steps. Each step: bold action verb title + one explanatory sentence."
Executive summary"Write a 3-paragraph executive summary: paragraph 1 = situation, paragraph 2 = key findings, paragraph 3 = recommendation."
Email format"Format as a professional email. Subject line: [topic]. Opening: [context]. Body: [key points]. Close: [clear CTA]."
JSON / structured data"Return the output as valid JSON with the following fields: name, summary, priority (high/medium/low), owner."
Tone vocabulary — say exactly what you mean
💼

Professional

Use: "professional, confident, authoritative, direct, plain English, no jargon, no hedging, no filler phrases, active voice."

🤝

Warm & Conversational

Use: "warm, conversational, friendly, approachable, human, informal but not casual, second person (you/your)."

🔬

Technical

Use: "technical, precise, detailed, assume the reader has domain expertise, include specifics, avoid simplification."

📣

Persuasive

Use: "persuasive, benefit-led, action-oriented, compelling, address objections, create urgency without pressure."

⚠️
Avoid vague tone words

"Make it better" or "make it more engaging" gives ChatGPT almost nothing to work with. Be precise: "Remove all passive voice sentences." "Replace every adjective with a specific example." "Cut every sentence that doesn't add new information." Specific instructions produce specific improvements.

Advanced Techniques

Power-user patterns for complex tasks and consistent results

📌

Session Anchoring

Open a complex session with a detailed context block: "For this entire conversation: I am [role], working on [project], the audience is [audience], and all outputs should follow [style rules]." Every follow-up inherits this context automatically.

🧩

Meta Prompting

Ask ChatGPT to write the prompt for you: "I need to produce [deliverable]. What's the best prompt I should use to get it? Show me the prompt, then run it." Useful when you're not sure how to frame a complex request.

🎲

Parallel Drafts

"Give me three completely different versions of this — vary the tone, structure, and opening significantly." Review all three, take the best elements of each, and ask ChatGPT to synthesise them into one final version.

🔁

Prompt Templates

Once you've found a prompt structure that works well for a recurring task, save it with [PLACEHOLDERS] in square brackets. Build a GPT or keep a notes file of your best templates — they compound in value over time.

The advanced briefing format

For high-stakes outputs, use this 6-part briefing structure. It takes 2 minutes to fill in and typically produces a near-final output on the first attempt:

SectionWhat to write
RoleAct as a [specific expert identity]
GoalThe purpose of this output and what success looks like
AudienceWho will read this, their level of knowledge, what they care about
ContextBackground information, existing material, relevant constraints
DeliverableExactly what to produce: format, sections, length, structure
RulesTone requirements, things to avoid, specific language guidelines
◉
The temperature trick — ask for contrasting options

"Write two versions: one conservative and one bold." The contrast helps you identify exactly what you're looking for and gives you raw material to combine. It's faster than iterating on one version and asking it to be "more bold" — because you're comparing, not imagining.

08

Common Mistakes & Fixes

Why prompts fail — and exactly how to fix them

The output is too generic — it could apply to anyone in any industry
You didn't provide specific context. Add: your industry, company size, audience job title, and the specific goal. Generic prompt = generic output. The fix is always more specificity, not a different tool.
The response is too long and padded with obvious statements
Add hard constraints: "Maximum 200 words." + "Remove every sentence that doesn't add new information." + "No filler openers like 'Great question' or 'Certainly'." These three instructions together cut output length by 40–60% and raise quality significantly.
ChatGPT keeps misunderstanding what I want
Try reverse prompting first: "Before you answer, tell me what you think I'm asking for and what you plan to produce. Wait for my confirmation before proceeding." This surfaces the misalignment before it produces unusable output.
The tone is wrong — too formal, too casual, or too generic
Tone descriptions like "professional" are too vague. Instead: "Write like a direct, experienced senior consultant — no hedging, no filler, no passive voice. Say what you mean." Or paste a short example: "Match the tone of this: [paste sample text]."
I'm getting the same kind of output no matter how I refine it
You've hit the ceiling of that prompt structure. Switch patterns: try few-shot (give examples), try parallel drafts ("give me three very different versions"), or try starting fresh with a completely reframed prompt. Sometimes the initial framing locks you into a response mode you need to break out of.
ChatGPT adds caveats and disclaimers I didn't ask for
Add to your prompt: "Do not add caveats, disclaimers, or recommendations to seek professional advice unless I ask for them." Or: "Respond as if you are writing for a professional audience who understands the limitations of AI-generated content." This removes most unsolicited hedging.

Suggested Prompts

Ready-to-use prompt templates for common scenarios

The full RTCF brief
Role: Act as a [specific expert — e.g. senior copywriter / financial analyst / HR business partner]. Goal: [what this output needs to achieve]. Audience: [who will read this — job title, knowledge level, what they care about]. Context: [relevant background, existing material, key facts]. Deliverable: [exactly what to produce — format, length, structure]. Rules: [tone, what to avoid, style requirements]. Now produce the deliverable.
Chain of thought analysis
I need you to analyse [problem / situation / decision]. Before giving your conclusion, reason through this step by step: (1) What are the key factors at play? (2) What assumptions is this analysis making? (3) What are the strongest arguments for each option? (4) What are the risks and trade-offs? Only after completing this reasoning, give me your conclusion and recommendation in no more than 150 words.
Few-shot style matching
I need you to produce [deliverable type] that matches this exact tone and style. Here are two examples of what I'm looking for: Example 1: [paste example]. Example 2: [paste example]. Study the style carefully — notice the sentence length, vocabulary level, level of formality, and how ideas are structured. Now write [specific new deliverable] in exactly the same style. Topic: [topic].
Self-critique and improve
Review your last response. Identify the three specific weaknesses — things that are too vague, too long, poorly argued, or off-tone. List them explicitly. Then rewrite the response fixing exactly those weaknesses. Do not simply rephrase — make substantive improvements to each identified issue.
Targeted refinement
Keep everything in your last response except [specific section or element]. Rewrite just that part to [specific instruction — e.g. focus on cost savings not features / reduce to 50 words / use a more direct tone / lead with the conclusion]. Leave the rest unchanged.
Parallel options
Give me three completely different versions of [specific element — e.g. the opening paragraph / the subject line / the recommendation]. Version 1: [tone A — e.g. direct and concise]. Version 2: [tone B — e.g. warm and persuasive]. Version 3: [tone C — e.g. bold and challenging]. I'll tell you which direction to develop further.

Your Roadmap

From occasional user to confident prompt architect

Four stages — each builds a skill that makes every future prompt better.

01

Use RTCF on One Real Task

Take something you'd normally send to ChatGPT with a vague prompt. Rebuild it using Role, Task, Context, Format. Compare the outputs. That difference is your benchmark.

02

Learn Two Patterns

Pick Chain of Thought and Critic & Revise. Apply them this week on tasks that matter. These two patterns alone solve 60% of the "ChatGPT isn't good enough" complaints.

03

Build a Prompt Template Library

Every time you craft a prompt that produces great results, save it with [PLACEHOLDERS]. In a month you'll have a personal library that makes every task faster and more consistent.

04

Turn Your Best Prompts into GPTs

Take your three most-used prompt templates and build a custom GPT for each one. Share them with your team. Your prompting expertise becomes a system anyone can use.

💡
Your Quick Win

Open ChatGPT now. Think of a task you completed this week where the output was "fine but not great." Rebuild the prompt using RTCF — add Role, make the Task precise, load the Context, specify the Format. Run it and compare.
