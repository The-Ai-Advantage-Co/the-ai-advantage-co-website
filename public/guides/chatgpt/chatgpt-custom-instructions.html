What is ChatGPT Custom Instructions?
The always-on rules that shape every ChatGPT response — set once, apply everywhere

Custom Instructions are the always-on rules ChatGPT applies to every conversation. Set them once in your account settings and ChatGPT remembers your context, preferences, and conventions across every chat — without you having to re-explain.

Best for: anyone using ChatGPT daily who wants the responses calibrated to their context (role, industry, audience, tone preferences) without retyping the brief every session.

⚙️
Always-On Rules
Set once in settings; applies to every conversation. No re-briefing each session.
📝
Two Boxes
Box 1: what ChatGPT should know about you. Box 2: how it should respond.
🧠
Pairs With Memory
Custom Instructions are static rules; Memory is dynamic learning. Use both.
🔒
Private
Visible only to ChatGPT; not shared with people you message.
✨ The single biggest output-quality lever per user ✨
⚙️ 2026 UPDATE — MEMORY + SOURCES
Custom Instructions are the static layer; Memory is the dynamic layer that learns from your conversations. 2026 added Memory Sources — selectable scopes that decide which past conversations Memory pulls from. Use Custom Instructions for permanent rules; let Memory handle context that evolves.
02
What Custom Instructions Are
Two prompts that shape every response

In ChatGPT settings → Personalization → Custom Instructions, you fill in two boxes:

  • 'What would you like ChatGPT to know about you?' — your context
  • 'How would you like ChatGPT to respond?' — your preferred response style
  • Both apply to every new conversation
  • Both are visible only to ChatGPT — not shared with the people you message

Why most users under-use Custom Instructions

Custom Instructions sit in settings — easy to forget about, easy to leave generic. The users who configure them carefully get dramatically better output across every conversation without any per-prompt effort.

  • Located in Settings → Personalization → Custom Instructions
  • Two free-text boxes; each ~1,500 characters max
  • Applies automatically to every new conversation
  • Editable any time; takes effect immediately
  • Privacy-respecting — content not shared with output recipients
03
Box 1 — Your Context
What to put here

Things that don't change across conversations: your role, industry, audience, the kinds of writing you do, the constraints you operate under.

  • Role — 'I'm a Product Marketing Lead for a B2B SaaS company'
  • Audience — 'Most of my writing goes to engineering teams or executive customers'
  • Constraints — 'We're a regulated industry; outputs must avoid promissory language'
  • Style — 'I prefer plain language, avoid jargon, lead with the headline finding'

✅ DO THIS

  • Lead with role: 'I'm a [role] at [company type]'
  • Specify main audiences: 'My writing goes to [audiences]'
  • List recurring tasks: 'I draft [types of documents] daily'
  • State constraints: 'We're a regulated industry; outputs must avoid promissory language'
  • Mention style preferences: 'I prefer plain language, no jargon'
  • Keep to 5-7 lines — concise beats comprehensive

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Generic descriptions ('I work in business')
  • Listing every possible task — focus on recurring ones
  • Including sensitive credentials (passwords, API keys, financial details)
  • Vague constraints ('be professional') — specify what professional means
  • Letting it grow past one screen — gets ignored at length
  • Forgetting to update when your role/context shifts

The Context box is where you tell ChatGPT who you are and what you do, so it doesn't have to be told every conversation. Treat it like a 5-7 line professional bio — role, industry, audience, what you write, what constraints you operate under, what style you prefer.

The most-skipped element is constraints. Industry regulation, internal style guides, prohibited claims — these are where ChatGPT consistently produces problematic drafts unless explicitly told. Adding 'avoid promissory language; regulated industry' or 'never use exclamation marks; our brand is restrained' makes every response better.

04
Box 2 — Your Response Style
How you want ChatGPT to respond
  • Tone — 'Direct, no throat-clearing'
  • Format — 'Default to bulleted lists for any list of 4+ items'
  • Length — 'Default to under 200 words unless the topic warrants more'
  • Pushback — 'Tell me if I'm asking the wrong question; don't just answer what I asked'
  • Citations — 'When you're uncertain, say so explicitly'

The response-style instructions that produce the biggest lift

Output style determines whether ChatGPT feels like a useful colleague or a generic chatbot. Specific instructions about tone, format, and pushback patterns produce dramatically more useful responses.

  • Tone — 'Direct, no throat-clearing. Skip pleasantries.'
  • Format — 'Default to bulleted lists for any list of 4+ items.'
  • Length — 'Default to under 200 words unless the topic warrants more.'
  • Pushback — 'Tell me if I'm asking the wrong question; don't just answer what I asked.'
  • Uncertainty — 'When you're not confident, say so explicitly.'
  • Examples — 'Include a concrete example with every recommendation.'

Privacy and Sharing
Where instructions live
  • Stored in your ChatGPT account — visible only to you
  • Don't include passwords, API keys, or anything sensitive
  • Custom GPTs you share don't reveal your instructions
  • Enterprise admin controls can restrict what's stored if needed

What's visible and what's not

Custom Instructions are private to your ChatGPT account. They influence responses but don't appear in conversation output, aren't shared with people you message, and aren't visible in Custom GPTs you share.

  • Stored in your ChatGPT account; visible only to you
  • Custom GPTs you share don't reveal your personal Custom Instructions
  • Enterprise admin controls can restrict what's stored if needed
  • Don't include passwords, API keys, financial credentials, or sensitive personal data
  • Editable any time; previous versions not retained

Common Mistakes & Fixes
What goes wrong — and how to avoid it

Most AI workflows that 'don't work' fall into one of these patterns. The fix is almost always sharper context and stricter review, not switching models or tools.

  • Treating ChatGPT Custom Instructions as a finished answer rather than a draft to review
  • Vague prompts that don't specify audience, format, or what you want changed
  • Forgetting to give it context — the file, the brief, the constraints
  • Skipping the verify step — always check numbers, dates, names, and policy claims
  • Using it for tasks where a 30-second human action would be faster

✅ DO THIS

  • Provide context — the file, the brief, the audience, the constraints
  • Iterate; don't just accept the first output
  • Always review numbers, names, dates, and policy claims
  • Treat AI as a fast draft generator, not an answer engine
  • Capture what works so you can replicate it
  • Match the model to the task — fast modes for drafts, reasoning modes for analysis

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Sending AI output without reading it end-to-end
  • Vague prompts with no audience, format, or success criteria
  • Assuming AI can see files or context it hasn't been given
  • Using AI for tasks faster done manually in under a minute
  • Treating numbers, quotes, or citations as verified without checking
  • Defaulting to the slow reasoning mode for every task

Suggested Prompts
Ready-to-use prompts you can adapt to your work

These prompts are designed to land in your real work today. Replace the bracketed placeholders with your actual context — the more specific you make them, the better the output. Treat each one as a starting brief, not a finished script.

CUSTOM INSTRUCTIONS DRAFT
I'm a [role] at [company type]. My main audiences are [list]. The kinds of writing I do are [list]. The constraints I work under are [list]. I prefer responses that are [tone/format/length] and don't include [things to avoid]. Generate a draft for both Custom Instructions boxes.
QUARTERLY REVIEW
Here are my current Custom Instructions. Audit them: anything stale, anything missing, anything ChatGPT might over-apply? Suggest specific edits.
ROLE SHIFT UPDATE
I just moved from [old role] to [new role]. My audiences and constraints are now [new]. Rewrite my Custom Instructions for the new context — keep what still applies, replace what doesn't.
