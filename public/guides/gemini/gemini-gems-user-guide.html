Gemini Gems

Create personalised AI assistants for specific tasks — with custom instructions, personas, and built-in knowledge that persist across every conversation.

1

What are Gems?

Gems are customised versions of Gemini you build yourself. Each Gem has its own name, instructions, persona, and optionally, files or knowledge you upload. When you start a conversation with a Gem, it already knows its role, your preferences, and any context you've given it — without you having to explain again.

Think of a Gem as a specialist on your team. You might have a Gem for writing, a different Gem for code review, another for brainstorming, and one for customer support scripts. Each is tuned for its purpose and ready instantly.

🎭 Custom persona

Give the Gem a role, tone, and personality. It will maintain that character across all conversations.

📋 Persistent instructions

Set rules that apply to every response — formatting, length, audience, approach.

📂 Embedded knowledge

Upload files, documents, or paste content that the Gem always has access to.

🔁 Reusable workflows

Automate your most-repeated AI tasks into a one-click Gem conversation.

Where to find Gems:

Gems are available in Gemini Advanced. Access them from the left sidebar of gemini.google.com or via the Gems Manager at gemini.google.com/gems.

Creating Your First Gem

1

Open Gems Manager

Go to gemini.google.com and click "Gems" in the left sidebar, then click "Create a Gem" or the "+" button.

2

Name your Gem

Give it a clear, descriptive name: "Email Drafter", "Code Reviewer", "Marketing Strategist". You'll see this in the sidebar.

3

Write the instructions

This is the core of your Gem — the persistent prompt that shapes every conversation. Be specific about role, tone, and behaviour.

4

Upload files (optional)

Upload documents, style guides, brand guidelines, or reference material the Gem should always know about.

5

Save and test

Click Save, then open the Gem and send a few test messages. Refine the instructions based on how it responds.

Google also provides pre-built Gems:

Gemini includes a library of ready-made Gems for common tasks like "Learning Coach," "Brainstorm Partner," and "Writing Editor." Browse these first — they may cover your use case already, and they make great starting templates.

3

Writing Instructions

The instructions field is the most important part of a Gem. Well-written instructions produce consistently useful, on-brand responses. Vague instructions produce inconsistent output.

Example: Email Drafter Gem Instructions
You are a professional email writing assistant for a B2B software company.

Your role: Draft clear, concise, professional emails on behalf of the user.

Tone: Warm but professional. Avoid jargon. Use plain English. Never use phrases like "Hope this finds you well."

Format: Subject line first, then email body. Keep emails under 200 words unless the user specifies otherwise.

Always ask: If the user doesn't provide the recipient's context or relationship, ask one clarifying question before drafting.

Instruction components that work best

ComponentWhat to IncludeExample
Role definitionWho the Gem is and what it does"You are a senior data analyst specialising in marketing metrics."
Tone and voiceHow it should communicate"Use a direct, practical tone. Avoid fluff. Use bullet points for lists."
Format rulesHow responses should be structured"Always start with a summary, then detail. Use headers for long responses."
ConstraintsWhat it should NOT do"Never make up data. If unsure, say so and suggest how to find out."
Clarifying behaviourWhen to ask questions vs proceed"If the brief is ambiguous, ask one clarifying question before starting."

Gem Personas & Roles

Personas make Gems dramatically more useful. When Gemini is playing a specific, well-defined role, its responses are more focused, more consistent, and more useful than general-purpose Gemini.

Persona types that work well

👔 Professional role

"You are a senior HR consultant specialising in performance management frameworks." — gives expertise and perspective.

🎓 Teacher / coach

"You are a patient Socratic tutor who helps people learn by asking questions rather than giving answers." — shapes the interaction style.

✍️ Editor / critic

"You are a professional copyeditor with high standards for clarity and concision. Be direct about what needs improving." — enables honest feedback.

🔎 Analyst

"You are a business analyst who thinks in frameworks, quantifies where possible, and always identifies assumptions." — shapes analytical output.

Add a perspective to your persona:

Rather than just a job title, add a point of view: "You are a UX designer who believes that most software is over-engineered and always advocates for simplicity." The perspective makes responses more distinctive and useful.

Audience awareness

Tell your Gem who it's talking to: "The user is always a non-technical business owner — never assume technical knowledge." This one instruction prevents Gemini from defaulting to technical jargon when you need plain-English explanations.

Context & Knowledge

Gems can be given persistent knowledge — information they always have access to, regardless of what you ask in a given conversation. This is what separates a generic Gemini conversation from a truly personalised AI assistant.

What to upload as Gem knowledge

  • Brand guidelines — tone of voice, vocabulary, dos and don'ts
  • Company information — what you do, who your customers are, your positioning
  • Product documentation — features, pricing, FAQs for a customer-support Gem
  • Previous work — sample emails, past reports, as style reference
  • Research reports — from Deep Research sessions, used to create a topic-expert Gem
Paste vs upload:

For shorter knowledge (under a few thousand words), pasting directly into the instructions field is often more reliable than uploading a file — it's immediately visible and easier to edit later.

Knowledge limitations

Gemini Gems have a context limit. Very large uploads may be truncated. For extensive knowledge bases (full product documentation, multiple lengthy reports), consider using NotebookLM instead — it's designed for large document corpora.

Sensitive information:

Don't upload files containing passwords, PII, or confidential financial data into Gems. Gem knowledge is stored in your Google account but review the Google privacy policy for how this data is handled.

Managing Your Library

Over time you'll build a library of Gems — each tuned for a specific purpose. Good library management keeps your Gems useful and easy to find.

Naming conventions

Use a consistent naming pattern: [Role/Function] — [Audience/Context]. Examples:

  • "Email Drafter — Client-Facing"
  • "Code Reviewer — Python"
  • "Blog Writer — AI & Tech Topics"
  • "Meeting Coach — Executive Presentations"

When to update a Gem

TriggerWhat to Update
Gem gives consistently off-tone responsesRefine the tone and voice instructions; add a "do not" example
Your role or product changesUpdate the company/context information in the knowledge section
You find yourself adding the same context every sessionThat context belongs in the Gem instructions permanently
Gem asks for information it should already knowAdd that information to the knowledge section
Duplicate before you experiment:

Before making major changes to a working Gem, duplicate it first (via the Gems Manager menu). This way you can experiment without losing a version that's already working well.

Advanced Techniques

The "start with a question" instruction

Add to your Gem instructions: "When the user starts a conversation, always ask one clarifying question before responding." This prevents Gems from making assumptions and produces better first drafts.

Build a Gem from a Deep Research report

Run a Deep Research session on a topic. Export to Google Docs. Then create a Gem and paste the key findings into the instructions as "background knowledge." The Gem becomes a subject-matter expert on that specific topic.

Chain Gems in a workflow

Use Gem A to create a first draft → copy output → use Gem B (your editor Gem) to refine it → copy to Gem C (your formatter Gem) to apply final structure. Each Gem does one thing well.

Response format templates

Include a desired output template directly in the instructions:

Template instruction example:

Always structure your analysis responses as:
**Situation:** [2 sentences max]
**Key finding:** [1 sentence]
**Options:** [3 bullet points]
**Recommendation:** [1 sentence with reasoning]

Use Gems for recurring client work

Create a separate Gem for each major client — loaded with their brand guidelines, product info, and communication preferences. Consistency across months of work becomes automatic.

Common Mistakes & Fixes

MistakeWhy It HappensFix
Instructions too vague"Be helpful and professional" applies to everything — gives Gemini no specific guidanceAdd role, tone, format, constraints, and audience as separate explicit items
Never testing after setupSaving a Gem without running test conversationsSend 5 different test prompts after setup; refine instructions based on responses
Uploading too muchTrying to put entire company wikis into one GemKeep knowledge focused on what the Gem actually needs. Use NotebookLM for large corpora
One Gem for everythingBuilding one "super Gem" instead of specialised onesSeparate Gems by task — a writing Gem and an analysis Gem will each outperform a combined one
Not updating after useTreating Gems as set-and-forgetAfter each session, note anything the Gem got wrong and update instructions accordingly
Forgetting which Gem to useLibrary grows without a clear naming systemUse a consistent [Role]—[Context] naming convention from the start

Suggested Prompts

Instruction Generator
"Help me write Gem instructions for a Gemini Gem that will act as a [role] for [audience]. It should always [key behaviour]. The tone should be [tone description]. It should never [constraint]. Here's an example of a good response it should give: [example]."
Gem Audit
"Review these Gem instructions and identify: (1) anything vague or ambiguous that could produce inconsistent responses, (2) missing constraints that could lead to unhelpful outputs, (3) improvements to the structure or clarity. Here are the instructions: [paste instructions]"
Knowledge Summary
"Condense the following document into a concise knowledge summary suitable for pasting into a Gem's instructions. Keep the most important facts, remove examples that won't generalise, and format it as labelled sections: [paste document]"
Writing Assistant
"[Start a conversation with your writing Gem] Here's a first draft I need you to review: [paste draft]. Focus on: clarity, concision, and whether the opening paragraph hooks the reader. Give me specific line-level suggestions."
Strategy Gem
"[Start a conversation with your strategy Gem] I'm trying to decide between these two options: [Option A] and [Option B]. The key factors are [factors]. Using your framework, what would you recommend and why? What am I potentially missing?"
Daily Briefing Gem
"[Start a conversation with a morning briefing Gem] Today is [day/date]. I have these items on my agenda: [list]. My priorities for this week are [priorities]. Give me a structured start to my day: what to focus on first and any potential blockers to flag."

See It In Action

A content marketer builds a "Brand Voice Editor" Gem with the company's style guide embedded.

Gemini Gems — Brand Voice Editor
Gem Instructions (summary)
You are a brand editor for Acme Corp. Tone: confident, plain-spoken, never corporate-speak. Never use: "leverage," "synergy," "ecosystem." Always use active voice. Keep sentences under 20 words. [Brand guide uploaded as PDF]
"Edit this paragraph for our brand voice: 'We leverage our robust ecosystem of integrated solutions to synergise client outcomes across all verticals.'"
Brand Voice Editor
Revised: "Our tools work together — so your clients get better results, faster."

Changes: removed "leverage," "ecosystem," "synergise," "verticals." Replaced corporate noun stack with concrete benefit. Cut from 14 words to 10.

Same Gem, every piece of copy — consistent brand voice without briefing a human editor each time.

Your Roadmap

01

Use a pre-built Gem

Start with Google's ready-made Gems. Try "Writing Editor" or "Learning Coach" to understand the format.

02

Build your first Gem

Pick your most repeated AI task. Write specific instructions with role, tone, format, and constraints.

03

Add knowledge

Upload a brand guide, style doc, or company overview. Test whether the Gem uses it correctly.

04

Build a Gem library

Create 3–5 specialised Gems for your most common workflows. Refine each based on real use.
