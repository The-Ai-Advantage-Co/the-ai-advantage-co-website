What is NotebookLM?

NotebookLM is Google's AI-powered research and knowledge management tool. Unlike general Gemini, NotebookLM is entirely grounded in the sources you provide — it only answers questions based on the documents, files, and links you've added to your notebook. It cannot hallucinate information from outside your sources.

This makes it ideal for situations where accuracy and source traceability matter: legal document review, academic research, product documentation, policy analysis, and competitive intelligence.

📚 Source grounding

All answers are based only on your uploaded documents — with citations linking to the exact passage.

❓ Q&A across sources

Ask questions that synthesise information across multiple documents simultaneously.

🎙️ Audio Overview

Generate a podcast-style discussion of your sources — two AI hosts explain the key ideas conversationally.

📝 Smart notes

Create notes that reference specific passages, generate study guides, and build structured summaries.

Access:

NotebookLM is free at notebooklm.google.com — no Gemini Advanced subscription required. NotebookLM Plus (enhanced features) is available with Google One AI Premium or Workspace plans.

Up to 50 sources per notebook:

Each notebook supports up to 50 sources. For very large projects, consider multiple notebooks organised by topic — then use Gemini chat with Drive extension to synthesise across notebooks.

Source quality guidance

NotebookLM is only as good as its sources. Add authoritative, complete sources — not summaries of summaries. For research topics, include original papers, not just news articles about them. For legal topics, include the actual documents, not just interpretations.

Asking Questions

The Q&A interface is NotebookLM's core feature. Ask questions in plain language and get answers grounded in your sources, with inline citations linking directly to the source passage.

Question types that work well

  • Factual retrieval: "What does the contract say about payment terms?"
  • Cross-source synthesis: "What do all three research papers agree on regarding [topic]?"
  • Comparison: "How does Company A's approach differ from Company B's based on these reports?"
  • Gap identification: "What does the document NOT cover that I might need to address?"
  • Contradictions: "Are there any contradictions between the findings in these sources?"
  • Interpretation: "Based on this policy document, would [scenario] be permitted?"
Follow-up questions:

NotebookLM supports multi-turn conversation. After an answer, ask follow-up questions: "Can you expand on the third point?" or "Which source makes this claim most strongly?" or "What's the evidence for and against this position?"

Suggested questions

When you first load sources, NotebookLM generates suggested questions based on the content. These are excellent starting points — often surfacing angles you hadn't considered asking.

Notes & Summaries

NotebookLM's Notes panel allows you to create structured notes that are grounded in your sources. Unlike a regular note-taking app, these notes can reference and pull directly from your source material.

Auto-generated note types

📋 Document summary

Click any source → "Generate summary" for a concise overview with key points and structure.

📖 Study guide

From the notes panel: "Generate study guide" — creates key concepts, vocabulary, and quiz questions.

📊 Briefing doc

"Generate briefing doc" — executive-style summary of all sources, structured for fast consumption.

📅 Timeline

"Generate timeline" — extracts and orders chronological events mentioned across all sources.

Creating manual notes

You can also write notes manually in the Notes panel — and then ask NotebookLM to expand, cite, or challenge them based on your sources. This is powerful for drafting arguments: write your claim, then ask "What evidence in my sources supports or contradicts this?"

Audio Overview

Audio Overview is one of NotebookLM's most distinctive features — it generates a podcast-style conversation between two AI hosts who discuss the key ideas from your sources. The result sounds like a real podcast episode.

Generating an Audio Overview

1

Open the notebook

Ensure your sources are loaded. Click "Audio Overview" in the right panel or the top toolbar.

2

Click Generate

NotebookLM processes all sources and generates a 10–20 minute podcast-style discussion. Generation takes 1–3 minutes.

3

Listen and download

Play directly in the browser or download the audio file. Share with colleagues who prefer audio learning.

What Audio Overview is best for:

Long research reports (listening while commuting), onboarding new team members to a topic, making academic papers accessible to non-experts, creating audio summaries of company policies or product documentation. It's NOT a substitute for reading detailed technical or legal content.

Citation & Source Grounding

Citation grounding is what makes NotebookLM fundamentally different from general AI chat. Every claim in a NotebookLM response is linked to a specific passage in a specific source — you can click through and verify.

How citations work

When NotebookLM answers a question, each sentence or claim includes a citation marker (like ¹ or [1]). Clicking the marker opens the source panel showing the exact paragraph where the information came from, with the relevant passage highlighted.

Why this matters:

Unlike standard AI tools that generate plausible-sounding content, NotebookLM is constrained to your sources. If information isn't in your sources, it will tell you — rather than inventing an answer. This makes it suitable for high-stakes research and compliance work.

When NotebookLM says "I don't know"

If you ask a question that can't be answered from your sources, NotebookLM will say so — rather than guessing. This is a feature, not a limitation. It tells you exactly where your source coverage has gaps, so you can add more relevant sources.

Citation for professional use

For academic or professional work, you can click each citation to view the original passage and copy it properly for your bibliography or footnotes. NotebookLM does the finding; you verify and cite correctly.

Advanced Techniques

Build a personal knowledge base

Create topic notebooks that grow over time: "AI in Healthcare," "Competitor Intelligence," "Legal Reference." Add new sources as you find them. Over months, the notebook becomes a comprehensive, queryable knowledge base on a topic.

Use Deep Research → NotebookLM pipeline

Run a Deep Research session in Gemini. Export the report to Google Docs. Add that Doc to a NotebookLM notebook alongside the original sources. Now you have both the raw material and the synthesised report — query both simultaneously.

Multi-source comparative analysis

Add competing viewpoints as separate sources: "What do the bullish and bearish sources each argue about [topic]? Where do they agree and disagree?" NotebookLM synthesises across the divide with citations from both sides.

Onboarding notebooks

Create a "New Employee" notebook with: company handbook, product documentation, key processes, team structure docs, and relevant past project reports. New team members can ask questions and get grounded, cited answers — dramatically reducing time-to-productivity.

Ask NotebookLM to challenge you:

After reading sources, ask: "What are the strongest counterarguments to the position taken in these documents?" or "What assumptions does this analysis rely on that could be wrong?" This stress-tests your understanding before you act on the research.

Suggested Prompts

Cross-Source Synthesis
"Across all the sources in this notebook, what are the 5 most important findings or conclusions? For each, tell me which sources support it and whether any sources contradict or qualify it."
Gap Analysis
"I'm trying to understand [topic]. Based on my sources, what important questions about this topic are NOT answered by any of the documents I've added? What gaps in my research should I address?"
Conflict Detection
"Review all sources and identify: (1) any claims made by one source that are contradicted by another, (2) any areas where sources agree but with different data or figures, (3) any assumptions made in one source that another source would challenge."
Study Guide
"Create a study guide for someone learning [topic] from scratch using only these sources. Include: key concepts and definitions, the most important facts, 5 quiz questions with answers, and a recommended reading order for the sources."
Executive Brief
"Generate a one-page executive brief from all sources. Format: context (2 sentences), key findings (5 bullets with citations), strategic implications (3 bullets), and recommended actions (3 bullets). Suitable for a C-suite audience with limited time."
FAQ Generation
"Based on these sources, what are the 10 most common questions someone would ask about [topic]? For each question, provide a concise answer grounded in the sources, with the citation. Format as a FAQ document."
