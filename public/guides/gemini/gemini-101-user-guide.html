What is Gemini?

Google's AI — and what makes it different

Gemini is Google's AI assistant, built on their most capable large language models. Like ChatGPT or Claude, you talk to it in natural language and it responds, reasons, writes, and analyses. But Gemini's distinctive strengths come from its deep Google integration: it's grounded in real-time web search by default, connected to Gmail, Docs, Drive, Calendar, and YouTube, and designed to work across your entire Google ecosystem.

Where other AI tools require you to paste in information, Gemini can reach into your Google account directly — summarising your emails, checking your calendar, referencing your Drive documents, and searching the web — all in a single conversation.

🌐

Web-Grounded by Default

Gemini searches the live web automatically when you need current information. Responses include source citations so you can verify — no add-on required.

📧

Google Workspace Integration

Connects to Gmail, Google Docs, Drive, Calendar, Meet, and Sheets. Ask it to summarise your inbox, find a file, or draft a document — without leaving Gemini.

🔬

Deep Research Mode

Gemini Advanced's Deep Research conducts multi-source, multi-step research on complex topics and delivers a structured report with citations in minutes.

🖼️

Multimodal — See, Hear, Generate

Understands images, audio, video, and documents. Generates images via Imagen. Available on mobile for real-time visual conversations using your camera.

Starting Conversations

How to prompt Gemini effectively from day one

Gemini responds to natural language — you don't need to learn special syntax. But the quality of what you get out is directly proportional to the quality of what you put in. A vague question gets a general answer. A specific, contextual prompt gets something you can actually use.

The Gemini prompt formula
🎯

Be specific about the task

"Summarise the main arguments for and against remote work for a finance team in a UK SME" is infinitely more useful than "tell me about remote work".

📋

Provide context

Tell Gemini who you are, who the audience is, and what you're trying to achieve. It uses this to calibrate tone, depth, and relevance.

📐

Specify the format

Gemini defaults to paragraphs. If you want a table, bullet list, numbered steps, or a specific structure — say so. Format specifications transform how usable the output is.

🔁

Iterate in conversation

Follow up, refine, redirect. "Make this more concise", "add a section on risks", "rewrite the second paragraph in plain English". Each exchange improves the output.

Gemini-specific prompt advantages
1

Ask for web-grounded answers

Gemini searches the web automatically, but you can direct it: "Based on the latest news, what are the current interest rate expectations for the UK?" You'll get cited, current information — not AI knowledge that may be months old.

2

Reference your Google content

With Workspace extensions enabled: "Summarise my emails about the Johnson account from this week" or "Find the proposal I wrote last month in Drive". Gemini retrieves and works with your actual content.

3

Use the microphone for longer context

On mobile, tap the microphone to speak your prompt. For complex requests with lots of context — project backgrounds, multi-part questions — speaking is often faster and more natural than typing.

4

Upload images, files, or screenshots

Tap the attachment icon to upload a document, photo, or screenshot. "Here's a chart from our last board meeting — what are the three most important trends?" Gemini reads it and responds with analysis.

04

Deep Research Mode

Multi-source research in minutes — Gemini's standout capability

Deep Research is Gemini Advanced's most powerful feature. You give it a complex research question, it builds a research plan, searches dozens of sources across the web, synthesises the findings, and delivers a structured, cited report — in minutes rather than hours.

How Deep Research works
1

Enter a research question

Click the "Deep Research" button in Gemini Advanced and type your question. Be specific about what you need and what format you want the output in.

2

Review and approve the research plan

Gemini shows you its planned approach — what sub-questions it will research, what areas it will cover. You can edit the plan before it starts. This step is important for complex topics.

3

Gemini researches across the web

It conducts iterative web searches, reads sources, cross-references findings, and builds the answer progressively — you can watch the process. Typically takes 2–5 minutes.

4

Receive a structured, cited report

The output is a formatted report with headings, key findings, and inline citations. You can export it to Google Docs for further editing, or continue asking questions in the same conversation.

What Deep Research is best for
📊

Market Research

Competitive landscape, market sizing, industry trends — synthesised from current sources with citations.

⚖️

Policy & Regulatory Review

Current regulatory landscape, upcoming changes, compliance requirements — pulled from official and authoritative sources.

🔬

Technology Assessment

What's the current state of a technology? What are the competing approaches? What do practitioners say? Deep Research synthesises it all.

🏢

Company & Sector Due Diligence

Background on a company, sector, or individual — news, filings, commentary — for meetings, pitches, or investment review.

📋

Background Briefs

Prepare for a meeting, conference, or client with a structured brief on the topic, organisation, or person you're meeting.

📰

News & Trend Analysis

What's happening right now in a sector? What are the emerging themes? Synthesised from current news and analysis.

⚠️
Always verify key facts from Deep Research

Deep Research is powerful but not infallible. For decisions that matter — financial, legal, medical, strategic — treat the output as a first-pass synthesis that still requires expert review. Check the cited sources for the most critical claims before relying on them.

Extensions & Google Workspace

Connecting Gemini to your Google account and apps

Extensions let Gemini reach into your Google services — with your permission — to retrieve and work with real content from your accounts. Once enabled, you can ask Gemini about your actual emails, documents, calendar events, and more.

Enabling extensions
1

Open Gemini settings

In Gemini, click the Extensions icon (puzzle piece) in the left panel, or go to Settings → Extensions.

2

Enable the extensions you want

Toggle on Gmail, Drive, Calendar, YouTube, and any others. Each extension shows exactly what data it will access — read the permissions carefully.

3

Use the @ symbol to call specific extensions

Type @Gmail, @Drive, or @Calendar in your prompt to direct Gemini to use that specific extension. E.g. "@Gmail summarise my unread messages from today."

⚠️
Workplace accounts — check with IT first

If you're using a Google Workspace account provided by your employer, extension availability may be controlled by your admin. Some organisations restrict which Google services AI can access. Check your organisation's AI usage policy before enabling extensions on a work account.

Gems — Custom AI Assistants

Pre-configured specialist assistants built for specific jobs

Gems are Gemini's equivalent of ChatGPT's GPTs — custom AI assistants with specific instructions, personalities, and focus areas pre-loaded. Instead of starting every conversation from scratch, you open the right Gem and it's already configured for its job.

📝

Pre-built Gems

Google provides ready-made Gems for common tasks: Brainstormer, Career Guide, Coding Partner, Learning Coach, Writing Editor. Available immediately in Gemini Advanced.

🛠️

Custom Gems

Build your own: give it a name, write its instructions, set its tone and scope. Share it with your team. A well-built Gem for a recurring task saves prompt-writing time every day.

Building a Gem — step by step
1

Open Gem Manager

In the Gemini left panel, click "Gem Manager" → "New Gem". Give it a name that reflects its role.

2

Write the instructions

Describe the role, the tone, what it should focus on, and what it should avoid. Be specific. "You are a proposal writing assistant for a UK management consultancy. Always write in a professional, confident, and concise style. Focus only on proposal content — decline requests unrelated to proposals."

3

Test it

The right panel shows a live preview. Try common requests you'd send to this Gem and check the quality of the response. Adjust instructions based on what you see.

4

Save and access from the sidebar

Your Gem appears in the left panel for quick access. Currently Gems are personal (not shareable with teams) — this may change as the feature develops.

💡
Start with the Learning Coach Gem

Google's pre-built Learning Coach Gem is excellent for upskilling — it adapts explanations to your level, asks questions to check understanding, and creates personalised learning plans. If you're onboarding staff to new tools or processes, it's an immediate win.

Common Mistakes & Fixes

Why Gemini underdelivers — and how to fix it

Gemini gives outdated information even though it has web access
Gemini searches the web automatically, but it may draw on training data for some topics. For time-sensitive information, be explicit: "Search the web for the current [topic] and only use information from the last 6 months." Check the sources Gemini cites — the dates are shown alongside each citation.
Extensions aren't finding my emails / documents
Check that the relevant extension is enabled (Gemini → Extensions). Then be more specific in your prompt — include dates, project names, sender names. If it still fails, try pasting the content directly rather than asking Gemini to retrieve it. Also verify your account has granted the necessary permissions to Gemini.
Deep Research output is too general — not specific enough to my topic
Narrow the research question and edit the research plan before it starts. Instead of "Research the UK housing market", try "Research the impact of interest rate changes on UK residential property transactions in 2024–2025, focusing on the £300K–£500K segment in London." The research plan step is your opportunity to steer the scope.
Gemini's responses are too long and hard to scan
Add format instructions: "Respond in bullet points only, maximum 5 points" or "Give me a table" or "Answer in under 100 words." Gemini respects format constraints well. You can also follow up with "make this more concise" or "give me the executive summary version."
Image generation doesn't match what I described
Be more precise about: subject, style, composition, and what to avoid. "A professional photo of a modern open-plan office with natural light, people collaborating at standing desks, warm tones, no clutter" works better than "office photo." Iterate — describe what's wrong with the first result and ask for adjustments.

Suggested Prompts

Ready-to-use prompts for Gemini's core capabilities

Deep Research brief
Research [specific topic] for me. I need: (1) a clear overview of the current situation, (2) the 3–5 most important recent developments, (3) key players or organisations, (4) implications for [my context — industry/role]. Use only sources from the last 12 months. Structure the output as a briefing document with headings. Export to Google Docs when done.
Competitive landscape analysis
Search the web for a competitive analysis of [company or product] vs its top 3 competitors. For each competitor include: positioning, key strengths, known weaknesses, recent news or changes, and approximate market share if available. Format as a comparison table followed by a brief written summary. Cite your sources.
Meeting prep brief
I'm meeting with [person/organisation] on [date] about [topic]. Search the web and use my calendar and emails (@Calendar @Gmail) to prepare me. I need: background on who I'm meeting and their current priorities, recent news about their organisation, the key topics we need to cover, and three smart questions I could ask to advance the relationship.
Inbox triage
@Gmail Look at my emails from the last 3 days. Give me: (1) a list of anything requiring a decision or response from me, with the sender and urgency, (2) anything that looks like it needs action this week, (3) anything I can safely ignore or archive. Group by priority: urgent / this week / FYI.
Document summary from Drive
@Drive Find [document name or description] in my Drive. Give me: (1) what the document is for and its current status, (2) the key decisions or recommendations it contains, (3) anything that looks incomplete or needs updating, (4) the three most important things someone needs to know before reading it. Keep your summary under 250 words.
Week planning from calendar
@Calendar Show me everything I have scheduled for the next 5 working days. For each commitment: tell me what preparation might be needed, flag any potential conflicts or back-to-back issues, and identify any gaps I could use for focused work. Then suggest how I should prioritise my time this week based on what's scheduled.

See It In Action

A complete Gemini workflow — research to draft to Google Docs

Below is a real Gemini session: a consultant needs a competitive briefing before a pitch meeting. Watch how Gemini pulls web research and Workspace data into a structured, exportable output.

Your prompt
Using Deep Research: prepare me a competitive briefing on Salesforce vs HubSpot for a pitch to a UK-based B2B SaaS company with 50–200 employees. Focus on: which platform typically suits this company size, pricing differences, integration ecosystems, and what sales teams most commonly say about each. Also check @Gmail for any previous correspondence with this client so I have context.
Gemini Advanced · Deep Research · @Gmail

I found 3 recent emails with this client (last contact: 14 days ago, discussing CRM migration timelines). Here's your competitive briefing:

Salesforce vs HubSpot for UK SMB B2B SaaS (50–200 employees)

Fit for company size: HubSpot is the more natural fit at 50–200 employees. Its Sales Hub is designed for growing teams — less admin overhead, faster onboarding, and a gentler learning curve. Salesforce at this size typically requires dedicated admin resource and CRM consultancy investment that many SMBs underestimate.

Pricing: HubSpot Sales Hub Professional starts at ~£420/mo (5 users). Salesforce Sales Cloud Professional runs ~£600/mo (5 users) before typical implementation and admin costs. Total cost of ownership gap widens significantly at implementation.

Integration ecosystem: Both integrate with the major tools, but Salesforce's AppExchange (7,000+ apps) is substantially larger. HubSpot's marketplace is more curated — fewer options, but simpler to implement without a developer.

What sales teams say: HubSpot users consistently cite ease of use and built-in marketing/sales alignment. Salesforce users cite power and customisability, but also complexity. For a 50–200 person team without a dedicated RevOps function, this is a material consideration.

Your Roadmap

From first prompt to a fully integrated Gemini workflow

Four stages — each one unlocks meaningfully more value than the last.

01

First Conversation

Ask Gemini a real question from your work this week. Try a research question — something you'd normally spend 30 minutes googling. Evaluate the result honestly.

02

Connect Your Workspace

Enable Gmail and Drive extensions. Ask Gemini to summarise your inbox or find a document. Experience what it feels like when the AI knows your actual work.

03

Take a topic you'd normally spend 2 hours researching. Run it through Deep Research. Export to Google Docs. Use the time you saved to review and improve the output.

04

Build a Gem

Identify your most-repeated task. Build a Gem for it. Open it every time you do that task. Measure how much time it saves over four weeks.
