Brand Consistency
On-brand images at scale

Define a visual style brief once. Reuse it across every prompt. For ongoing brand work, encode in a Gem.

🎨 THE BRAND BRIEF REUSABLE PATTERN
Tone, palette (primary + 2 accents), illustration style, composition rules, what to never include. 5 sentences. Reuse on every image — keeps a hundred different images visually consistent.
🎨 THE REUSABLE BRAND BRIEF
Define brand style once: tone, palette, illustration style, composition rules, things to avoid. 5 sentences. Apply to every image prompt by pasting alongside specific description. For ongoing work, encode in a Gem so every team-generated image inherits the brand.

The reusable brand brief structure

  • Tone — warm/cool, soft/sharp, calm/energetic
  • Palette — primary + 2 accents (specific hex codes)
  • Illustration style — photorealistic / vector / 3D / watercolor / specific
  • Composition — centred / rule-of-thirds / asymmetric / negative space rules
  • What to avoid — specific elements that don't fit (text, logos, certain colors)

Slides Image Workflow
From deck brief to embedded visuals

Build the deck structure first with Gemini. Then for each slide that needs an image, prompt Imagen with: 'For the slide titled [title], generate an image. Style: [brand brief]. Subject: [specific].' Iterate until each slide has the right visual.

  • Build deck structure first — text, then images
  • Brand brief upfront — apply to every slide's image
  • Slide-specific prompts — short, focused
  • Iterate before moving on — fix each slide's image then advance

Imagen integration in Slides is the highest-leverage image generation pattern for most users. Build the deck structure first with Gemini (text content, slide order). Then for each slide that needs an image, prompt Imagen with the slide title + brand brief. The deck-building workflow drops from hours to minutes.

The brand brief is the part most users skip. Without it, every slide's image looks slightly different — same model, similar prompts, but visual style drifts. With a consistent brand brief applied to every slide's image prompt, the deck reads cohesively.

Video Generation
Pro and Ultra short-form video

Gemini's video generation produces short clips (5-15 seconds) from text prompts. Quality is dramatically improved in 2026 — competitive with leading models. Best for social, ad creative, and motion accents on slides.

  • Keep clips short — 5-10 seconds for social
  • Specify motion — 'slow pan', 'subject moves left to right'
  • Subject placement — 'centred subject with negative space top-left'
  • Iterate before scaling — costs and time per clip add up

Short-form video patterns

Gemini's video generation produces short clips (5-15 seconds) on Pro/Ultra. Quality dramatically improved in 2026 — competitive with leading models. Best uses: social, ad creative, motion accents on slides.

  • Keep clips short — 5-10 seconds for social
  • Specify motion — 'slow pan', 'subject moves left to right'
  • Subject placement — 'centred subject with negative space top-left'
  • Iterate before scaling — costs and time per clip add up
  • Multiple clips for B-roll — generate variations of same scene
  • Stills first, then video — verify the look in still form before committing to motion

What Imagen Isn't Good At
Honest limits
  • Photo-real product shots — still glitches on product details
  • Recognisable people — restricted for named public figures
  • Highly technical diagrams — engineering schematics, anatomical accuracy
  • Brand logos — never generate; not defensible
  • Anything requiring text accuracy in fine detail

✅ DO THIS

  • Use Imagen for editorial imagery, slide illustrations, social headers
  • Use Imagen for product mockup illustrations (not photo-real product shots)
  • Use Imagen for infographics with text — 2026 text rendering is reliable
  • Use Imagen for hero images on landing pages
  • Combine with brand brief for consistency at volume
  • Iterate via 'same image but with [change]'

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Photo-real product photography — fingers, faces, product details glitch
  • Recognisable real people for commercial use — restricted by policy
  • Highly technical diagrams (engineering schematics, anatomical accuracy)
  • Brand logos — never let AI generate; not defensible
  • Anything requiring text accuracy in fine detail (small captions, dense labels)
  • Customer-facing visuals without human review pass

Rights and Disclosure
Who owns the output
  • Google's terms — commercial rights generally granted for Pro/Ultra subscribers
  • Training data risk — small risk of resemblance to copyrighted training images
  • Disclosure — be honest about AI-generated visuals where audiences expect human work
  • Public figures — restricted; don't generate likenesses commercially
🚫 RESTRICTIONS WORTH KNOWING
Don't generate named real people for commercial use — restricted by policy and often by jurisdiction. Don't generate brand logos — not defensible if challenged. Watermark or disclose AI-generated images where audiences expect human work (fine art, illustrations, regulated industries).

Common Mistakes & Fixes
What goes wrong — and how to avoid it

Most AI workflows that 'don't work' fall into one of these patterns. The fix is almost always sharper context and stricter review, not switching models or tools.

  • Treating Gemini for Image Generation and Creative Work as a finished answer rather than a draft to review
  • Vague prompts that don't specify audience, format, or what you want changed
  • Forgetting to give it context — the file, the brief, the constraints
  • Skipping the verify step — always check numbers, dates, names, and policy claims
  • Using it for tasks where a 30-second human action would be faster

✅ DO THIS

  • Provide context — the file, the brief, the audience, the constraints
  • Iterate; don't just accept the first output
  • Always review numbers, names, dates, and policy claims
  • Treat AI as a fast draft generator, not an answer engine
  • Capture what works so you can replicate it
  • Match the model to the task — fast modes for drafts, reasoning modes for analysis

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Sending AI output without reading it end-to-end
  • Vague prompts with no audience, format, or success criteria
  • Assuming AI can see files or context it hasn't been given
  • Using AI for tasks faster done manually in under a minute
  • Treating numbers, quotes, or citations as verified without checking
  • Defaulting to the slow reasoning mode for every task

Suggested Prompts
Ready-to-use prompts you can adapt to your work

These prompts are designed to land in your real work today. Replace the bracketed placeholders with your actual context — the more specific you make them, the better the output. Treat each one as a starting brief, not a finished script.

BRAND IMAGE
Generate an image. Subject: [specific]. Style: photorealistic editorial, warm natural lighting. Palette: cream, deep teal, soft gold accent. Composition: centred subject, negative space top-left, calm focused mood. Avoid: text, logos.
SLIDES IMAGE BATCH
For this deck on [topic], generate an image per slide. Brand brief: [paste]. Slide titles: [list]. For each, propose 2-3 specific image briefs that fit the slide's content, in the brand style.
VIDEO CLIP
Generate a 6-second video clip. Subject: [specific]. Motion: [direction/speed]. Style: cinematic, shallow depth of field. Mood: [mood]. Avoid: text, dialogue, logos.
