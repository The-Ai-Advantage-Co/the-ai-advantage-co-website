The mode choice maps to depth. Standard search is for quick facts ('what's the current EU AI Act enforcement date?'). Gemini with web grounding is for synthesised answers needing 3-5 sources ('summarise the EU AI Act's main provisions'). Deep Research Max is for multi-step structured research ('build a competitive analysis of major regulatory frameworks across EU, US, UK with provisions, enforcement timelines, and industry response').

Pick the mode that matches the task's complexity. Over-using Deep Research for simple questions wastes time; under-using it for genuine research means you miss what it can produce.

03
Web Grounding Pattern
For most research

Ask Gemini a factual question. Gemini queries the web, pulls relevant sources, synthesises an answer with citations. The citations are clickable; you can verify any claim.

  • Be specific about time period — 'in 2026', 'recent', 'as of last quarter'
  • Ask for citations explicitly if not offered
  • Verify claims that matter — Gemini occasionally paraphrases sources inaccurately
  • Ask for both supportive and counter-evidence on contested topics

Question patterns that work

Web-grounded queries work best when they're specific about scope. Vague questions ('tell me about X') return summary; specific questions ('what's the current EU AI Act enforcement timeline as of 2026') return useful synthesis with citations.

  • Be specific about time period — 'in 2026', 'recent', 'as of last quarter'
  • Ask for citations explicitly if not offered
  • Verify claims that matter — Gemini occasionally paraphrases sources inaccurately
  • Ask for both supportive and counter-evidence on contested topics
  • Narrow scope produces sharper output than broad scope

Citation Discipline
What to trust and verify

Gemini cites sources but doesn't always quote accurately. The cited source is real; the synthesis around it might miss nuance.

📎 VERIFY QUOTES AND NUMBERS
For any claim you'll act on or publish, check the citation directly. Gemini's recall of source content is good but not perfect — particularly numbers and exact quotes.
📎 ALWAYS VERIFY NUMBERS AND QUOTES

Gemini's citations are real — the sources exist. But Gemini's recall of source content isn't perfect. Numbers and exact quotes occasionally drift between source and synthesis. For any claim you'll act on or publish, click through to the source and verify.

The citation verification habit

Building the citation verification habit early prevents bad downstream artifacts. The discipline is checking before acting — not after the analysis is published.

  • For every quoted statement, click to source and verify wording
  • For every cited number, click to source and verify the figure
  • For every claimed trend, verify direction and magnitude
  • Where source is paywalled, treat the claim as unverified until you can access
  • Save URLs of verified sources for your audit trail
  • Flag any source that doesn't actually support the claim — Gemini occasionally over-reaches

Bias and Source Quality
Where Gemini can mislead
  • Source mix — Gemini tends to over-weight high-traffic sources
  • Recency bias — newer sources sometimes preferred over more authoritative older ones
  • Echo chambers — for contested topics, sometimes returns the dominant view without alternatives
  • Always ask for counter-evidence — 'What's the strongest case against this?'

✅ DO THIS

  • Ask for counter-evidence on contested topics — 'what's the strongest case against this?'
  • Verify source authority — is this from a paper, an op-ed, a vendor blog?
  • Check recency — newer doesn't always mean more authoritative
  • Cross-check claims across multiple sources for high-stakes work
  • Note where you're relying on a single source
  • Acknowledge uncertainty where Gemini sounds confident but evidence is thin

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Trusting Gemini's confidence calibration on contested topics
  • Assuming popularity equals authority on a source
  • Skipping the counter-evidence check — leaves you with one-sided synthesis
  • Letting Gemini hide bias by sounding authoritative
  • Building decisions on single-source claims without verification
  • Treating Deep Research output as final without analyst review
08
Honest Limits
Where Gemini research isn't enough
  • Highly current events (last 24-48 hours) — not always up to date
  • Paywalled sources — usually only accesses public previews
  • Specialised research databases — Gemini doesn't have access
  • Original research — Gemini synthesises existing sources, can't generate new data

Where Gemini research isn't enough

Gemini's research capabilities have material limits. Knowing them upfront prevents painful gaps in downstream work.

  • Highly current events (last 24-48 hours) — not always up to date
  • Paywalled sources — usually only accesses public previews
  • Specialised research databases — Gemini doesn't have access
  • Original research — Gemini synthesises existing sources, can't generate new data
  • Foreign-language source coverage — strongest in English, varies by language
  • Industry-specific terminology — generic Gemini is weaker than specialised tools

Common Mistakes & Fixes
What goes wrong — and how to avoid it

Most AI workflows that 'don't work' fall into one of these patterns. The fix is almost always sharper context and stricter review, not switching models or tools.

  • Treating Gemini for Research with Web Grounding as a finished answer rather than a draft to review
  • Vague prompts that don't specify audience, format, or what you want changed
  • Forgetting to give it context — the file, the brief, the constraints
  • Skipping the verify step — always check numbers, dates, names, and policy claims
  • Using it for tasks where a 30-second human action would be faster

✅ DO THIS

  • Provide context — the file, the brief, the audience, the constraints
  • Iterate; don't just accept the first output
  • Always review numbers, names, dates, and policy claims
  • Treat AI as a fast draft generator, not an answer engine
  • Capture what works so you can replicate it
  • Match the model to the task — fast modes for drafts, reasoning modes for analysis

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Sending AI output without reading it end-to-end
  • Vague prompts with no audience, format, or success criteria
  • Assuming AI can see files or context it hasn't been given
  • Using AI for tasks faster done manually in under a minute
  • Treating numbers, quotes, or citations as verified without checking
  • Defaulting to the slow reasoning mode for every task
