The Four-Element Brief
What every writing prompt needs

Gemini writes dramatically better with: context, task, format, audience. Skip any and you get something competent but generic.

  • Context — what you're writing about
  • Task — what you want produced
  • Format — length, structure
  • Audience — who reads it

Why the brief matters more than the model

Gemini writes dramatically better with four elements: context, task, format, audience. Skip any and you get something competent but generic. The brief is doing more of the work than the model — getting it right is the highest-leverage move.

  • Context — what you're writing about, key facts, what's already happened
  • Task — specific instruction: draft / edit / rewrite / tighten / expand
  • Format — length, structure, sections, what to include or omit
  • Audience — who reads, what they care about, what tone fits
  • Skip any element and quality drops noticeably

Canvas in Docs is Gemini's recommended surface for any non-trivial writing in 2026. Open a Doc, brief Gemini in the side panel, watch the draft appear in the Canvas, edit inline or ask for targeted revisions on selected text. The collaborative-document model fits writing work better than the side-panel chat model fits.

The pattern that consistently produces good output: outline first (Gemini drafts a structure from your brief), then expand section by section (avoid asking Gemini to write the whole thing at once), then edit ruthlessly. The 'one-shot full draft' approach reliably produces longer-but-shallower writing than the section-by-section approach.

Long-Context Writing
Where Gemini's 1M tokens shine

For pieces that need to reference a lot of source material (long-form journalism, research articles, year-end reviews, regulatory submissions), drop the source material into the conversation. Gemini holds it all in context simultaneously.

Tone and Voice Matching
Making Gemini sound like you

Gemini matches tone if you give it 2-3 samples. Paste examples, then ask Gemini to write in that voice.

🎭 SAMPLES BEAT DESCRIPTIONS
'Friendly but professional' doesn't help. Three actual emails do. Gemini reverse-engineers the voice from examples — same as Claude, same as ChatGPT.
🎭 ENCODE VOICE IN A GEM
For sustained brand voice work, build a Gem with the voice samples as knowledge files. Every team member produces content in the same voice without having to remember the brief each time. Scales voice consistency across larger teams.

The voice-matching prompt that works

Abstract descriptions don't work. Three actual samples do. Gemini reverse-engineers the voice from examples — including the things you can't articulate but want preserved.

  • Paste 3 emails / posts / drafts in the target voice
  • Specify what NOT to imitate (some samples have things to leave behind)
  • Mention specific moves: 'I tend to open with a question'
  • Ask Gemini to draft, then verify: 'does this match the voice in samples?'
  • For brand voice, encode in a Gem — reusable across the team

Content Calendar Building
From brief to month
  • Brief Gemini on themes, audience, cadence
  • Get a 4-week calendar with topics, hooks, format per slot
  • Expand each slot into a draft
  • Iterate hooks before publishing

✅ DO THIS

  • Brief themes — 'Our themes are X, Y, Z'
  • Specify cadence — '4 LinkedIn posts/week, 2 Twitter threads/week'
  • Define audience — specific roles and concerns
  • Get back a 4-week calendar with topics, hooks, formats per slot
  • Expand winning slots into full drafts via Canvas
  • Iterate hooks before publishing — generate 10, pick 2

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Vague theme briefs ('content about our product')
  • Skipping audience specification
  • Letting Gemini decide cadence — usually inflates
  • Publishing first-draft hooks without iteration
  • Using AI calendar as fixed plan — should be starting point
  • Forgetting to capture which posts performed for future calibration

What Gemini Is Less Strong At
Honest limits
  • Highly sensitive or nuanced tone — Claude tends to win here
  • Very technical writing for engineers — Claude or ChatGPT often better
  • Voice imitation of specific public figures — restricted by policy
  • Citation precision — always verify quotes; Gemini can paraphrase as direct quote
⚠️ HONEST COMPARISON
Highly sensitive or nuanced tone — Claude tends to win here. Very technical writing for engineers — Claude or ChatGPT often better. Voice imitation of specific public figures — restricted by policy. Citation precision — always verify quotes; Gemini can paraphrase as direct quote.

Common Mistakes & Fixes
What goes wrong — and how to avoid it

Most AI workflows that 'don't work' fall into one of these patterns. The fix is almost always sharper context and stricter review, not switching models or tools.

  • Treating Gemini for Content Creation and Drafting as a finished answer rather than a draft to review
  • Vague prompts that don't specify audience, format, or what you want changed
  • Forgetting to give it context — the file, the brief, the constraints
  • Skipping the verify step — always check numbers, dates, names, and policy claims
  • Using it for tasks where a 30-second human action would be faster

✅ DO THIS

  • Provide context — the file, the brief, the audience, the constraints
  • Iterate; don't just accept the first output
  • Always review numbers, names, dates, and policy claims
  • Treat AI as a fast draft generator, not an answer engine
  • Capture what works so you can replicate it
  • Match the model to the task — fast modes for drafts, reasoning modes for analysis

❌ AVOID THIS

  • Sending AI output without reading it end-to-end
  • Vague prompts with no audience, format, or success criteria
  • Assuming AI can see files or context it hasn't been given
  • Using AI for tasks faster done manually in under a minute
  • Treating numbers, quotes, or citations as verified without checking
  • Defaulting to the slow reasoning mode for every task

Suggested Prompts
Ready-to-use prompts you can adapt to your work

These prompts are designed to land in your real work today. Replace the bracketed placeholders with your actual context — the more specific you make them, the better the output. Treat each one as a starting brief, not a finished script.

CANVAS BRIEF
Open a Doc with Canvas. We're writing a 2000-word piece on [topic]. Audience: [audience]. Tone: [tone]. Source material is in /Drive/[folder]. Draft the outline first, then we'll expand section by section.
LONG-CONTEXT DRAFT
I've attached 18 monthly reports. Draft our annual review piece — 1500 words, board-pack tone. Lead with the 3 most important trends across the year, support each with specific examples from the reports.
SLIDE IMAGE GEN
Generate a hero image for the title slide of our [topic] presentation. Style: clean editorial, soft palette aligned to our brand colours [colours]. Composition: centred subject, generous negative space. No text.
