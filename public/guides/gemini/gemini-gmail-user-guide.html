Gemini in Gmail

Write better emails faster, tame your inbox, and turn Gemini into your built-in communications partner — without leaving Gmail.

1

What is Gemini in Gmail?

Gemini is built directly into Gmail — available through the compose window, the email view panel, and the Gmail side panel. It helps you write, rewrite, summarise, and manage email communication without leaving your inbox.

Unlike using Gemini at gemini.google.com, Gemini in Gmail has direct access to your emails (with your permission) and works within the context of what you're composing or reading. It's a native AI layer on top of Gmail, not a separate tool.

✍️ Help Me Write

Generate complete email drafts from a short prompt, directly in the compose window.

📄 Summarise

Collapse long email threads into key points with one click.

🔄 Smart Compose

Inline suggestions as you type — complete sentences and phrases in your voice.

💬 Smart Reply

One-tap suggested replies for quick acknowledgements and responses.

Availability:

Help Me Write and side panel Q&A require a Google Workspace account with Gemini for Workspace enabled, or a personal Gmail account with Gemini Advanced. Smart Compose and Smart Reply are available to all Gmail users.

2

Help Me Write

Help Me Write is the most powerful Gemini feature in Gmail. Access it by clicking the pen/sparkle icon at the bottom of the compose window. Enter a brief description of what you want to say, and Gemini drafts the full email.

1

Open a new compose window

Click Compose in Gmail. Look for the "Help me write" icon (sparkle pen) at the bottom left of the compose area.

2

Describe what you need

Enter a short brief: "Follow up with the client after yesterday's demo. Confirm next steps and ask for their timeline." Gemini writes the full email.

3

Refine with options

Use the built-in options to adjust: Formalise, Elaborate, Shorten, or regenerate with a different approach.

4

Edit and send

Accept the draft into your compose window, personalise it (add names, specifics), and send.

Better briefs = better drafts:

Include: the relationship (e.g. "to a client we've worked with for 2 years"), the tone needed ("professional but warm"), the purpose ("to push back politely on their timeline request"), and any key facts ("the delay is due to staffing issues, not budget").

Refining existing drafts

If you've already started writing, you can use Help Me Write to refine: select all your text, click the sparkle icon, and choose "Elaborate," "Shorten," or type a custom instruction like "Make this more assertive."

Summarising Emails

The email summarise feature condenses long email threads into bullet-point summaries. Look for the "Summarise this email" banner at the top of long threads, or the sparkle icon in the email toolbar.

When to use it

  • Catching up on a thread you weren't originally included in
  • Quickly refreshing your memory before a meeting or call
  • Long customer support threads with multiple back-and-forth messages
  • After returning from annual leave with hundreds of unread emails
  • Understanding a complex technical email before forwarding to someone else
Follow up in the side panel:

After summarising, open the Gmail side panel (right side of the Gmail window) and ask follow-up questions about the email: "What action is this email asking me to take?" or "What was agreed in this thread?"

What summary includes

Gmail summaries typically cover: the core topic, key decisions or requests, who is responsible for action items, and any deadlines mentioned. They do not include all the conversational back-and-forth — just the substance.

Smart Compose & Smart Reply

Smart Compose and Smart Reply are lighter-weight AI features available to all Gmail users — no Gemini Advanced subscription required.

Smart Compose

As you type an email, Smart Compose predicts the rest of your sentence and shows it in grey. Press Tab to accept the suggestion, or keep typing to ignore it. Over time it learns your writing style and improves its suggestions.

Enable Smart Compose:

Settings (gear icon) → See all settings → General → Smart Compose → Writing suggestions on.

Smart Reply

At the bottom of incoming emails, Gmail shows 3 suggested short replies. These are generated by AI based on the email content — things like "Sounds good!", "I'll take a look," or "Thanks for letting me know." Click one to insert it as a draft reply.

Managing Inbox Overload

Gemini in Gmail helps cut through inbox overload with a combination of AI summarisation, the side panel Q&A, and the Gemini chat extension (covered in the Extensions guide).

The overload toolkit

📋 Thread summaries

Summarise every long thread before deciding whether it needs a response. Stop reading walls of text.

❓ Side panel Q&A

Open a specific email and ask "What is this person asking me to do?" — get a direct action-oriented answer.

🔍 Gemini extension

In Gemini chat (with Gmail extension enabled): "Show me all emails from this week that are waiting for my response."

✉️ Batch drafting

Use Help Me Write to quickly generate responses to similar types of emails — follow-ups, acknowledgements, polite declines.

The 3-question triage:

For each important email, ask the side panel: (1) "What is this email asking me to do?" (2) "By when?" (3) "What information do I need to respond?" You'll process emails 3× faster.

Gmail Side Panel

The Gmail side panel is a full Gemini conversation window that lives inside Gmail. Open it with the Gemini icon on the right side of the Gmail interface. Unlike the compose-window features, the side panel lets you have an open-ended conversation with Gemini about your emails and other tasks.

What you can do in the side panel

  • Ask questions about a specific open email: "What's the deadline mentioned in this thread?"
  • Draft a response based on the email context: "Write a polite decline to this invitation"
  • Search your emails: "Find emails from [person] about [topic] in the last month"
  • Get help composing content related to an email: "Draft talking points for a call following up on this proposal"
  • Ask general Gemini questions without leaving Gmail: "What are the GDPR rules around email retention?"
Context awareness:

When an email is open, the side panel is aware of its content — you can reference it without copying text. "Write a response to this" will use the open email as context automatically.

Advanced Techniques

Tone and relationship calibration

Help Me Write's quality depends heavily on relationship context. Always specify: "This is to a supplier we've had issues with — the tone should be firm but professional, not confrontational." Vague briefs produce generic drafts.

Draft templates as Help Me Write prompts

Build a library of Help Me Write briefs for your most common email types. Keep them in a Google Doc: "Follow-up after no response — 3 days since initial email, politely chase without being annoying." Paste the brief each time for consistent quality.

Combine with Google Docs for long communications

For complex emails (proposals, project updates, detailed requests), draft them in Google Docs using Gemini there, then paste the final version into Gmail. Docs gives you more space for refinement before committing to send.

Use side panel for pre-send review

Before sending important emails, use the side panel: "Review this email I'm about to send. Does it achieve the goal? Is the tone appropriate? What might the recipient misunderstand?" A second opinion before sending costly emails.

Language translation:

Gemini in Gmail can translate emails and help you respond in other languages. Open a foreign-language email and ask the side panel: "Translate this email and draft a polite reply in the same language."

Suggested Prompts

Professional Follow-Up
"Follow up on a proposal sent 5 days ago. Tone: professional and persistent but not pushy. Acknowledge they may be busy. Offer to schedule a 15-minute call. Keep it under 100 words."
Polite Decline
"Decline an invitation to speak at an event due to scheduling conflicts. Keep the door open for future opportunities. Be warm and appreciative of the invitation. Keep it brief — 3 sentences maximum."
Complaint Escalation
"Write a firm but professional email to a supplier who has missed their delivery deadline for the third time. Summarise the impact on our business. Request an urgent call and a formal remediation plan. Tone: serious, not aggressive."
Pre-Send Review
"Review the email I've drafted below before I send it. Tell me: (1) Is the goal clear? (2) Is the tone appropriate for this relationship? (3) What might the recipient misunderstand or react negatively to? [paste email]"
Thread Briefing
"Summarise this email thread for me. I need: what's been decided, what's still open, who's responsible for next steps, and any deadlines mentioned. Format as a short bullet list."
Response Drafting
"Based on this email, draft a response that: agrees to the meeting, proposes three times next week, confirms you'll send the agenda 24 hours in advance, and asks them to confirm dietary requirements for the lunch."

See It In Action

A consultant uses Help Me Write to draft a difficult email — a polite but firm response to a client who has asked for work outside the agreed scope.

Gmail — Help Me Write
Re: Q4 Report — Additional Analysis Request
From: Client — just received
Hi, hope you're well. We were wondering if you could also add a section on the competitive landscape for our top 3 products to the Q4 report? We think it would add a lot of value. Let us know!
Help Me Write brief
"Politely decline the additional competitive analysis request. This is out of scope. Offer to quote separately. Keep the relationship warm. Keep it under 120 words."
✨ Gemini Draft
Hi [Name],

Thanks for the suggestion — a competitive landscape section would certainly add value. However, this falls outside the scope we agreed for the Q4 report, so we wouldn't be able to include it within the current engagement.

We'd be happy to scope this as a separate piece of work. I can put together a brief proposal and quote by end of the week if that would be useful?

Looking forward to delivering the Q4 report on schedule.

Best,
[Your name]
