Claude Projects — Organise Your AI Work

Projects are Claude's built-in system for organising related conversations with shared context, persistent instructions, and a shared knowledge base. Instead of re-explaining yourself every time you open a new chat, you set it once — and every conversation in that Project benefits automatically.

Version 1.0 April 2026 Audience: Pro / Team Users
01

What are Claude Projects?

A persistent workspace that keeps context, instructions, and files in one place

In a standard Claude conversation, every new chat starts blank. Claude has no memory of what you discussed yesterday, no knowledge of your role or preferences, and no access to your documents unless you paste them in again. Projects solve this.

A Project is a persistent workspace. You define it once — give it a name, write a set of instructions, and upload any relevant files — and every conversation you open inside that Project automatically inherits that context. Claude knows who you are, what the Project is for, and what rules apply, without you having to repeat it.

📋

Custom Instructions

Set Claude's role, tone, rules, and background once. Applied to every conversation in the Project automatically.

📂

Shared Knowledge Base

Upload documents, templates, guidelines, and data. Claude can reference them in any conversation within the Project.

🗂️

Grouped Conversations

All chats stay organised under the Project — easy to find, easy to continue, no more hunting through history.

👥

Team Sharing

Share Projects with teammates on Team and Enterprise plans. Consistent context for the whole team.

💡
Think of it like a briefing folder
A Project is the briefing document you hand to Claude every time it joins one of your conversations — except it's automatic. The better the briefing, the less time you spend re-explaining and the more time Claude spends producing useful output.
02

Creating a Project

Set up a new Project in under five minutes

Creating a Project takes a few minutes. The setup investment pays back across every conversation you have inside it — so it's worth doing properly from the start.

1

Open the Projects panel

Click Projects in the left sidebar of Claude.ai. If you don't see it, make sure you're on a Pro, Team, or Enterprise plan — Projects are not available on the Free plan.

2

Click "New Project"

Give it a clear, specific name. Use the naming pattern [Area] — [Purpose] to keep things organised as your list grows. Examples: Client Comms — Acme Co, Q3 Marketing Campaign, Legal — Contract Reviews.

3

Write your Project Instructions

This is the most important step. Instructions are the standing brief Claude follows in every conversation. Cover: who you are, what this Project is for, Claude's role, tone, and any rules or constraints. See Section 03 for a detailed guide.

4

Upload your knowledge base files

Add any documents Claude should know about — brand guidelines, templates, product specs, past reports. These are available in every conversation in the Project. See Section 04 for what to include.

5

Start your first conversation

Click New chat from inside the Project. Notice that you don't need to re-explain the context — Claude already has it. Jump straight to the task.

ℹ️
You can edit anytime
Project Instructions and knowledge base files can be updated at any time. Changes apply to new conversations immediately. Existing conversations retain the context they were started with.
03

Project Instructions

The standing brief that shapes every conversation in the Project

Project Instructions are the most powerful part of a Project. They act as a persistent system prompt — a background brief that Claude reads before every conversation. A well-written instruction set means every chat starts with Claude already oriented to your needs.

What to Include

Instruction Builder — Cover These Five Areas

Work through each section and you'll have a strong Project instruction set in under 10 minutes.

Who you are
Your role, company, industry, and relevant background. Example: "I am a senior marketing manager at a B2B SaaS company in the HR tech space, managing a team of three."
What this Project is for
The purpose of this specific Project. Example: "This Project is used for drafting client-facing communications, proposals, and follow-up emails."
Claude's role
How Claude should behave — as an editor, strategist, drafter, analyst, etc. Example: "Act as a professional communications advisor. Prioritise clarity, brevity, and a confident tone."
Tone & style rules
Specific language preferences, things to avoid, formatting expectations. Example: "Always write in Australian English. Avoid jargon. Use short paragraphs. Never use exclamation marks."
Constraints & priorities
What matters most, what to avoid, and any mandatory inclusions. Example: "Always end emails with a clear next step. Never make commitments about pricing without flagging it to me first."

Weak vs. Strong Instructions

❌
Weak
"Help me with work emails." — Claude has no idea who you are, what kind of work, what tone to use, or what kind of emails. Every conversation will start with re-explaining.
✅
Strong
"I'm a client success manager at a logistics software company. This Project is for client communications — account updates, escalation responses, and renewal discussions. Write in a professional but warm tone. Keep emails under 200 words. Always end with a specific next step. Avoid passive voice."
💡
Ask Claude to write your instructions for you
Open a regular (non-Project) chat and ask: "Help me write a Claude Project instruction set for [your use case]. Ask me questions first." Claude will interview you and produce a draft you can paste straight in.
04

The Knowledge Base

Upload files that Claude can reference across every conversation in the Project

The knowledge base is where you give Claude the documents it needs to do your work properly. Upload once — Claude can reference the content in any conversation inside the Project without you needing to paste it in again.

What to Upload

File TypeWhat to IncludeWhy It Helps
Brand guidelinesTone of voice docs, style guides, logo rulesClaude writes on-brand without being told every time
TemplatesEmail templates, report formats, proposal structuresClaude produces work in your format, not its default
Product / service docsFeature lists, pricing, FAQs, case studiesAccurate, informed output with no hallucinated details
Reference reportsPast outputs, client history, research papersClaude can build on and remain consistent with prior work
Process docsSOPs, checklists, internal playbooksClaude follows your process, not a generic one
Data / CSV filesMetrics, financials, audience dataClaude can reason over real numbers, not guesses

Supported File Types

Claude accepts PDFs, Word documents (.docx), plain text files (.txt), Markdown (.md), images (PNG, JPG), and CSV files. For large files, Claude reads and indexes the content — upload quality, readable documents for best results.

ℹ️
Knowledge base vs. uploading in chat
Files in the knowledge base are always available across all conversations in the Project. Files uploaded directly in a chat are only available in that specific conversation. Use the knowledge base for anything you'll reference repeatedly. Upload in-chat for one-off files.

Keep It Curated

  • Only include files Claude actually needs. Irrelevant content dilutes focus.
  • Use clear, descriptive file names — Claude uses them to understand what each file is.
  • Remove or replace outdated documents when information changes.
  • Split very large documents into focused sections if possible — more digestible for Claude.

Organising Conversations

How to structure and navigate your Project conversations effectively

A Project can contain many conversations over time. Good organisation habits keep things findable and manageable as your use of Claude scales.

Starting a New Conversation

Always open new conversations from inside the Project — click the Project name in the sidebar first, then click New chat. This ensures Claude has the Project instructions and knowledge base available. A conversation started outside the Project won't have access to them.

One Topic Per Conversation

Don't try to do everything in one long conversation. Claude has a context window — the amount of text it can process at once. Very long conversations can start to lose coherence. Better practice: one conversation per discrete task or topic.

Instead of…Do this
One massive "work" chat with everythingSeparate conversations per client, campaign, or deliverable
Continuing an old chat on a new topicOpen a new chat — the instructions persist, so context isn't lost
Unnamed conversations in a listClaude auto-names chats; rename them manually if the name is unclear

Finding Past Conversations

  • All conversations under a Project are listed when you open it in the sidebar.
  • Use the search function (top of sidebar) to find conversations by keyword.
  • Star important conversations for fast access.
  • Rename conversations manually for clarity — click the chat name to edit it.
💡
Use multiple Projects for different work areas
Don't try to make one Project do everything. Create a separate Project for each distinct work context — one for client comms, one for internal reporting, one for research. Each gets its own tailored instructions and knowledge base.

Projects vs. Default Chat

When to use a Project and when a standard conversation is fine

Not everything needs a Project. Understanding when to use each saves setup time and keeps your workspace clean.

Scenario Use a Project Use Default Chat
Recurring work area (client, campaign, function) ✓ Yes — context accumulates ✗ Re-explain every time
One-off question or quick task ✗ Overkill ✓ Yes — faster to just chat
Multiple conversations on the same topic ✓ Yes — shared context saves time ✗ No link between chats
Specific documents Claude needs to know ✓ Yes — knowledge base available always ✗ Must re-upload every chat
Experimental or exploratory use ✗ No need yet ✓ Yes — low commitment
Team needs same context ✓ Yes — share the Project ✗ No sharing option
Consistent tone / style rules ✓ Yes — enforced via instructions ✗ Must specify each time
ℹ️
Rule of thumb
If you find yourself explaining the same context at the start of a new conversation more than twice, that work belongs in a Project.

Team & Shared Projects

Giving your whole team consistent, configured access to Claude

On Team and Enterprise plans, Projects can be shared with other members of your workspace. Shared Projects give everyone access to the same instructions and knowledge base — meaning the whole team benefits from well-configured context, not just the person who set it up.

How Sharing Works

FeatureHow It Works
Shared instructionsEveryone who opens the Project sees the same instructions — consistent Claude behaviour across the team
Shared knowledge baseFiles uploaded to the Project are accessible to all members in any conversation
Individual conversationsEach team member's conversations within the Project are private by default — sharing the Project doesn't share your specific chats
Editing permissionsProject owners can update instructions and knowledge base; members can start conversations but typically cannot change the setup

Good Use Cases for Shared Projects

  • Sales team: One Project with product docs, objection handling guides, and email templates — every rep uses the same configured Claude.
  • Content team: Brand voice guidelines, tone of voice docs, and content briefs in one place — consistent output across all writers.
  • Operations: Process documentation and SOPs uploaded once — every team member can ask questions and get consistent, accurate answers.
  • Legal / compliance: Approved contract templates and policy documents — Claude drafts within approved boundaries automatically.
⚠️
Review what's in the knowledge base before sharing
When you share a Project, all knowledge base files become accessible to everyone with access. Review your uploaded files before sharing to ensure no sensitive or confidential documents are included that shouldn't be accessible to all members.

Best Practices

How to get the most out of Projects over time

Projects improve with use. The habits below will help you build a set of Projects that become genuinely useful over time — not just tidy shortcuts.

Instructions: Write Once, Refine Often

  • Start with a solid draft, then update it after your first few conversations once you see what Claude gets wrong or misses.
  • Be specific about what you want Claude to do and what you want it to avoid — both matter.
  • Add examples of good output to the instructions if tone or format keeps coming out wrong. Claude learns from examples.
  • Review instructions quarterly — your needs and context change over time.

Knowledge Base: Quality Over Quantity

  • Upload clean, well-formatted documents — Claude reads them better than messy or scanned files.
  • Remove outdated files immediately when information changes. Stale documents confuse Claude.
  • Add a brief description at the top of each document explaining what it is and when to use it.
  • Keep the total knowledge base focused — 10 high-quality, relevant files outperform 50 loosely related ones.

Conversations: Stay Purposeful

  • Start each conversation with a clear task — don't open-endedly browse. The instructions handle the context; you focus on the task.
  • Use conversation names to label what was done — e.g. "Acme renewal email — v2 approved". Future you will thank present you.
  • When a task is done and the output is saved, the conversation has served its purpose. Don't keep editing indefinitely.
💡
The Project Audit Habit
Once a month, spend 10 minutes reviewing your active Projects. Update instructions, remove stale files, archive Projects you no longer use. A well-maintained set of Projects is dramatically more useful than a cluttered one.

Suggested Prompts

Ready-to-use prompts for setting up and using Projects effectively

Use these prompts to set up strong Projects and get the most from your conversations inside them. Replace the bracketed parts with your specifics.

Build My Project Instructions
"Help me write a set of Claude Project instructions for this use case: I am a [role] at a [company/industry]. This Project is for [purpose]. Ask me five clarifying questions first, then draft the instructions in a structured format I can paste directly into Claude."
Improve Existing Instructions
"Here are my current Claude Project instructions: [paste them]. Review them and tell me: (1) what's missing, (2) what's unclear or too vague, and (3) give me an improved version I can use straight away."
Plan My Project Structure
"I want to use Claude Projects more systematically at work. My main work areas are: [list them]. Help me design a Project structure — how many Projects to create, what to name them, and what instructions and files each one needs."
Create a Shared Team Project Brief
"Write a Claude Project instruction set for a shared team Project. Our team is [describe team]. The Project is for [purpose]. Team members have varying experience with AI. The instructions need to be clear enough that anyone on the team can use them effectively without training."
Summarise a Document for the Knowledge Base
"I'm going to add the following document to a Claude Project knowledge base. Write a brief summary paragraph (3–4 sentences) I can paste at the top of the document so Claude knows what it is and when to use it. [paste document]"
Jump Straight Into a Task
"[Task description]. Use the relevant files in the knowledge base if they apply. Output format: [specify]. Length: [specify]. Flag any assumptions you're making."
Use a Specific Knowledge Base File
"Using the [file name] document in the knowledge base as your primary reference, [task]. If anything in the document is unclear or out of date, flag it rather than filling in gaps with assumptions."
Stay Consistent with Previous Work
"I'm going to paste an example of previous work from this Project. Study the tone, structure, and style. Then produce [new task] that matches the same standard. [paste example output]"
Audit Your Instructions Mid-Project
"Here are my current Project instructions: [paste them]. Based on our recent conversations in this Project, are there any gaps, assumptions that aren't stated, or rules I should add to make your output more consistent and useful?"
Batch Multiple Tasks in One Session
"I need to complete the following [number] tasks in this session. Work through them in order and present each output clearly labelled before moving to the next. Task 1: [describe]. Task 2: [describe]. Task 3: [describe]."

Your Roadmap

Five steps to a well-organised, high-value Project setup

You don't need to set up everything at once. Follow this sequence and you'll have a properly configured, genuinely useful Project workspace within a week.

1

Identify your top recurring work area

Pick the one thing you do most often that involves writing, analysis, or research. That's your first Project. Don't start with your most complex use case — start with the most frequent one.

2

Write a proper instruction set

Use the five-area framework from Section 03. Take 10 minutes. If you're not sure what to write, use the "Build My Project Instructions" prompt from Section 09 and let Claude help you draft it.

3

Upload 3–5 high-value knowledge base files

Don't upload everything — upload what Claude actually needs. A brand guide, a template, and one reference document is often enough to start. You can add more as you discover gaps.

4

Use the Project for a full week

Do your actual work inside the Project — don't just test it. After a week, you'll know exactly what the instructions are missing, which files you needed but didn't have, and what to refine. Update accordingly.

5

Build out your Project suite

Once your first Project is running well, apply the same process to your next two or three work areas. Within a month, you'll have a configured workspace where Claude knows your context for every area of your work — and you'll spend far less time re-explaining and far more time getting useful output.

🚀
The compounding effect
Projects get better the more you refine them. Every time you notice Claude getting something wrong or missing context, update the instructions. Over time, your Projects become increasingly accurate representations of how you work — and Claude's output gets proportionally better.
